G.E7.1. East Asian Buddhist Festivals — Overview

This group introduces the annual observances of East Asian Mahāyāna Buddhism. They commemorate the Buddha's life, honour bodhisattvas and ancestors, and provide rhythms for temple and family practice. Festivals should be read through their teaching, shared practice, and local custom—not treated as identical across countries.

1. Three main groups of Mahāyāna festivals

The first group commemorates major events in the Buddha's life: birth, awakening, final passing, first teaching, and renunciation. The second honours major bodhisattvas, including Avalokiteśvara, Kṣitigarbha, Mañjuśrī, and Samantabhadra. The third includes ancestral and community observances such as Vu Lan / Ullambana, Ghost Festival traditions, and New Year temple customs.

2. Why do the same festivals fall on different dates?

Most are lunar observances. Vietnam, China, Korea, Japan, and other regions have used related but not identical lunar, lunisolar, and modern civil calendars. A festival can therefore share a traditional meaning while fall on different Gregorian dates, or even have different lunar-day labels. Check the particular country and year rather than assuming one conversion applies everywhere.

3. Four calendar systems in the G.E7 group

This group distinguishes Vietnamese lunar dates; Chinese lunar dates; Korean lunar dates; and Japanese observances, many of which now use fixed Gregorian dates after Japan's calendar reforms. Calendar notation identifies timing, not a difference in the Dharma commemorated.

4. How to read a festival — three layers

Foundational teaching concerns the Buddha, a bodhisattva, or a principle of practice. Shared practice includes chanting, offerings, listening to the Dhamma, precepts, and dedication of merit. Custom includes lanterns, processions, food, local calendar arrangements, and public events. Keeping the layers distinct lets a newcomer participate sincerely without mistaking custom for compulsory doctrine.

5. Summary and reading path

  • East Asian Mahāyāna festivals commemorate the Buddha's life, bodhisattvas, ancestors, and community rhythms.
  • Dates vary because calendar systems vary; Gregorian dates must be checked by country and year.
  • Read every observance through teaching, practice, and custom.

Glossary:

  • Lunisolar calendar: a calendar using lunar months with adjustments to remain aligned with the solar year.
  • Dedication of merit: dedicating wholesome results to others.
  • Ullambana / Vu Lan: a major East Asian festival associated with gratitude, ancestors, and merit dedication.

Source notes

  • Festival dates and forms vary across East Asia and within local communities.
  • Calendar dates are not converted here into a fixed Gregorian date for every year.
