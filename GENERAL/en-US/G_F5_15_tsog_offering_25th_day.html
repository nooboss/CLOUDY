G.F5.15. (Phugpa) Tsog Offering — Twenty-fifth Day (Ḍākinī Day)
Twenty-fifth day of each Tibetan month

The 25th day of each month in the Tibetan Buddhist calendar is the second tsog offering day of the month (Tibetan: tsog — communal offering feast — explained in G.F5.13), called in Sanskrit Gaṇacakra Pūjā — Pañcaviṃśatī (Sanskrit: pañcaviṃśatī — English: the twenty-fifth day — "the twenty-fifth day"). If the 10th day is linked with Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche — article G.F5.13), then the 25th day is linked with the Dakinis (Sanskrit: ḍākinī — English: dakini, the female embodiments of the teaching in the Tantric tradition) — hence this day is also called "Dakini Day".

1. Dakini Day

In the Tantric tradition of Tibetan Buddhism, the Dakini (Sanskrit: ḍākinī — English: dakini) are female embodiments — manifestations of dynamic wisdom (the dynamic quality of wisdom in Tantric symbolism). Dakinis appear in many forms: from sacred celestial maidens — to enlightened female disciples in the transmission lineages. The 25th day of each month is the day of offering to the Dakinis: monasteries and practice groups hold a tsog offering with prayers for wisdom — awakening in practice. This day is also a way of remembering the nuns — women in the history of the transmission lineages (presented at an introductory level; for details of Tantric symbolism — see G.F2).

2. How It Is Observed

  • At monasteries / practice groups: tsog offering — recitation of Dakini prayers — the liturgies of the lineage; offering of lamps (butter lamps — lamps made with yak butter used as candles — English: butter lamps); sharing of substances as in every tsog.
  • Lay devotees: join the tsog at a monastery when convenient; or recite sutras — recite mantras (Sanskrit: mantra — English: mantra), and make a small offering at home.
  • Monthly rhythm: the 25th day + the 10th day = two tsog offerings each month; together with the 15th day (full moon — article G.F5.14) — three major practice points in the second half — first half of the month.

3. Three Levels for Reading the 25th Day

  • Root doctrinal level: directing the mind toward wisdom — awakening in practice — through the symbolism of the Dakini; the letting-go — sharing practice of the tsog.
  • Common practice level: tsog, sutra recitation, mantra recitation, lamp offering.
  • Customary level: customs of preparing substances; ritual forms differing among the lineages (article G.F2).

4. Summary and Next Step

  • The 25th day of each month = the "Dakini Day" tsog — directed toward wisdom — the dynamism of practice.
  • Together with the 10th day (Guru Rinpoche) and the 15th day (full moon), it forms a steady monthly rhythm of practice.

Glossary:

  • Pañcaviṃśatī (Sanskrit: pañcaviṃśatī — English: the twenty-fifth day): "the twenty-fifth day" — the name for the 25th day of each month.
  • Dakini (Sanskrit: ḍākinī — English: dakini): female embodiments of the teaching in the Tantric tradition — manifestations of dynamic wisdom.
  • Symbolism (English: symbolism): the use of images — figures to suggest qualities — doctrinal truths, not a literal description.
  • Tsog (Tibetan, from Sanskrit "gaṇacakra" — English: tsok offering): communal offering feast — see article G.F5.
  • Butter lamps (English: butter lamps): lamps made with yak butter — a familiar offering article of Himalayan monasteries.
  • Mantra (Sanskrit: mantra — English: mantra): short phrases repeated in practice — see article G.F5.
