G.F5.1. Overview — Vajrayāna Festivals (Tibetan)
Phugpa Calendar

1. The Phugpa Tibetan calendar — general picture

The Tibetan calendar is a lunisolar calendar (lunar months fitted to the solar year, like the Vietnamese — Chinese lunar calendar): months begin with the moon phases; from time to time there is a leap month and "skipped" days (a day omitted or repeated) under the astronomical calculations of the Phugpa system. Hence:

  • Tibetan festival dates differ by about one — two months from the Vietnamese — Chinese lunar calendar, depending on the year; they cannot be converted rigidly into fixed Gregorian dates.
  • The reading rule for the whole group: each article records the date in the Tibetan Buddhist calendar (counted from Losar New Year); for a specific year, consult that year's Tibetan — Gregorian calendar table.
  • Many observances follow the moon phases — the 1st (new moon) and the 15th (full moon) matter as in other traditions (article G.D3 on Uposatha days).

2. Map of 16 festivals — three groups

  • Annual festivals (10 articles — G.F5.2 → G.F5.11): Losar New Year; the Miracle Season; the Saga Dawa month; Dzamling Chisang; the three remaining Düchens; Tsongkhapa's memorial day; Gutor — overview in section 3.
  • Monthly practice days (6 articles — G.F5.12 → G.F5.17): the 8th (Tārā — Medicine Buddha); the 10th (Tsog); the 15th (full moon); the 25th (Tsog); the 29th (Dharma Protectors); the 30th (new moon) — the "six practice days" cycle repeating each month.

3. The four Düchens — "great days of merit"

The Tibetan word "Düchen" (Tibetan: Düchen — "great day of merit") denotes the four greatest days of the year — commemorating four events in the Buddha's life and Dharma history:

  • Chötrul Düchen — the day the Buddha displayed miracles (defeating rival teachers in the miracle contest — article G.F5.4).
  • Saga Dawa Düchen — the day the Buddha's birth, awakening, and final passing converge in one day (article G.F5.6) — corresponding to Vesak — Buddha's Birthday in other traditions (articles G.D5.3, G.E7.2).
  • Chökhor Düchen — the day of the first turning of the Dharma wheel (article G.F5.8).
  • Lhabab Düchen — the day the Buddha descended from the Trāyastriṃśa heaven (article G.F5.9).

These four days are regarded as merit-multiplying days — wholesome acts on these days are held to multiply many times over, and unwholesome acts likewise (the teaching — practice layer of the Tibetan tradition).

4. Reading a festival through three layers

The shared reading method of this section applies to group G.F5:

  • Foundational teaching layer: the event / figure commemorated — often the same core as the Theravāda and Mahāyāna festival articles (birth, awakening, first teaching, descent from heaven, final passing).
  • Practice layer: chanting, homage, offerings — plus distinctive Tibetan methods: mantra (Sanskrit: mantra — a short repeated sacred formula), visualization (clearly picturing a sacred figure in the mind), pūjā (Sanskrit: pūjā — offering ceremony with substances and prayers).
  • Custom layer: Tibetan — Bhutanese — Mongolian cultural customs: prayer flags, prayer wheels (article G.F2 on foundational concepts), folk rites around Losar New Year.

Glossary:

  • Phugpa calendar (Tibetan: Phugpa): the traditional Tibetan calendar system — the most widely used calendar today for dating Tibetan Buddhist festivals.
  • Düchen (Tibetan, "great day of merit"): the common name for the four greatest festival days of the year in Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Miracle display (Sanskrit: prātihārya): the display of supernormal powers for teaching — see article G.F5.
  • Mantra (Sanskrit: mantra): a short formula repeated in practice — central to Vajrayāna methods.
  • Visualization: the method of clearly picturing a sacred figure in the mind — a distinctive practice of the Tibetan tradition.
  • Pūjā (Sanskrit: pūjā — offering ceremony): an offering rite with substances — water, flowers, lamps — and prayers.
  • Tibetan Buddhist calendar: the year — month reckoning of the Tibetan calendar used for festival dates; counted from Losar New Year (G.F5.2).
