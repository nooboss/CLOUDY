G.F5.17. (Phugpa) New-Moon Practice Day (Amāvāsyā)
Thirtieth day of each Tibetan month

The 30th of each month — the new moon day (Sanskrit: amāvāsyā - English: new moon) — is in the Tibetan Buddhist calendar the end-of-month practice day: after the "clean-up" 29th (the Dharmapala day — previous article - G.F5.16), the 30th is the occasion for a fresh beginning — making vows and aspirations for the coming month, dedication of merit and prayers.

1. The new moon in the practice rhythm

The monthly rhythm of Tibetan Buddhism has two lunar points: the full moon (15th — Pūrṇimā) is the point of "clarity — strong merit" (article G.F5.14); the new moon (30th — Amāvāsyā) is the point of "emptiness — stillness" — favourable for gathering inward — making vows and aspirations. The same rhythm exists in the Southern tradition (the new-moon observance day — article G.D3) — a common root from the time of the Buddha. If the 29th "clears away the old" (Dharmapala — obstacles are removed), the 30th is the day for welcoming the new — making aspirations for the new month: completing the monthly cycle of "clearing the old — welcoming the new", like Gutor → Losar in the yearly rhythm (articles G.F5.11, G.F5.2).

2. What people do on the day

  • At monasteries: reciting scriptures — prayers; many monasteries recite prayers for the deceased — dedication of merit (the new-moon day in many traditions is associated with remembering the departed); making vows and aspirations for the new month of practice.
  • Lay followers: reciting scriptures — reciting mantras (Sanskrit: mantra - English: mantra); writing down — renewing practice commitments (for example: keeping the precepts for one more day, adding one more passage of scripture to study); small offerings.
  • Note: some Tibetan months have 29 days (the 30th day is skipped) according to the astronomical calculations of the Phugpa system — the 30th day "jumps" — monasteries following the official calendar adjust accordingly.

3. Three layers when reading the 30th day

  • Root doctrinal layer: the rhythm of the lunar phases — gathering inward — making vows and aspirations — a fresh beginning; of the same root as the observance days (article G.D3).
  • Shared practice layer: reciting scriptures, dedication of merit, making vows and aspirations, making offerings.
  • Customary layer: regional customs of remembering the departed; different forms of organization among the lineages.

4. Summary

  • The 30th of each month in the Tibetan Buddhist calendar = the new-moon practice day (Amāvāsyā) — making vows and aspirations — a fresh beginning.

Glossary:

  • New moon (Sanskrit: amāvāsyā - English: new moon): the new moon — the beginning of the lunar cycle (counted in the Tibetan reckoning as the last day of the month).
  • Full moon (Sanskrit: pūrṇimā - English: full moon): the full moon — see article G.F5.
  • Dedication of merit (transferring merit to others - English: dedication of merit): transferring merit from wholesome deeds to others — see article G.E7.
  • Vow/aspiration (Sanskrit: praṇidhāna - English: vow, aspiration): committing oneself to wholesome deeds — practice — the foundation of the bodhisattva path (article G.C4).
  • Observance day (Pali: uposatha - English: observance day): the practice day following the lunar phases — see article G.D3.
  • Mantra (Sanskrit: mantra - English: mantra): the short phrase repeated in practice — see article G.F5.
