G.E7.4. Vietnam: The Buddha's Final Passing (Mahāparinirvāṇa)
Fifteenth day of the second lunar month

This observance commemorates the Buddha's final passing, Mahāparinirvāṇa, traditionally kept in Vietnam on the fifteenth day of the second lunar month. It is not a festival of despair. It recalls impermanence, the completion of liberation, and the Buddha's final encouragement to practise diligently.

1. What does the day commemorate?

Traditional accounts place the Buddha's final passing at Kuśinagar when he was eighty. In Buddhist teaching, parinirvāṇa is not the annihilation of a permanent self, because Buddhism does not affirm such a self. It signifies the final ending of the conditions for rebirth. See G.B2 and G.C7.

2. Why this date?

Vietnamese Mahāyāna calendars record the observance on the second-lunar-month full moon. Calendar dates differ among traditions and countries; the date is a convention of commemoration rather than an independently proven chronological anniversary.

3. Understanding departure through the Dharma

The final teaching traditionally summarised from the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta is: conditioned things are impermanent; strive diligently. The observance therefore asks practitioners to face change without denial and to use limited life wisely. Chanting, memorial forms, and images of the reclining Buddha are supports for this reflection, not ends in themselves.

4. What newcomers should know

  • Approach the day quietly; it is suited to reflection, chanting, generosity, and meditation.
  • Do not confuse the final passing with a permanent soul travelling elsewhere.
  • Offerings are voluntary; the most direct participation is renewed ethical and contemplative practice.

5. Summary and next steps

  • The final-passing observance falls in Vietnam on the fifteenth day of the second lunar month.
  • It teaches impermanence and diligent practice, not despair or annihilationism.
  • Its calendar date and ritual forms are customary; its core is the Dharma.

Glossary:

  • Mahāparinirvāṇa: the Buddha's final passing and final release from rebirth.
  • Conditioned phenomena: things that arise through causes and conditions and therefore change.
  • Kuśinagar: the place traditionally associated with the Buddha's final passing.

Source notes

  • The final-passing account and final exhortation are associated with the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta, Dīgha Nikāya 16.
  • The Vietnamese date is a calendar convention, not a fixed Gregorian date or independently established historical anniversary.
