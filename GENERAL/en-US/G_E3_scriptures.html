G.E3. Mahāyāna Scriptures

Mahāyāna scriptures are a large, layered collection rather than one short canon. They retain shared Buddhist foundations while adding sutras that shaped East Asian practice and philosophy. Traditional accounts of these texts and modern historical scholarship should be distinguished, not conflated.

1. A map of Mahāyāna scriptures

Important families include Perfection of Wisdom texts; Lotus, Flower Garland, Pure Land, Buddha-nature, and Yogācāra sutras; and later tantric texts. In East Asia, most are read through Chinese translations and commentaries. The scriptural map is plural: a sutra's importance varies by school, place, and practice.

2. Heart and Diamond sutras

The Heart Sutra is a concise Perfection of Wisdom text, famous for teaching that form is emptiness and emptiness is form. The Diamond Sutra repeatedly loosens attachment even to Buddhist concepts: bodhisattva activity is to be compassionate without clinging to a fixed self, person, or achievement. Neither text teaches nihilism.

3. Lotus Sutra

The Lotus Sutra teaches the One Vehicle: diverse teachings are skilful means leading beings toward buddhahood. Its parables—such as the burning house and the prodigal son—present compassion as adapting teaching to a listener's capacity. It became especially influential in Tiantai, Tendai, and Nichiren traditions.

4. Flower Garland Sutra

The Flower Garland Sutra presents a vast vision of mutual interpenetration: every phenomenon contains and is contained by every other without losing its own particularity. Its final Gandavyūha chapter follows Sudhana's pilgrimage through many teachers. It is the principal text of the Huayan tradition.

5. Vimalakīrti Sutra

The Vimalakīrti Sutra centres on a wise lay practitioner who teaches even distinguished disciples and bodhisattvas. It is influential for its teaching of non-duality and for affirming that profound practice is not confined to monastic life.

6. Summary and next steps

  • Mahāyāna scriptures are a diverse collection received and interpreted through different schools.
  • The Heart and Diamond sutras clarify emptiness; the Lotus teaches skilful means and universal buddhahood; the Flower Garland depicts interpenetration; and Vimalakīrti explores non-duality and lay practice.

Glossary:

  • Perfection of Wisdom (Prajñāpāramitā): a family of sutras focused on wisdom and emptiness.
  • Skilful means (upāya): teaching adapted compassionately to a listener's capacity.
  • One Vehicle: the Lotus Sutra's teaching that diverse paths ultimately lead to buddhahood.
  • Non-duality: freedom from rigid, grasped distinctions.

Source notes

  • The Heart, Diamond, Lotus, Flower Garland, and Vimalakīrti sutras are primary sources for this overview.
  • Traditional claims about scripture and modern textual history are distinct interpretive frameworks.
