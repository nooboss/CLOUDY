G.D5.22. Laos: Kaṭhina — the Robe-Offering Season
From the full moon of the eleventh to the full moon of the twelfth Buddhist month

Kaṭhina is Laos's annual robe-offering season, one of the community's major occasions for generosity. It lasts one lunar month, from the full moon ending the rains retreat to the next full moon. The shared Vinaya rules are introduced in G.D3; Thailand observes the corresponding season as Thot Kathin.

1. The one-month season

The season begins after the retreat-ending full moon and closes at the next full moon. It is limited to this one-month period, is offered to a Saṅgha that completed the rains retreat, and may be conducted only once each year at a monastery. The robe is offered to the monastic community, not as a personal gift to one individual.

2. Kaṭhina in Laos

Three layers for understanding the season

  • Foundational meaning: a Vinaya-regulated offering of robes to an eligible Saṅgha after the rains retreat.
  • Shared practice: each monastery selects a day for a robe procession, chanting, Dhamma teaching, the robe offering, and gifts of requisites (Pali: paccaya).
  • Custom: Lao communities may hold festive robe processions with music, dance, decorated vehicles, food, and money trees. Villages and families often pool offerings, and people living away from home may return for their village monastery's ceremony.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Each monastery sets its own date within the one-month season.
  • Join through pooled contributions, practical requisites, or the procession according to your means.
  • Dress modestly and remember that merit concerns the intention to give, not the size of an offering.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Kaṭhina is Laos's one-month post-retreat robe-offering season.
  • Its shared core is a Vinaya-regulated offering to an eligible monastic community.
  • Its Lao expression is a major communal festival of processions, pooled offerings, and homecoming.

Glossary:

  • Kaṭhina (Pali): the regulated robe-offering ceremony and season after the rains retreat.
  • Thot Kathin (Thai): Thailand's name for the corresponding season.
  • Requisites (Pali: paccaya): robes, food, lodging, and medicines.
  • Money tree: a decorated offering tree to which money is attached as a customary donation.

Source notes

  • Kaṭhina regulations — Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga, Kathinakkhandhaka - Theravāda Buddhism.
  • Lao processions and communal forms of sponsorship are customary practices.
  • The lunar season does not correspond to fixed Gregorian dates.
