G.F5.16. (Phugpa) Dharmapāla Practice Day
Twenty-ninth day of each Tibetan month

The 29th day of each month in the Tibetan Buddhist calendar is the dharma protector (Sanskrit: dharmapāla) practice day — the offering ceremony (Sanskrit: pūjā) days (explained in article G.F5.1) dedicated to the Dharma Protectors, praying that obstacles be removed — clearing away difficulties in practice and daily life. The 29th day is the day just before the new moon — the end of the Tibetan month (a 30-day month); a monthly "clearing" occasion — in the same spirit as the year-end cleansing of Gutor (article G.F5.11), but on a monthly rhythm.

1. Who the Protectors are — the correct understanding

The dharma protector (Sanskrit: dharmapāla) figures — appearing in wrathful or peaceful forms — have the role of protecting practitioners and places of worship in Tibetan Buddhist symbolism. The correct understanding (as discussed in article G.F2 on mantra — maṇḍala — deity): the Protectors are symbolic images of the capacities that counter afflictions and obstacles; "asking the Protectors for help" in practice means awakening that capacity within one's own mind — not begging favour from an external spirit. In monasteries, the Protectors are enshrined in a separate protector chapel; each lineage (Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug — article G.F2) has its own principal Protectors.

2. How the rite is performed

  • In monasteries: recitation of the Protector offering ceremonies — for example the recitation of the six-armed Mahakala (Sanskrit: Mahākāla) of the Kagyu lineage — or the Protectors of the other lineages; offering ritual cake (Tibetan: torma) — the moulded symbolic offering cakes characteristic of Tibetan rites.
  • Lay followers: join in or recite a short practice at home; praying for obstacles to be removed — so that practice and daily life go smoothly.
  • Monthly rhythm: the 29th day is the end-of-month "clearing" → the 30th day (the new moon — article G.F5.17) is a fresh beginning; a monthly rhythm of "clearing out the old — welcoming the new".

3. Three layers for reading the 29th day

  • Doctrinal layer: reviewing the month's obstacles — awakening the inner capacity against mental afflictions (Sanskrit: kleśa) (article G.C4 on afflictions and the path of transformation).
  • Shared practice layer: the Protector offering ceremony, offering torma, sutra chanting.
  • Custom layer: end-of-month clearing customs; the forms of the offering ceremony differ among the lineages.

4. Summary and next step

  • The 29th day of each month = the Dharmapāla practice day — the end-of-month "clearing"; praying that obstacles be removed.
  • The correct understanding: the Protectors symbolise the inner capacity against afflictions — not an external spirit.

Glossary:

  • Dharma protector (Sanskrit: dharmapāla): the Protectors — figures protecting the teaching in Tibetan Buddhist symbolism.
  • Offering ceremony (Sanskrit: pūjā): a rite of offering with material gifts and prayers — see article G.F5.
  • Ritual cake (Tibetan: torma): the moulded symbolic offering cakes — the characteristic offering object of Tibetan rites.
  • Mental afflictions (Sanskrit: kleśa): the emotions and states of mind that cause suffering (greed — hatred — delusion — article G.C4).
  • Protector chapel: the shrine room for the Protector figures in a Tibetan monastery.
  • Six-armed Mahakala (Sanskrit: Mahākāla): an emblematic Protector — especially honoured in the Kagyu lineage.
  • Year-end cleansing (Tibetan: Gutor): the year-end clearing rite — see article G.F5.11; the monthly 29th day is a "small Gutor" on a monthly rhythm (a memorable label used in this article).
