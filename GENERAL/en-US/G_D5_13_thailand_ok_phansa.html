G.D5.13. Thailand: Ok Phansa — the Invitation Ceremony
Full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month

Ok Phansa means “leaving the rains” in Thai. It concludes the rains retreat with Pavāraṇā, the Invitation Ceremony in which monastics invite one another to point out faults seen, heard, or suspected during the three months of communal life. It falls on the full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month, exactly three months after Khao Phansa.

1. The end of the retreat

The full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month completes the retreat that began on the first day of the ninth month. Vietnam records the same full moon with another lunar month name. The following day opens the one-month kaṭhina robe-offering season.

2. Ok Phansa in Thailand

Three layers for understanding the day

  • Foundational meaning: Pavāraṇā, by which monastics invite mutual correction after the retreat.
  • Shared practice: laypeople visit monasteries, make offerings, hear the Dhamma, and join closing services.
  • Custom: it is a Thai public holiday and opens the late-rains festival season, including Tak Bat Devo and kaṭhina robe offerings. Local boat races are cultural celebrations, not monastic rites.

Pavāraṇā is an internal monastic procedure. Laypeople do not perform it; their role is to support the community and prepare for the coming kaṭhina season.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Mutual acknowledgement of faults is intended as compassionate accountability, not argument.
  • This is a good time to learn about the kaṭhina robe offering at a nearby monastery.
  • Attend respectfully and follow the temple's instructions.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Ok Phansa ends the rains retreat on the full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month.
  • Its core is the monastic Pavāraṇā Invitation Ceremony.
  • In Thailand it begins the late-rains festival sequence, including Tak Bat Devo and kaṭhina.

Next: G.D5.14 presents Tak Bat Devo, the alms-offering observance associated with the Buddha's descent from the Tāvatiṃsa heaven.

Glossary:

  • Ok Phansa (Thai): leaving the rains.
  • Pavāraṇā (Pali): the Invitation Ceremony, in which monastics invite feedback concerning faults.
  • Completed the rains retreat: having remained for the full retreat at a monastery; this is relevant to subsequent kaṭhina privileges.
  • Kaṭhina: the robe-offering season after the rains retreat.

Source notes

  • Pavāraṇā and its Vinaya setting — Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga - Theravāda Buddhism.
  • Thai public observance and associated festivals belong to the customary layer.
  • The lunar date varies in the Gregorian calendar.
