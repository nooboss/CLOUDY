G.F5.7. (Phugpa) Dzamling Chisang — Universal Incense Offering
Fifteenth day of the fifth Tibetan month

The Dzamling Chisang (Tibetan: Dzamling Chisang, meaning "the world raises incense" — "dzamling" is the world, "chisang" is burning incense as an offering everywhere — the Universal Incense Offering, the worldwide incense-offering festival) takes place on the 15th day of the fifth Tibetan month — the full-moon day of the fifth month. This is an incense-offering festival — Tibetan monasteries and families burn fragrant grasses and woods as offerings, with prayers for the well-being of the environment and of all beings; a distinctive practice of Tibetan Buddhism rarely found in other traditions.

1. Meaning of the incense offering

In Tibetan Buddhism, the incense offering (Tibetan: sang — the ritual of burning incense with fragrant smoke) is an offering made through fragrant smoke spreading everywhere — symbolising prayers spreading in all directions, and the aspiration to purify space (article G.C4 on the conception of realms — the environment as shaped by karma). The festival name "the world raises incense" expresses the scale of this aspiration: offerings are made not only within monasteries, but for the whole world — praying for peace, harvests, and the health of all beings. This day is often linked with the account that it was the time when the Buddha taught the teachings on purifying the environment, according to the tradition of some Tibetan lineages — this part belongs to the lineage-tradition layer and is recorded here at an introductory level.

2. How the observance is performed

  • At monasteries: sang rituals (incense burning) — burning sacred grasses and woods in special burners; chanting sutras and prayers; in Bhutan, this festival is a major occasion when ceremonial scarves (Tibetan: khata) and new prayer flags are hung across mountain tops (the custom layer — article G.F2).
  • At home: families burn incense sticks and fragrant grasses before their houses; they offer an incense burner on the roof or in the courtyard; and they pray for the coming planting season.
  • A further meaning: the festival falls at the start of summer on the high plateau — the time when herding and nomadic communities move through mountain regions; the incense offering also carries the meaning of asking permission of nature (in the folk understanding) before entering the active season (the custom layer of high-plateau communities).

3. Three layers for reading Dzamling Chisang

  • Doctrinal — practice layer: offering through incense — spreading prayers, the aspiration to purify the environment and beings.
  • Shared practice layer: burning incense, chanting sutras, praying — linked to the seasonal cycle of high-plateau life.
  • Custom layer: hanging new prayer flags, customs of asking permission of nature — Tibetan — Bhutanese culture.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Dzamling Chisang is the 15th day of the fifth Tibetan month — the full moon; the incense-offering festival for the whole world — praying for the well-being of the environment and beings.
  • Its distinctive point: the festival centres on the sang ritual — offering through smoke — a distinctive feature of Tibetan Buddhism.

Glossary:

  • Dzamling Chisang (Tibetan, "dzamling" is the world, "chisang" is offering incense everywhere — the Universal Incense Offering): the worldwide incense-offering festival — the 15th day of the fifth Tibetan month.
  • Incense offering (Tibetan: sang): the ritual of burning fragrant grasses and woods as an offering through smoke — a distinctive practice of Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Incense burner: the place for burning incense — a familiar implement of Tibetan rituals.
  • Ceremonial scarf (Tibetan: khata): the white scarf — an offering expressing reverence and purity in Tibetan culture.
  • Realms — the environment as shaped by karma (article G.C4): the Buddhist conception of the connection between actions (karma) and the living environment.
