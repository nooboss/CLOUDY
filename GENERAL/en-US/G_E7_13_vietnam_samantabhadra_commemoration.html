G.E7.13. Vietnam: Samantabhadra Bodhisattva Commemoration
Twenty-first day of the second lunar month

This day honours Samantabhadra, the bodhisattva of great vows and practice, on the twenty-first day of the second lunar month in Vietnamese custom.

1. Who is Samantabhadra?

Samantabhadra represents putting insight into action. Often depicted riding an elephant, he complements Mañjuśrī's wisdom: wisdom sees clearly, while vows and practice carry that insight into relationships and the world.

2. Why this date?

The date is a Vietnamese devotional calendar convention, not independently verified biography.

3. The Ten Great Vows

The Flower Garland Sutra associates Samantabhadra with ten broad vows: reverence for buddhas, praise of their qualities, extensive offerings, repentance, rejoicing in others' goodness, requesting teaching, requesting buddhas to remain, learning from the Buddha, adapting to beings, and dedicating merit universally. They are practical orientations, not a checklist for earning rewards.

4. What newcomers should know

  • Choose one vow as a daily practice, such as rejoicing in another person's good action.
  • Temple offerings are voluntary.
  • Let vows lead to concrete conduct rather than recitation alone.

5. Summary and next steps

  • Samantabhadra is honoured on the twenty-first day of the second lunar month.
  • He represents the transformation of wisdom into vows and compassionate action.
