G.D5.17. Laos: Boun Khao Chi — Saṅgha Day
Full moon of the third Buddhist month (Tawai)

Boun Khao Chi is the Lao name for Māgha Pūjā, or Saṅgha Day. It recalls the traditional gathering of 1,250 arahants and the Ovāda Pāṭimokkha teaching. In Laos it falls on the full moon of the third Buddhist month, the same lunar occasion as Thailand's Makha Bucha.

1. The meaning of the name

Reading the Lao name

  • Boun: a merit-making festival, a common prefix for Lao Buddhist observances.
  • Khao chi: grilled sticky rice, traditionally shaped, roasted, and offered to monastics on this day.

The name therefore joins the shared doctrinal observance of Māgha Pūjā with a local customary offering.

2. Boun Khao Chi in Laos

Three layers for understanding the day

  • Foundational meaning: the assembly of 1,250 arahants and the teaching: avoid evil, cultivate good, and purify the mind.
  • Shared practice: temple visits, Dhamma listening, offerings, and stronger observance on the full moon.
  • Custom: offering grilled sticky rice and village-based arrangements vary by locality.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Morning is the usual time for food offerings; khao chi is welcome but not obligatory.
  • Joining the preparation of the rice offering is a simple and communal way to participate.
  • Follow the monastery's guidance and approach the day with quiet generosity.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Boun Khao Chi is Laos's Māgha Pūjā observance, held on the third-month full moon.
  • Its distinctive local feature is the offering of grilled sticky rice.
  • The central teaching remains the shared Theravāda message of ethical restraint, wholesome action, and purification of mind.

Glossary:

  • Boun (Lao): a merit-making festival.
  • Khao chi (Lao): grilled sticky rice offered to monastics.
  • Māgha Pūjā (Pali): Saṅgha Day.
  • Arahant (Pali: arahant): one who has fully ended the defilements of mind.
  • Ovāda Pāṭimokkha: the teaching associated with the traditional gathering of 1,250 arahants.
