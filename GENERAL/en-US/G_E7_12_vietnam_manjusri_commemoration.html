G.E7.12. Vietnam: Mañjuśrī Bodhisattva Commemoration
Fourth day of the fourth lunar month

This observance honours Mañjuśrī, the bodhisattva of wisdom, on the fourth day of the fourth lunar month in Vietnamese custom.

  • 2. Why this date?
  • 3. How is the day observed?

1. Who is Mañjuśrī?

Mañjuśrī represents penetrating wisdom. He is commonly depicted with a flaming sword that cuts ignorance and a sutra resting on a lotus. Wisdom here is not cleverness or collecting information; it is clear seeing of dependent arising, impermanence, and emptiness.

2. Why this date?

The date is a Vietnamese devotional calendar convention, not an independently verified historical birthday.

3. How is the day observed?

Temples may chant sutras, make offerings, undertake study, and dedicate merit. Students often use the occasion to renew diligent learning, but wisdom is expressed equally through ethical conduct and humility.

4. What newcomers should know

  • Read, listen, and ask questions carefully rather than seeking quick certainty.
  • Offerings are voluntary.
  • Let the day encourage the wisdom that reduces harm.

5. Summary and next steps

  • Mañjuśrī is honoured on the fourth day of the fourth lunar month.
  • He embodies wisdom that cuts ignorance and guides compassionate action.

Glossary:

  • Mañjuśrī: Mahāyāna bodhisattva of wisdom.
  • Wisdom: insight that sees reality clearly and reduces ignorance.

Source notes

  • The Vietnamese date is customary; Mañjuśrī's imagery appears across Mahāyāna sutras and art.
