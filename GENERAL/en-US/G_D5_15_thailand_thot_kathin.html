G.D5.15. Thailand: Thot Kathin — the Kaṭhina Robe-Offering Season
From the full moon of the eleventh to the full moon of the twelfth Buddhist month

Thot Kathin is Thailand's kaṭhina robe-offering season, a major annual period of generosity. It lasts one lunar month, from the full moon ending the rains retreat to the next full moon. The Vinaya foundations of kaṭhina are introduced in G.D3.

1. A one-month season

Kaṭhina begins on the day after the full moon concluding the rains retreat and ends at the next full moon. The core rules are shared across Theravāda countries: the ceremony is held only during this period, for a Saṅgha that completed the rains retreat, and no more than once in a year at a given monastery. The offering is made to the community, not to an individual monastic.

2. Thot Kathin in Thailand

Three layers for understanding the season

  • Foundational meaning: offering robes to the Saṅgha after the completed rains retreat.
  • Shared practice: a monastery selects one day for chanting, Dhamma talks, robe offering, and gifts of the four requisites—robes, food, lodging, and medicines.
  • Custom: Thai processions may include music, dance, food, and ton kathin, money trees. Families, businesses, and communities may sponsor the ceremony together; royal kaṭhina offerings have also been an important historical form.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Each monastery chooses its own date within the season; ask the local community for its calendar.
  • Contributions of money, robes, or practical items are voluntary and may be pooled.
  • Processions can be festive, but visitors should remain modest and respectful.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Thot Kathin is Thailand's one-month kaṭhina season after the rains retreat.
  • The offering is made to an eligible monastic community, within the Vinaya's limited period.
  • Thai customs give it a large communal festival character.

Glossary:

  • Thot Kathin (Thai): kaṭhina robe offering.
  • Kaṭhina (Pali): the post-retreat robe-offering ceremony and its regulated season.
  • Requisites (Pali: paccaya): robes, food, lodging, and medicines.
  • Ton kathin (Thai): a money tree prepared as an offering to a monastery.

Source notes

  • Kaṭhina regulations — Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga, Kathinakkhandhaka - Theravāda Buddhism.
  • Thai processions and sponsorship forms are customary practices.
  • The lunar season does not correspond to fixed Gregorian dates.
