G.E7.5. Vietnam: The Buddha's Renunciation (Abhiniṣkramaṇa)
Eighth day of the second lunar month

This observance commemorates the Buddha's Great Renunciation (Sanskrit: abhiniṣkramaṇa), traditionally kept in Vietnam on the eighth day of the second lunar month. It recalls Prince Siddhārtha's decision to leave royal life in order to seek a way beyond ageing, illness, and death. The historical and traditional accounts are introduced in G.B2.

1. What does the day commemorate?

According to traditional biography, Siddhārtha encountered the realities of old age, sickness, death, and a renunciant. He left home to search for liberation, first trying teachers and severe asceticism before discovering the Middle Way and awakening. The story should not be read as rejection of family or society; it is a radical response to the universal problem of suffering.

2. Why this date?

Vietnamese Mahāyāna calendars traditionally mark the Great Renunciation on the eighth day of the second lunar month. The date is a devotional calendar convention. Different traditions may remember the event on different dates or within a wider life-of-the-Buddha sequence.

3. The meaning of the “Great Going Forth”

The renunciation is “great” because it is not escape from responsibility. It begins a search for a path that can benefit all beings. For monastics, it is the root image of going forth. For laypeople, it can mean a smaller but real renunciation: letting go of harmful habits, greed, resentment, or ways of living that increase suffering. The Middle Way warns against both indulgence and self-mortification.

4. What newcomers should know

  • Temple services may include chanting, recollection of the Buddha's life, offerings, and a Dhamma talk.
  • Do not treat the day as pressure to leave lay life. Buddhism recognises both lay and monastic paths.
  • Choose one practical form of “going forth” from an unwholesome habit, and sustain it kindly.
  • Offerings are voluntary; modest dress and quiet attention are sufficient.

5. Summary and next steps

  • The Great Renunciation is kept in Vietnam on the eighth day of the second lunar month.
  • It commemorates Siddhārtha's search for liberation and the beginning of the path that led to awakening.
  • For laypeople, its meaning is not abandoning ordinary life but relinquishing what causes harm and cultivating the Middle Way.

Glossary:

  • Abhiniṣkramaṇa (Sanskrit): the Great Going Forth or Great Renunciation.
  • Going forth: leaving household life for the monastic path; more broadly, turning away from harmful habits toward practice.
  • Middle Way: the path avoiding sensual indulgence and self-mortification.
  • Siddhārtha: the prince who later became Śākyamuni Buddha.
