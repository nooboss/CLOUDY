G.D5.12. Thailand: Khao Phansa — Beginning the Rains Retreat
First day of the ninth Buddhist month

Khao Phansa, literally “entering the rains” in Thai, begins the rains retreat (Pali: vassa): three months in which monastics remain at one residence for more intensive training. In Thailand it begins on the day after Asalha Bucha, the first day of the ninth Buddhist month, and concludes on the full moon of the eleventh month with Ok Phansa.

1. A lunar-calendar beginning

The retreat begins on the day after the Āsāḷha full moon. Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia describe it as the first day of the ninth Buddhist month; Vietnam uses a different lunar month name. These are different calendar conventions for the same beginning of the three-month retreat. The Vinaya basis and purpose of vassa are explained in G.D3.

2. Khao Phansa in Thailand

Three layers for understanding the day

  • Foundational meaning: the Vinaya period of three months' residence during the rainy season.
  • Shared practice: laypeople make offerings for the retreat and may strengthen their precepts or meditation.
  • Custom: Thailand observes a public holiday and offers large thian phansa, or rains-retreat candles. Temporary ordination and three-month resolutions, such as abstaining from alcohol, are encouraged customs rather than mandatory doctrine.

The season is often understood as a community-wide period for wholesome commitments: a modest, sustainable practice undertaken for three months can be more meaningful than an ambitious but short-lived resolution.

3. What newcomers should know

  • It is an excellent time to begin a regular practice of Dhamma study, ethical conduct, or daily meditation.
  • Offering candles or practical requisites to a monastery is voluntary.
  • At a temple ceremony, arrive early, dress modestly, and follow the community's lead.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Khao Phansa begins Thailand's rains retreat on the first day of the ninth Buddhist month.
  • Thai customs include retreat candles, temporary ordination, and personal wholesome commitments.
  • The retreat ends on the full moon of the eleventh month at Ok Phansa.

Glossary:

  • Khao Phansa (Thai): entering the rains.
  • Rains retreat (Pali: vassa): the three-month period of residence and intensive practice for monastics.
  • Thian phansa (Thai): large candles offered to monasteries for the rains-retreat season.
  • Temporary ordination: ordination undertaken for a limited period.

Source notes

  • The Vinaya rules for the rains retreat — Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga - Theravāda Buddhism.
  • Thai public observance, candles, and personal commitments belong to the customary layer of the festival.
  • The lunar date varies in the Gregorian calendar.
