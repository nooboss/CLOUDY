G.D5.24. Laos: Boun Khao Salak — Lottery-Draw Offering
Full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month

Boun Khao Salak is a Lao observance held on the full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month, the same day as Boun Ork Phansa, which concludes the rains retreat (G.D5.21). It combines a lottery-draw offering with dedication of merit to the departed, continuing the theme of Boun Khao Padab Din two weeks earlier (G.D5.23). The shared Buddhist basis—giving and dedicating merit—is introduced in G.C4.

1. What does “Khao Salak” mean? — the lottery-draw offering

The Lao name can be read by its parts:

Reading the Lao name

  • Boun (Lao): a merit-making festival (G.D5.17).
  • Khao (Lao): rice or cooked rice, the central food offering.
  • Salak (Lao; from Pali salākā): a lottery stick or slip used in drawing lots.

The observance is distinctive for its lottery-draw offering, called salak in Lao. Donors write their names—or those of deceased relatives—on the offering slips; monastics then draw lots to receive the offerings. This form embodies non-selective giving: one gives without designating a particular recipient, as a way of practising relinquishment. Lot-drawn allocations are known in early Pali tradition; in Laos, the practice continues in a vivid local form through offerings marked with the names of relatives and connected with the dedication of merit (G.D5.23).

2. How is the day observed?

Three layers for understanding Boun Khao Salak in Laos

  • Foundational meaning: non-selective giving as a practice of relinquishment, together with dedication of merit to the departed (G.C4).
  • Shared practice: families bring rice, food, and requisites to the temple; they may name deceased relatives in the offerings; monastics draw lots for the portions; chanting and merit dedication follow.
  • Custom: because the observance coincides with Boun Ork Phansa (G.D5.21), many places combine the occasions into a larger sequence: the monastic ceremony at the temple, the laity's salak offerings, and later boat-lantern festivities. The arrangement varies by locality.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Temples are busier than usual because the day coincides with the end of the rains retreat; arrive early if you want a convenient place.
  • If you would like to make a salak offering, ask the temple about its procedure beforehand, as local arrangements differ.
  • For the doctrinal basis of dedicating merit, see G.C4; for the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Boun Khao Salak is a Lao lottery-draw offering and merit-dedication observance for the departed, held on the full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month, when the rains retreat ends.
  • Its distinctive spirit is non-selective giving as a practice of relinquishment; the salak custom has roots in early Pali tradition.

Glossary:

  • Boun Khao Salak (Lao; from Pali salākā): Laos's lottery-draw offering observance.
  • Salākā (Pali): a lottery stick or slip used when drawing lots; its use in allocating offerings has roots in early Pali tradition.
  • Non-selective giving: giving without designating a particular recipient, as a practice of relinquishment in generosity.
  • Dedication of merit: dedicating the wholesome results of a good action to others, including deceased relatives (G.C4).
