G.D5.7. Vietnam: Kaṭhina — the Robe-Offering Season
From the sixteenth day of the ninth to the fifteenth day of the tenth lunar month

Kaṭhina is the one-month robe-offering season that opens immediately after the rains retreat. It is a Vinaya-regulated offering to an eligible Saṅgha, not a personal gift to an individual monastic. In this article's Vietnamese calendar notation, it runs from the sixteenth day of the ninth lunar month to the fifteenth day of the tenth, after Pavāraṇā (G.D5.6).

1. The one-month season

The season begins on the day after the retreat-ending full moon and ends at the next full moon. It is limited to this one month, may be held only once each year at a monastery, and is offered to the monastic community that completed the rains retreat. These conditions are central to the Vinaya, not optional local customs. Thailand and Laos record the same season from the eleventh-month full moon to the twelfth-month full moon (G.D5.15, G.D5.22), while Cambodia observes its Kathen season on the same lunar rhythm (G.D5.32).

2. Kaṭhina in Vietnam

Three layers for understanding kaṭhina in Vietnam

  • Foundational meaning: a Vinaya-regulated robe offering to an eligible Saṅgha after the rains retreat.
  • Shared practice: monasteries select a day for chanting, Dhamma listening, a robe procession, the robe offering, and gifts of requisites (Pali: paccaya).
  • Custom: Vietnamese Theravāda and Khmer communities may hold festive processions, pooled offerings, music, food, and homecoming. These community forms are cultural expressions, not the Vinaya ceremony itself.

The offering should be understood as generosity to the community. The Saṅgha receives and assigns the robe according to its discipline; intention matters more than the value of a contribution.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Each monastery sets its own ceremony date within the one-month season.
  • Join through pooled offerings, practical requisites, or the procession according to your means.
  • Remember that the robe is offered to the Saṅgha, not to a personally chosen monastic.
  • Dress modestly and follow local guidance; giving is voluntary (G.C4).

4. Summary and next steps

  • Kaṭhina is Vietnam's one-month post-retreat robe-offering season, from the sixteenth day of the ninth to the fifteenth day of the tenth lunar month.
  • Its core is a Vinaya-regulated offering to a Saṅgha that completed the rains retreat, once per monastery per year.
  • Processions and other community festivities are customary forms around that shared core.

Glossary:

  • Kaṭhina (Pali): the regulated robe-offering ceremony and one-month season after the rains retreat.
  • Saṅgha (Pali): the Buddhist monastic community.
  • Requisites (Pali: paccaya): robes, food, lodging, and medicines.
  • Completed the rains retreat: having resided for the full retreat, a condition for kaṭhina eligibility.

Source notes

  • Kaṭhina regulations — Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga, Kathinakkhandhaka - Theravāda Buddhism.
  • Vietnamese and Khmer processions are customary community expressions.
  • The lunar season is not converted into fixed Gregorian dates.
