G.F5.12. (Phugpa) Tārā and Medicine Buddha Practice Day
Eighth day of each Tibetan month

The eighth day of each month in the Tibetan Buddhist calendar is the practice day of Bodhisattva Tārā (Sanskrit: Tārā, Tara the Liberator — the female Bodhisattva, "the Liberator") and Medicine Buddha (Sanskrit: Bhaiṣajyaguru — introduced in article G.E7.25). At Tibetan monasteries, this day is marked by pūjā (Sanskrit: pūjā — offering ceremony — explained in article G.F5.1) with sutra chanting and mantra recitation offered to the two deities; lay devotees likewise recite mantras and pray for healing and liberation from fear.

1. Why Tārā and Medicine Buddha

  • Bodhisattva Tārā — the female Bodhisattva of liberation from fear and danger: in the Tibetan tradition, Tārā is revered as the "compassionate mother" — swift to hear cries for help; she appears in different forms (Green Tārā — White Tārā...); the central text is the Praise to the Twenty-one Taras (in Tibetan — the hymn to the twenty-one forms of Tārā).
  • Medicine Buddha — the Buddha of medicine and healing (article G.E7.25): prayers for health — for healing the illnesses of body and mind; the Medicine Buddha sutra and mantra are recited for the sick — before surgeries, and for the well-being of the community.

The two deities are joined on the same eighth day because of their shared theme: well-being — healing — liberation from fear.

2. How to practise on this day

  • At monasteries: chanting the Tārā pūjā and the Medicine Buddha pūjā; offering gifts (water, flowers, butter lamps — yak-butter lamps used as candles); chanting the related sutras and mantras.
  • Lay devotees: reciting the Tārā mantra (Sanskrit: mantra — the short phrase "OM TARE TUTTARE TURE SOHA" — as transmitted in the Tibetan tradition) or the Medicine Buddha mantra; praying for the sick — for the well-being of the family.
  • Practice context: this is one of the six monthly practice days of group G.F5 (G.F5.12 → G.F5.17) — days when monasteries and devotees intensify their practice.

3. Three layers for reading the eighth day

  • Doctrinal layer: praying for healing and liberation from fear — directed towards the compassionate qualities of the commemorated deities (article G.C4 on the shared path of practice).
  • Shared practice layer: chanting sutras, reciting mantras, making offerings — a regular monthly rhythm of practice.
  • Custom layer: the custom of choosing auspicious days to pray for well-being or to seek medical treatment; accompanying cultural customs.

4. Summary and next steps

  • The 8th day of each Tibetan calendar month = the practice day of Tārā and Medicine Buddha — theme: healing — liberation from fear.
  • One of the six monthly practice days of group G.F5.

Glossary:

  • Tārā (Sanskrit: Tārā, Tara the Liberator): the female Bodhisattva of liberation from fear — revered as the "compassionate mother" in Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Bodhisattva (Sanskrit: bodhisattva, enlightenment being): one who vows to attain awakening in order to save beings — see article G.C4.
  • Medicine Buddha (Sanskrit: Bhaiṣajyaguru): Medicine Buddha — the Buddha of medicine and healing (article G.E7.25).
  • Offering ceremony (Sanskrit: pūjā): an offering rite with gifts and prayers — see article G.F5.
  • Mantra (Sanskrit: mantra): a short phrase repeated in practice — see article G.F5.
  • Praise to the Twenty-one Taras (in Tibetan): a short hymn recounting the twenty-one forms of Tārā — one of the most popular hymns of Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Butter lamps: yak-butter lamps — a familiar offering at Himalayan monasteries.

Source notes

  • The Tārā — Medicine Buddha pūjā rites and mantras — as transmitted in the Tibetan Buddhist lineages; presented at an introductory level.
  • The Tārā mantra — given in the popular transmission of the tradition; beginners need not practise it formally — understanding its symbolic meaning is enough (article G.E6).
  • The monthly practice day of the 8th day of each Tibetan calendar month — the standard festival calendar of this subsection (the Phugpa system).
