G.E7.24. China: Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva Commemoration (Dìzàng Púsà Shèngdàn)
Thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month

China's Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva commemoration (Chinese: Dìzàng Púsà Shèngdàn, Kṣitigarbha's Birthday) commemorates the birth of Kṣitigarbha, the Earth-Store Bodhisattva, on the thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month. Vietnam keeps the same date (core article G.E7.14). Coming half a month after Ullambana (the preceding article — G.E7.23), the Kṣitigarbha commemoration continues the theme of dedicating merit to the departed — the bodhisattva is honoured as the helper of beings in realms of suffering, after his celebrated vow: "Until the suffering realms are empty, I vow not to realise buddhahood" (article G.E7.14).

1. The observance in China

On the thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month (falling on the twenty-ninth in short months), Chinese Buddhist monasteries hold sutra chanting and dedication of merit, above all the chanting of the Kṣitigarbha Sūtra (Chinese: Dìzàng Jīng, the Sutra of the Great Vows of Kṣitigarbha) — the sutra telling of the bodhisattva's vows and how to dedicate merit to the departed. Devotees come to temples to offer incense and make aspirations for parents and ancestors; in many places, forty-nine-day group chanting is held for the recently deceased, following Chinese custom. Mount Jiuhua (Chinese: Jiǔhuá Shān) — one of China's four sacred Buddhist mountains — is the bodhisattva's principal seat.

2. Three layers for understanding the Kṣitigarbha commemoration

  • Foundational meaning: the bodhisattva's vow — abandoning no one in realms of suffering; dedicating merit helps the departed (articles G.E7.14, G.C4).
  • Shared practice: chanting the Kṣitigarbha Sūtra, dedication of merit, offerings, incense.
  • Custom: forty-nine-day chanting and ancestral rites attached to the Chinese seventh lunar month — Chinese culture, not doctrine.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva is an image of compassion that abandons no one — even those in the most painful conditions; anyone can learn from this quality (article G.E7.14).
  • For what dedication of merit means and how to practise it, see G.E7.15 and G.C5.
  • For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.

4. Summary and next steps

  • China's Kṣitigarbha commemoration falls on the thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month — the same date as Vietnam (G.E7.14); its Chinese name is Dìzàng Púsà Shèngdàn.
  • Its theme is dedication of merit to the departed; its principal sutra is the Kṣitigarbha Sūtra.

Next: the following article — G.E7.25 — introduces China's Medicine Buddha Commemoration (Yàoshī Fó Shèngdàn), on the thirtieth day of the ninth lunar month.

Glossary:

  • Dìzàng Púsà Shèngdàn (Chinese, Kṣitigarbha's Birthday): the Chinese name for the Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva commemoration.
  • Kṣitigarbha (Sanskrit: Kṣitigarbha, Earth-Store Bodhisattva): the bodhisattva of the vow to help beings in realms of suffering (article G.E7.14).
  • Kṣitigarbha Sūtra (Chinese: Dìzàng Jīng, the Sutra of the Great Vows of Kṣitigarbha): the sutra telling of Kṣitigarbha's vows — a Mahāyāna sutra.
  • Mount Jiuhua (Chinese: Jiǔhuá Shān): the sacred mountain in Anhui, China — Kṣitigarbha's principal seat.
  • Four Sacred Buddhist Mountains of China: Wutai (Mañjuśrī), Emei (Samantabhadra), Putuo (Avalokiteśvara), Jiuhua (Kṣitigarbha).

Source notes

  • The festival's core meaning is presented fully in G.E7.14 with its corresponding sources.
  • The Kṣitigarbha Sūtra is a Mahāyāna sutra.
  • Forty-nine-day chanting and Mount Jiuhua are general descriptions of Chinese Buddhism.
  • The thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month (China) is the standard festival-calendar notation used in this subsection; it is not converted into a fixed Gregorian date, which varies by year.
