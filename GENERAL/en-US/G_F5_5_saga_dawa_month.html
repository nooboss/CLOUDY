G.F5.5. (Phugpa) Saga Dawa Month (Vaiśākha)
First to thirtieth days of the fourth Tibetan month

The Saga Dawa Month (Tibetan: Saga Dawa, from the Indian name of the fourth month — Sanskrit: Vaiśākha — the month of Vaiśākha) is the most important festival month of Tibetan Buddhism, running from the first to the thirtieth days of the fourth Tibetan month. The whole month commemorates the three greatest events of the Buddha's life — birth, enlightenment, and passing away — believed to converge in this month, climaxing on the 15th day (full moon) — Saga Dawa Düchen (next article — G.F5.6). This month corresponds to the Theravāda Vesak (article G.D5.3) and to the Mahāyāna days of the Buddha's Birth — Enlightenment — Passing Away (articles G.E7.2 — G.E7.6).

1. Why the whole month is a festival season

According to Tibetan tradition, the fourth month of the Tibetan Buddhist calendar (called Saga Dawa) is the month in which all three great events of the Buddha's life took place: birth, enlightenment, and passing away. The full-moon day (the 15th) is the day on which all three events converge — making it one of the four Düchen days (article G.F5.1). The whole month is therefore regarded as an occasion when merit is multiplied many times over — the closer to the full-moon day, the stronger the multiplication; this festival month corresponds to the Theravāda month of Vesak and to the East Asian Mahāyāna triad of observances (Buddha's Birthday on 8/4 — Enlightenment Day on 8/12 — Passing Away Day on 15/2 of the lunar calendar, articles G.E7.17 — G.E7.19).

2. What people practise throughout the month

  • Sutra chanting and mantra recitation (Sanskrit: mantra) more than on ordinary days; many people vow to keep precepts throughout the whole month (for example taking a vegetarian meal — eating without meat or fish — or not killing).
  • Offerings to monasteries; offering butter lamps.
  • Life release (Tibetan: tsetar — ransoming and freeing beings about to be killed) — very popular in this month.
  • Pilgrimage circuit around sacred places (Tibetan: kora, from the Sanskrit "cakra" — circle — a pilgrimage circuit around holy mountains and monasteries); the most famous is the pilgrimage circuit around Mount Kailash (Tibetan: Kang Rinpoche — "precious mountain") — a journey many undertake precisely in the Saga Dawa Month.

3. Three layers for reading the Saga Dawa Month

  • Foundational meaning: commemorating the three milestones of the Buddha's life — birth, enlightenment, and passing away — the same core as article G.B2 (the life of the Buddha) and article G.D5.3 (Vesak).
  • Shared practice: sutra chanting, keeping precepts, making offerings, offering lamps, life release.
  • Custom: the kora pilgrimage circuit around holy mountains; community festivals; customs tied to the high-plateau nomadic — farming season.

4. Summary and next steps

  • The Saga Dawa Month runs from the 1st to the 30th days of the fourth Tibetan month — the greatest festival month; it commemorates the birth — enlightenment — passing away of the Buddha.
  • Climax: the full-moon 15th day — Saga Dawa Düchen (G.F5.6).

Glossary:

  • Saga Dawa (Tibetan, from the Indian-system name of the fourth month — Sanskrit: Vaiśākha — the month of Vaiśākha): the Tibetan name of the greatest festival month — commemorating the three milestones of the Buddha's life.
  • Vaiśākha (Sanskrit: Vaiśākha): the name of the fourth month in the ancient Indian calendar system — the root name of the Vesak — Buddha's Birthday observances.
  • Mantra (Sanskrit: mantra): a short phrase repeated in practice — see article G.F5.
  • Life release (Tibetan: tsetar): ransoming and freeing beings about to be killed — a widespread compassionate act of Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Pilgrimage circuit (Tibetan: kora, from the Sanskrit "cakra" — circle): a devotional circuit around a sacred place (mountain, monastery, stupa) — a common practice of Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Mount Kailash (Tibetan: Kang Rinpoche — "precious mountain"): the sacred mountain in Tibet — the most famous pilgrimage-circuit site, visited by many pilgrims precisely in the Saga Dawa Month.
  • Butter lamps: lamps fuelled by butter — a familiar offering at Himalayan monasteries.

Source notes

  • The tradition of commemorating birth — enlightenment — passing away in the Saga Dawa Month is the Tibetan-tradition understanding; presented at an introductory level.
  • Kora, tsetar, and pilgrimage customs are the practice — custom layers of Tibetan Buddhism.
  • The festival dates of the 1st–30th days of the fourth Tibetan month follow the standard festival calendar of this subsection (the Phugpa system); they are not converted into a fixed Gregorian date, which varies by year.
