G.E7.27. Korea: Buddha's Birthday (Bucheonnim Osin Nal)
Eighth day of the fourth lunar month

Korea's Buddha's Birthday (Korean: Bucheonnim Osin Nal, the Day the Buddha Came to the World) commemorates the birth of the Buddha on the eighth day of the fourth lunar month — the same date as Vietnam's Buddha's Birthday (core article G.E7.2) and China's (article G.E7.17). Korea's distinctive features: the day is an official national public holiday and is associated with lotus lanterns — the best-known custom of Korean Buddhism.

1. The observance in Korea

Korean Buddhism belongs chiefly to the Jogye Order — the largest Zen school. On Buddha's Birthday, temples hold sutra chanting and prostrations, bathing of the infant Buddha image (the rite introduced in the core article G.E7.2), and people light lanterns and make aspirations. Unlike Vietnam, Korea's Buddha's Birthday is recognised by the government as a national public holiday — one of the country's two major religious holidays (alongside Christmas).

2. Lotus lanterns — the symbol of Korean Buddha's Birthday

During the Buddha's Birthday season, the streets of Seoul and other large cities are decorated with thousands of lotus lanterns (Korean: yeondeunghoe, Lotus Lantern Festival) — the lotus symbolises birth in the mud yet rising unstained (article G.C4). The custom of offering lamps to the Buddha has scriptural roots (light symbolises wisdom — see article G.E7.25 on lamp offerings); the Korean lotus lantern is a distinctive cultural and customary layer. Its climax is the grand lantern parade (tens of thousands of people carrying lanterns through central Seoul) — recognised as intangible cultural heritage.

Three layers for understanding Buddha's Birthday in Korea

  • Foundational meaning: commemorating the birth of the Buddha — the same core as G.E7.
  • Shared practice: sutra chanting, prostrations, bathing the infant Buddha image, lighting lanterns and making aspirations.
  • Custom: the Lotus Lantern Festival and the grand lantern parade — Korean custom; a national public holiday.

3. What newcomers should know

  • The Korean name Bucheonnim Osin Nal means "the day the Buddha came to this world" — a familiar expression, close to the image of "a child of humanity being born" (article G.E7.2).

4. Summary and next steps

  • Korea's Buddha's Birthday falls on the eighth day of the fourth lunar month — a national public holiday; its Korean name is Bucheonnim Osin Nal; its symbol is the lotus lantern.
  • It shares its date with Vietnam's Buddha's Birthday (G.E7.2) and China's (G.E7.17).

  • Bucheonnim Osin Nal (Korean, the Day the Buddha Came to the World): the Korean name for Buddha's Birthday.
  • Jogye Order: the largest Zen school of Korean Buddhism.
  • Zen (Sanskrit: dhyāna, Korean: Seon): cultivation through stillness of mind — see article G.C4.
