G.E7.16. Vietnam: Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva Commemoration
Thirteenth day of the seventh lunar month

This observance honours Mahāsthāmaprāpta, known in Vietnam as Đại Thế Chí, on the thirteenth day of the seventh lunar month. In Pure Land imagery, he stands with Amitābha and Avalokiteśvara, representing the power of wisdom.

1. Who is Mahāsthāmaprāpta?

Mahāsthāmaprāpta means “Great Power Arrived.” Together with Amitābha and Avalokiteśvara, he forms the central Pure Land triad. His power is not domination; it is the strength of collected mind and wisdom that supports awakening.

2. Why this date?

The thirteenth day of the seventh lunar month is a Vietnamese devotional-calendar convention, not independently established biography.

3. How is the day observed?

Temples may recite Amitābha's name, chant Pure Land texts, make offerings, and dedicate merit. The day is an opportunity to unite the three qualities represented by the Pure Land triad: Amitābha's awakened light, Avalokiteśvara's compassion, and Mahāsthāmaprāpta's wisdom and concentrated power.

4. What newcomers should know

  • Join recitation quietly and follow local guidance.
  • Practise steady attention in one small activity rather than looking for dramatic experiences.
  • Offerings are voluntary.

5. Summary — closing the Vietnamese calendar group

  • Mahāsthāmaprāpta is honoured on the thirteenth day of the seventh lunar month.
  • He represents the strength of wisdom and concentrated mind within Pure Land practice.
