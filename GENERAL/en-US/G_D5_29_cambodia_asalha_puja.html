G.D5.29. Cambodia: Asalha Puja — Dhamma Day
Full moon of the eighth Buddhist month (Āsāḷha)

Asalha Puja is the Khmer name for Dhamma Day (Pali: Āsāḷha Pūjā), the observance of the Buddha's first discourse at the Deer Park to his five former companions. There the Middle Way and the Four Noble Truths were first taught. Its core meaning is explained in G.D5.4, in the Vietnamese calendar group. In Cambodia, it falls on the full moon of the eighth Buddhist month, the month of Āsāḷha; the Khmer rains retreat begins the following day (G.D5.30).

1. What is the full moon of the eighth Buddhist month?

It is the full moon of the month of Āsāḷha, immediately before the rains retreat. In tradition, the first discourse and the Saṅgha's first rains retreat are closely connected events (G.D5.4). Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia designate this same full moon as the eighth Buddhist month; Vietnam records it as the fifteenth day of the sixth lunar month (G.D5.4). When an exact Gregorian date is needed, check the particular year (G.D5.1).

2. Asalha Puja in Cambodia

Three layers for understanding Asalha Puja in Cambodia

  • Foundational meaning: the Buddha's first discourse—the Middle Way and the Four Noble Truths (G.D5.4); this core is the same throughout Theravāda countries.
  • Shared practice: temple visits, Dhamma listening, offerings, and precept observance. In the evening, people make a candle procession around the temple, circling three times with candles, incense, and flowers—a shared Theravāda custom (G.D5.8).
  • Custom: the occasion opens the season for short-term ordination in Khmer custom. Many men temporarily ordain for the rains retreat, as in Thai and Lao custom (G.D5.11, G.D5.19). In Cambodia, having ordained, even briefly, is socially valued and associated with filial respect for one's parents. This is a cultural and social layer.

3. What newcomers should know

  • The rhythm from “festival of the teaching” to “season of practice” is especially clear: Asalha Puja on the full moon of the eighth month is followed immediately by Chol Vossa, the beginning of the rains retreat, on the first day of the ninth month.
  • Offer according to your means; listening to the Dhamma is central to the observance.
  • For the festival's teaching, read the core article: G.D5.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Asalha Puja is the Khmer name for Dhamma Day, held on the full moon of the eighth Buddhist month; its core is explained in G.D5.
  • Its Khmer features include an evening candle procession and the opening of the short-term ordination season, a custom deeply rooted in Cambodian social life.

Glossary:

  • Asalha Puja (Khmer, from Pali Āsāḷha pūjā): the Khmer name for Dhamma Day; its core meaning is explained in G.D5.
  • Turning the wheel of Dhamma (Pali: Dhammacakkappavattana): the image of the wheel of Dhamma beginning to turn, referring to the Buddha's first discourse.
  • Short-term ordination: the customary practice of ordaining for a defined period. In Cambodia it is associated with filial respect; a man who has experienced monastic life is customarily regarded as having reached maturity.
  • Buddhist Era (BE): a calendar era traditionally reckoned from the Buddha's final passing.
