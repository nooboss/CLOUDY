G.E7.29. Korea: Buddha's Nirvana Day (Yeolban-jaeil)
Fifteenth day of the second lunar month

Korea's Buddha's Nirvana Day (Korean: Yeolban-jaeil, Buddha's Nirvana Day) commemorates the Buddha's final passing on the fifteenth day of the second lunar month — the same date as Vietnam (core article G.E7.4) and China (article G.E7.19).

1. The observance in Korea

On the fifteenth day of the second lunar month, Korean temples hold sutra chanting, prostrations, and offerings; many monasteries join the day with talks on impermanence (Pali: anicca, impermanence) — the central lesson of the final passing (article G.B4). Monasteries with a great bell (Korean: beomjong, temple bell) sound it at dawn and dusk — its tone recalls the transience of all existence.

2. Three layers for understanding Korea's Nirvana Day

  • Foundational meaning: nirvana (Pali: Nibbāna, liberation) is the ending of all suffering; the final passing and the last teaching — articles G.E7.4, G.B4.
  • Shared practice: sutra chanting, prostrations, offerings; talks on impermanence.
  • Custom: the temple-bell sounding — a Korean Zen custom; remembering departed family members on this occasion, following Korean custom.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Final passing is the "complete rest" of an awakened one, not "death" in the ordinary sense (article G.B4).

Glossary:

  • Yeolban-jaeil (Korean, Buddha's Nirvana Day): the Korean name for the Buddha's final-passing day.
  • Nirvana (Pali: Nibbāna, liberation): the ending of all suffering — see article G.E7.
  • Impermanence (Pali: anicca): all things constantly change and abide nowhere — the central lesson of transience.
  • Temple bell (Korean: beomjong): the monastery's great bell — its sound joins the morning and evening services.

Source notes

  • The fifteenth day of the second lunar month (Korea) is the standard festival-calendar notation used in this subsection; it is not converted into a fixed Gregorian date, which varies by year.
