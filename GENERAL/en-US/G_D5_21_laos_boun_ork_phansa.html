G.D5.21. Laos: Boun Ork Phansa — the Invitation Ceremony
Full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month

Boun Ork Phansa, “leaving the rains,” is Laos's observance of the end of the rains retreat. Its monastic core is Pavāraṇā, the Invitation Ceremony: after three months of living together, monastics invite one another to point out faults that have been seen, heard, or suspected. It falls on the full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month, three months after Boun Khao Phansa.

1. The retreat-ending full moon

The full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month completes the rains retreat. Laos, Thailand, and Cambodia use this Buddhist-month notation, while Vietnam uses a different lunar month name for the same full moon. The following day opens Laos's kaṭhina robe-offering season.

2. Boun Ork Phansa in Laos

Three layers for understanding the day

  • Foundational meaning: Pavāraṇā, the monastic practice of inviting mutual correction.
  • Shared practice: laypeople visit temples, make offerings, hear the Dhamma, and support monastics who have completed the retreat.
  • Custom: the observance opens Laos's late-rains festival season. Boun Lai Heua Fai, a river boat-lantern festival, is held soon afterward in many places; Boun Khao Salak and the That Luang festival also fall in this period.

Pavāraṇā itself is a monastic procedure. Boat lanterns and related festivals are cultural celebrations associated with the season, not part of the Vinaya rite.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Temple services are generally held in the morning; boat-lantern events are usually evening occasions.
  • These are two different rhythms—monastic observance and public festival—not one ritual.
  • Participate with quiet generosity and follow local guidance.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Boun Ork Phansa ends Laos's rains retreat on the eleventh-month full moon.
  • Its core is the monastic Pavāraṇā Invitation Ceremony.
  • It opens a late-rains festival season that includes boat lanterns and the kaṭhina period.

Glossary:

  • Boun Ork Phansa (Lao): leaving the rains.
  • Pavāraṇā (Pali): the Invitation Ceremony that ends the rains retreat.
  • Boun Lai Heua Fai (Lao): a river boat-lantern festival.
  • Completed the rains retreat: having resided for the full retreat at a monastery.

Source notes

  • The Vinaya basis of Pavāraṇā is found in the Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga.
  • Lao boat-lantern and other late-rains festivals are customary cultural observances.
  • The lunar date varies in the Gregorian calendar.
