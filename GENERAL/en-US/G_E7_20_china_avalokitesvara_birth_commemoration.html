G.E7.20. China: Avalokiteśvara Birth Commemoration (Guānyīn Dàn)
Nineteenth day of the second lunar month

China's Avalokiteśvara birth commemoration (Chinese: Guānyīn Dàn, Avalokiteśvara's Birthday) honours the birth of Avalokiteśvara, the Bodhisattva of Compassion, on the nineteenth day of the second lunar month. Vietnam keeps the same date (core article G.E7.9). The three Avalokiteśvara commemorations in the year — birth on the nineteenth day of the second month, awakening on the nineteenth day of the sixth, and renunciation on the nineteenth day of the ninth — are a shared feature of Chinese Buddhism, spreading to Vietnam and other countries shaped by Chinese culture.

1. The core and the observance in China

The meaning of the day — the birth of the bodhisattva of compassion — is presented fully in the core article G.E7. In China, monasteries observe it with chanting, prostrations, and offerings; devotees come to temples to make aspirations and offer incense, especially around Mount Putuo (Chinese: Pǔtuó Shān — the sacred island in Zhejiang Province, Avalokiteśvara's principal seat in Chinese tradition). In liturgies, the bodhisattva is invoked as Great Compassion Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva.

2. The three Avalokiteśvara commemorations — an overview

  • Nineteenth day of the second lunar month — birth: commemorating the bodhisattva's birth (this article).
  • Nineteenth day of the sixth lunar month — awakening: commemorating the bodhisattva's awakening (article G.E7.21).
  • Nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month — renunciation: commemorating the bodhisattva's renunciation (article G.E7.22).

In Chinese folk narrative, the three dates are linked to three turning points in the life of Princess Miaoshan, regarded in tradition as a manifestation of Avalokiteśvara — a folk-traditional layer, distinct from the foundational teaching. The key point: a bodhisattva is neither born nor passes away in the ordinary human sense; the commemoration is an occasion to recollect and turn toward the bodhisattva's qualities rather than to mark a historical event. This point is analysed in the core article G.E7.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Name: Avalokiteśvara, known in Vietnam as Quán Thế Âm — the One Who Perceives the Sounds of the World; Vietnamese devotees often speak of the bodhisattva familiarly as the compassionate Mother (article G.E7.9).
  • For the full core article, see G.E7.9; for the Pure Land context of Avalokiteśvara devotion in China, see G.E7.
  • On the "core article" approach, read G.E7.1 if you have not yet done so.

4. Summary and next steps

  • China's Avalokiteśvara birth commemoration falls on the nineteenth day of the second lunar month — the same date as Vietnam (G.E7.9); its Chinese name is Guānyīn Dàn.
  • This day is the first of the three commemorations on the nineteenth day of the second, sixth, and ninth lunar months; the next is the awakening commemoration.

Next: the following article — G.E7.21 — introduces China's Avalokiteśvara Awakening Commemoration (Guānyīn Chéngdào), on the nineteenth day of the sixth lunar month.

Glossary:

  • Guānyīn Dàn (Chinese, Avalokiteśvara's Birthday): the Chinese name for the Avalokiteśvara birth commemoration.
  • Avalokiteśvara (Sanskrit: Avalokiteśvara): the Bodhisattva of Compassion; see the core article G.E7.
  • Bodhisattva (Sanskrit: bodhisattva, enlightenment being): one who vows to reach awakening in order to liberate beings; see article G.C4.
  • Mount Putuo (Chinese: Pǔtuó Shān): the sacred island in Zhejiang, China — Avalokiteśvara's seat in Chinese tradition.
  • Princess Miaoshan: a figure in Chinese folk narrative regarded as a manifestation of Avalokiteśvara — a folk-traditional layer.
  • Great loving-kindness and great compassion: the defining qualities of Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva.
