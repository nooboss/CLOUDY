G.E7.28. Korea: Buddha's Renunciation Day (Chulga-jaeil)
Eighth day of the second lunar month

Korea's Buddha's Renunciation Day (Korean: Chulga-jaeil, Buddha's Renunciation Day) commemorates the day Prince Siddhārtha left the palace to seek the path of liberation, on the eighth day of the second lunar month. This date appears in the Korean calendar list but not in the Vietnamese or Chinese groups — a distinctive Korean feature within East Asia. The core teaching on the renunciation is presented in G.E7.5 (the Vietnamese calendar).

1. The observance in Korea

On the eighth day of the second lunar month, Korean temples hold sutra chanting, prostrations, and Dharma talks; Zen (Seon) monasteries hold a retreat-opening ceremony (Korean: yongmae) — in the Jogye tradition, the year has two retreat periods (Pali: vassa, rains retreat): summer and winter (Korean: ha-angeo and dong-angeo), intensive periods of cultivation (see the retreat in the article group G.D5).

2. Three layers for understanding Korea's Renunciation Day

  • Foundational meaning: the renunciation — giving up royal power to seek the answer to suffering — the core is in G.E7.5 and G.B2.
  • Shared practice: sutra chanting, prostrations, talks on letting go.
  • Custom: this date is distinctive to the Korean Buddhist calendar — not widespread in the other East Asian countries in this article group.

3. What newcomers should know

  • "Going forth" (leaving household life for cultivation) is the choice of some; for laypeople, this day recalls the spirit of letting go in ordinary life (article G.E7.22 discusses this spirit).
  • For the core article on the renunciation, see G.E7.5; for the narrative, see G.B2.
  • For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Korea's Renunciation Day falls on the eighth day of the second lunar month; its Korean name is Chulga-jaeil.
  • It is unique to the Korean Buddhist calendar — not found in the Vietnamese or Chinese groups.

Next: the following article — G.E7.29 — introduces Korea's Buddha's Nirvana Day (Yeolban-jaeil), on the fifteenth day of the second lunar month.

Glossary:

  • Chulga-jaeil (Korean, Buddha's Renunciation Day): the Korean name for the Buddha's renunciation day.
  • Going forth: choosing a life of cultivation free from household entanglements — see article G.E7.
  • Retreat-opening ceremony (Korean: yongmae): the opening rite of the Jogye Order's cultivation season.
  • Rains retreat (Pali: vassa): the intensive cultivation season of monks and nuns — see the article group G.D5. Korea keeps two periods: summer (ha-angeo) and winter (dong-angeo).
