G.E7.25. China: Medicine Buddha Commemoration (Yàoshī Fó Shèngdàn)
Thirtieth day of the ninth lunar month

China's Medicine Buddha commemoration (Chinese: Yàoshī Fó Shèngdàn, Medicine Buddha's Birthday) commemorates the birth of Bhaiṣajyaguru, the Medicine Buddha, on the thirtieth day of the ninth lunar month. This date appears in the Chinese calendar list but has no separate article in the Vietnamese calendar group — because devotion to Bhaiṣajyaguru is less widespread in Vietnam; this article is therefore the first core treatment of the Medicine Buddha in the G.E7 group.

1. Who is the Medicine Buddha

The Medicine Buddha is the buddha of medicine and healing in Mahāyāna Buddhism. According to the Medicine Buddha Sutra (Sanskrit: Bhaiṣajyaguru Sūtra, Chinese: Yàoshī Jīng), when still a bodhisattva, he made twelve great vows — the best known being the vow to help beings heal illness of body and mind, lack nothing, and reach awakening. His image often holds a medicine bowl in the hand — a symbol of healing both body and mind. He is attended by two bodhisattvas, Suryavairocana and Candravairocana, embodiments of sunlight and moonlight shining everywhere.

2. The observance in China

On the thirtieth day of the ninth lunar month, Chinese Buddhist monasteries — especially those with a Medicine Buddha hall — hold chanting of the Medicine Buddha Sutra, lamp offerings (the offering of light, a symbol of wisdom dispelling darkness) and aspirations for health and healing for all beings. Devotees often recite the Medicine Buddha mantra — a short invocation in the Mahāyāna manner.

Three layers for understanding the Medicine Buddha commemoration

  • Foundational meaning: bodily illness is suffering — healing belongs to the path; mental illness is greed, hatred, and delusion, healed by the teaching (article G.C4).
  • Shared practice: chanting the Medicine Buddha Sutra, reciting the mantra, offering lamps, aspiring for healing.
  • Custom: merit lamp offerings and peace-and-blessing rites attached to Chinese culture.

3. What newcomers should know

  • The Medicine Buddha does not replace medicine or physicians: he is a symbol of healing body and mind; aspiration and medical treatment do not conflict (article G.E6).
  • The name "Medicine Master" is the Chinese translation of the Sanskrit name Bhaiṣajyaguru.
  • For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6; for the bodhisattva image and vows, see G.C4.

4. Summary and next steps

  • China's Medicine Buddha commemoration falls on the thirtieth day of the ninth lunar month; its Chinese name is Yàoshī Fó Shèngdàn.
  • Its theme is healing of body and mind; the bodhisattva's twelve great vows; its principal sutra is the Medicine Buddha Sutra.

Glossary:

  • Yàoshī Fó Shèngdàn (Chinese, Medicine Buddha's Birthday): the Chinese name for the Medicine Buddha commemoration.
  • Bhaiṣajyaguru (Sanskrit: Bhaiṣajyaguru, Medicine Buddha): the buddha of medicine and healing in Mahāyāna Buddhism.
  • Medicine Buddha Sutra (Sanskrit: Bhaiṣajyaguru Sūtra, Chinese: Yàoshī Jīng): the sutra telling of his twelve great vows — a Mahāyāna sutra.
  • Medicine Buddha mantra: a short invocation of the Medicine Buddha's name — in the Mahāyāna manner.
  • Offering of light: the lamp-offering rite — a symbol of wisdom dispelling darkness.
  • Suryavairocana and Candravairocana Bodhisattvas: the two bodhisattva attendants of the Medicine Buddha.
  • Greed, hatred, and delusion: the three poisons of the mind — see article G.C4.

Source notes

  • The Medicine Buddha Sutra and the twelve great vows are Mahāyāna sources; the content is summarised from the general account.
  • Lamp offerings and the Medicine Buddha mantra are general descriptions of Chinese Buddhist observance — practice and customary layers.
  • The thirtieth day of the ninth lunar month (China) is the standard festival-calendar notation used in this subsection; it is not converted into a fixed Gregorian date, which varies by year.
