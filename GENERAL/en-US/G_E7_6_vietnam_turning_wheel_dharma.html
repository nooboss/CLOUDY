G.E7.6. Vietnam: Turning the Wheel of Dharma
Fifteenth day of the sixth lunar month

This observance commemorates the Buddha's first discourse at the Deer Park, traditionally kept in Vietnam on the fifteenth day of the sixth lunar month. It is the same occasion introduced for Theravāda in G.D5.4: the Middle Way and Four Noble Truths were first taught to the group of five former companions.

1. What does the festival commemorate?

Turning the Wheel of Dharma (Sanskrit: Dharmacakrapravartana) is the traditional name for the Buddha's first teaching after awakening. It presented the avoidance of two extremes, the Middle Way, and the Four Noble Truths. Kondañña is traditionally remembered as the first hearer to realise the teaching, beginning the Saṅgha.

2. Why this date?

Vietnamese Mahāyāna calendars mark the observance on the sixth-lunar-month full moon. Other Theravāda and East Asian calendars label the same traditional full-moon period differently; check the country and year for a Gregorian date.

3. The wheel begins to turn

The wheel is an image for the Dharma becoming active in the world: a teaching is not complete merely because it is heard, but when it is understood and practised. The day directs attention to the Four Noble Truths (G.C1) and Noble Eightfold Path (G.C2), rather than to ceremony alone.

4. What newcomers should know

  • Temple services may include chanting, a Dhamma talk, offerings, and precept observance.
  • Read the Four Noble Truths beforehand to participate with understanding.
  • Offerings are voluntary; quiet attention and a sincere wish to practise are enough.

5. Summary and next steps

  • The festival commemorates the first discourse and falls on the fifteenth day of the sixth lunar month in Vietnam.
  • Its core teaching is the Middle Way, Four Noble Truths, and the beginning of the Saṅgha.

Glossary:

  • Turning the Wheel of Dharma: the Buddha's first discourse.
  • Deer Park: the site near Vārāṇasī traditionally associated with the first discourse.
  • Middle Way: the path avoiding indulgence and self-mortification.

Source notes

  • The first discourse is preserved in SN 56.11; life-story details are presented as tradition.
  • The Vietnamese date is a devotional lunar-calendar convention.
