G.E7.15. Vietnam: Vu Lan — Ullambana Festival
Fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month

Vu Lan, or Ullambana, is Vietnam's major Buddhist observance of gratitude to parents and dedication of merit to ancestors and the departed. It falls on the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month.

1. What does the festival commemorate?

The Ullambana story concerns Maudgalyāyana, who sought to help his deceased mother. The Buddha directs him to make offerings to the Saṅgha at the end of the rains retreat and dedicate the resulting merit. The point is not that one person can control another's fate; it is gratitude, generosity, community support, and wholesome practice.

2. Why this date?

The festival is connected with the end-of-rains offering to the Saṅgha and is observed in Vietnam on the seventh-lunar-month full moon. It intersects with wider East Asian ancestor customs, but its Buddhist meaning should not be reduced to a ghost festival.

The central practices

Services include offerings to the monastic community, chanting, dedication of merit, remembrance of parents and ancestors, and sometimes lantern ceremonies. These are supports for gratitude and ethical life, not transactions for supernatural outcomes.

Vu Lan in Vietnam today

Vietnamese Vu Lan often includes the rose ceremony: a red rose for those whose mother is living, a white rose for those whose mother has died. This moving custom is modern cultural expression, not a required rite. Its most direct practice is care for parents and elders while they are alive.

What newcomers should know

  • Attend quietly and join the chanting or reflection as comfortable.
  • Offerings are voluntary; calling or caring for a parent is also appropriate practice.
  • Do not let memorial practice replace ethical responsibility toward living family members.

6. Summary and next steps

  • Vu Lan is held on the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month.
  • It joins filial gratitude, generosity to the Saṅgha, and dedication of merit to the departed.
  • Lanterns and roses are customary expressions around this core.

Glossary:

  • Ullambana / Vu Lan: East Asian festival of filial gratitude and merit dedication.
  • Maudgalyāyana: a chief disciple traditionally associated with the Vu Lan story.
  • Rose ceremony: Vietnamese custom expressing gratitude to mothers.

Source notes

  • The Ullambana story is preserved in Mahāyāna tradition; Vietnamese service forms and the rose ceremony are customary.
