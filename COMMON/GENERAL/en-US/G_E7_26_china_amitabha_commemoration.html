G.E7.26. China: Amitābha Buddha Commemoration (Āmítuó Fó Shèngdàn)
Seventeenth day of the eleventh lunar month

China's Amitābha Buddha commemoration (Chinese: Āmítuó Fó Shèngdàn, Amitābha's Birthday) commemorates the birth of Amitābha, the Buddha of Infinite Light, on the seventeenth day of the eleventh lunar month. It is the last date in the Chinese calendar group (G.E7.17 → G.E7.26). The core teaching on Amitābha in the Vietnamese calendar is presented in G.E7.7; this article emphasises the Chinese Pure Land perspective — where Buddha-recitation practice developed most strongly.

1. The observance in China — a Pure Land perspective

Amitābha Buddha is the centre of the Pure Land school (explained in article G.E7.19) — the most widespread school in China, Vietnam, and East Asia. Its central practice is Buddha-recitation (repeating the name with a concentrated mind): repeating "Namo Amitābha Buddha" (Homage to Amitābha Buddha) with faith, vow, and practice (trust in the buddha, aspiration for rebirth in his land, and living by the teaching). On the seventeenth day of the eleventh lunar month, Pure Land monasteries hold a seven-day Amitābha recitation retreat or communal Buddha-recitation sessions.

2. Three layers for understanding the Amitābha commemoration

  • Foundational meaning: according to the Amitabha Sutra (Sanskrit: Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, Chinese: Āmítuó Jīng), Amitābha is the buddha of Sukhāvatī (explained in article G.E7.7) — an ideal setting for cultivation; reciting his name is a gateway of practice — not a miracle replacing personal effort (article G.C4).
  • Shared practice: Buddha-recitation, chanting the Amitabha Sutra, vowing rebirth in Sukhāvatī.
  • Custom: seven-day recitation retreats, merit inscription, and rites attached to Chinese monastic culture.

3. What newcomers should know

  • "Buddha-recitation" is not petitioning for favours: it is a method of concentrating the mind — recollecting the compassion and wisdom that Amitābha represents (article G.C4).
  • "Namo" (from Sanskrit namas, homage, refuge) is the reverent greeting before a buddha's or bodhisattva's name.
  • For the core teaching on Amitābha, see G.E7.7; for the Pure Land school, see G.E7.

4. Summary — closing the Chinese calendar group

  • China's Amitābha commemoration falls on the seventeenth day of the eleventh lunar month; its Chinese name is Āmítuó Fó Shèngdàn.
  • The Chinese calendar group (G.E7.17 → G.E7.26) comprises ten dates: the Buddha's Birthday, Awakening, Nirvana, the three Avalokiteśvara commemorations, Ullambana, the Kṣitigarbha, Medicine Buddha, and Amitābha commemorations — all in the Chinese lunar calendar.

Glossary:

  • Āmítuó Fó Shèngdàn (Chinese, Amitābha's Birthday): the Chinese name for the Amitābha Buddha commemoration.
  • Amitābha (Sanskrit: Amitābha, Buddha of Infinite Light): the buddha of Sukhāvatī — the centre of the Pure Land school (article G.E7.7).
  • Buddha-recitation: the practice of repeating a buddha's name with a concentrated mind — the method of the Pure Land school.
  • Namo (from Sanskrit namas, homage, refuge): the reverent greeting before a buddha's or bodhisattva's name.
  • Namo Amitābha Buddha (Homage to Amitābha Buddha): the invocation recited in the Pure Land school.
  • Seven-day Amitābha recitation retreat: a seven-day period of dedicated Buddha-recitation at a monastery.
  • Faith, vow, practice: the three conditions of Pure Land practice in this school's account.
  • Amitabha Sutra (Sanskrit: Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra, Chinese: Āmítuó Jīng): the short sutra describing Sukhāvatī — a Mahāyāna sutra.

Source notes
