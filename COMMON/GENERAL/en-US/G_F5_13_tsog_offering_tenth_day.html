G.F5.13. (Phugpa) Tsog Offering — Tenth Day (Gaṇacakra Pūjā)
Tenth day of each Tibetan month

The tenth day of each month in the Tibetan Buddhist calendar is the day of the tsog offering (Tibetan: tsog, from Sanskrit "gaṇacakra" — the circle of the assembly - English: tsok offering, the communal offering feast) — known in Sanskrit as Gaṇacakra Pūjā — Daśamī (Sanskrit: gaṇacakra pūjā, "daśamī" is the tenth day - English: the ganachakra offering on the tenth day). It is a communal offering — the practice community together presents offerings and recites prayers; the tenth day is especially connected with the Tantric masters — above all Padmasambhava (Tibetan: Guru Rinpoche — the "Precious Lama" - English: Padmasambhava) — the founder of the Nyingma school (article G.F2).

1. What is tsog

Tsog (or "tsok") is a community offering practice: the participants together present offerings (food — flowers — lamps), recite prayers according to a shared liturgy, and at the end share the offered substances — eating together as a sacred feast. The spirit: offering is not feeding gods, but a practice of letting go — sharing — community (article G.C4 on generosity in the path of practice). In monasteries, tsog is an occasion for monastics and lay followers to sit in one row together — dissolving the boundary between high and low.

2. Why the tenth day — and the twenty-fifth

  • The tenth day of each month is connected with Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche): according to the Nyingma account, the great events of his life fell on the tenth days of the months; thus the tenth is the day of offering to Guru Rinpoche — reciting the prayers to him.
  • The twenty-fifth day of each month (article G.F5.15) is the tsog day connected with the female awakened ones — Dakini (Sanskrit: ḍākinī - English: dakini, the female embodiments of the teachings in the Tantric tradition) and other lineages — forming a rhythm of two tsogs each month.
  • Besides the regular tsog, the great annual festivals (G.F5.2 → G.F5.11) also commonly include tsog.

3. Three layers when reading a tsog day

  • Root doctrinal layer: the practice of letting go — sharing — community; remembering the masters of the lineage (article G.F2 on teacher — disciple in Vajrayana).
  • Shared practice layer: presenting offerings, reciting the liturgy, sharing the sacred feast.
  • Customary layer: customs of preparing offering foods; regional rites differing among the schools (Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug — article G.F2).

4. Summary and next step

  • The tenth day of each month = tsog connected with Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche) — communal offering — sharing.
  • The spirit of tsog: letting go — sharing — connection; not "feeding gods".

Glossary:

  • Tsog / tsok (Tibetan, from Sanskrit "gaṇacakra" — the circle of the assembly - English: tsok offering): the communal offering feast — presenting offerings, reciting prayers, sharing.
  • Gaṇacakra Pūjā (Sanskrit: gaṇacakra pūjā - English: the ganachakra offering): the Sanskrit name of the tsog rite.
  • Daśamī (Sanskrit: daśamī - English: the tenth day): "the tenth day" — the name for the tenth day of each month.
  • Padmasambhava / Guru Rinpoche (Tibetan: Guru Rinpoche — the "Precious Lama" - English: Padmasambhava): the 8th-century Tantric master — founder of the Nyingma school (articles G.B6, G.F2).
  • Dakini (Sanskrit: ḍākinī - English: dakini): the female embodiments of the teachings in the Tantric tradition — the figure connected with the 25th day (G.F5.15).
  • Nyingma (Tibetan, meaning "old" — the old school - English: the Nyingma school): the oldest Tibetan Buddhist school — founded through Padmasambhava (article G.F2).
