G.D5.30. Cambodia: Chol Vossa — Beginning the Rains Retreat
First day of the ninth Buddhist month

Chol Vossa (Khmer: “entering the rains”) marks the beginning of the rains retreat (Pali: vassa), the three-month period when monastics remain in one place for focused study and practice. The retreat's core meaning is explained in G.D5.5, in the Vietnamese calendar group. In Cambodia, it begins on the first day of the ninth Buddhist month, immediately after Asalha Puja (G.D5.29), and ends on the full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month (G.D5.31).

1. What is the first day of the ninth Buddhist month?

The rains retreat begins on the day after the Āsāḷha full moon. Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia record this as the first day of the ninth Buddhist month; Vietnam records the same marker as the sixteenth day of the sixth lunar month (G.D5.5). It is the same occasion expressed through different calendar notations (G.D5.1).

2. Chol Vossa in Cambodia

Three layers for understanding Chol Vossa in Cambodia

  • Foundational meaning: the Vinaya provision for a three-month rains retreat (G.D5.5); this core is the same throughout Theravāda countries.
  • Shared practice: temples hold retreat-opening ceremonies; laypeople offer requisites and candles for the season, while many people make a commitment to practise more diligently throughout the three months.
  • Custom: the rains retreat is a major Cambodian festival season. After Chol Vossa, the sequence of village-temple observances includes Bon Phchum Ben, a festival for making offerings and dedicating merit to ancestors held in the latter half of the retreat, and other full-moon festivals. The season is also the high point for short-term ordination (G.D5.29). Bon Phchum Ben belongs to the customary festival life of Khmer communities and is introduced again in the article on the end of the retreat (G.D5.31).

For the Khmer community in southern Vietnam (G.D4), Chol Vossa likewise opens the year's major festival sequence on the same calendar. Its customary features are discussed in G.D5.5 and G.D4.

3. What newcomers should know

  • The rains retreat is a good time to begin a small, sustainable commitment: hear the Dhamma regularly or observe the precepts more carefully (G.D5.5).
  • Offerings of practical requisites to the village temple during the rains retreat are voluntary.
  • For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Chol Vossa marks the beginning of Cambodia's rains retreat on the first day of the ninth Buddhist month; its core is explained in G.D5.
  • Its Khmer features include the year's major sequence of festivals, notably Bon Phchum Ben, which is associated with dedicating merit to ancestors, and the high point of short-term ordination.
  • The season continues until the full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month, the Chenh Vossa observance.

Next: G.D5.31 introduces Chenh Vossa, the Invitation Ceremony that ends Cambodia's rains retreat on the full moon of the eleventh Buddhist month. See G.D5.6 for the ceremony's core meaning.

Glossary:

  • Chol Vossa (Khmer): “entering the rains,” the Khmer name for the beginning of the rains retreat; Thailand calls it Khao Phansa (G.D5.12), while Laos calls it Boun Khao Phansa (G.D5.20).
  • Rains retreat (Pali: vassa): the three-month period when monastics remain in one place for focused practice; its core is explained in G.D5.
  • Bon Phchum Ben (Khmer): a major Khmer festival for making offerings and dedicating merit to ancestors, held during the rains retreat; a distinctive customary and cultural observance.
  • Wat (Khmer-Thai): a monastery, the religious and communal centre (G.D5.27).
  • Short-term ordination: the customary practice of ordaining for a defined period (G.D5.11, G.D5.29).
  • Buddhist Era (BE): a calendar era traditionally reckoned from the Buddha's final passing.

Source notes

  • The rains retreat's core is presented fully in G.D5.5, with its corresponding sources (Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga - Theravāda Buddhism).
  • The name Chol Vossa, the rains-retreat festival sequence including Bon Phchum Ben, and short-term ordination are described in accounts of Cambodian Buddhism; the customary and cultural layers are distinguished from the foundational teaching.
  • The first day of the ninth Buddhist month is the standard festival-date notation used in this subsection; it is not converted into a fixed Gregorian date, which varies by year.
