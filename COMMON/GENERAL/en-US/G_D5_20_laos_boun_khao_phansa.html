G.D5.20. Laos: Boun Khao Phansa — Beginning the Rains Retreat
First day of the ninth Buddhist month

Boun Khao Phansa is the Lao observance that begins the rains retreat (Pali: vassa): three months in which monastics remain in one residence for intensive practice. It begins on the day after Asalha Puja, the first day of the ninth Buddhist month, and concludes at the eleventh-month full moon.

1. The beginning of the retreat

The retreat begins immediately after the Āsāḷha full moon. Laos, Thailand, and Cambodia record the date as the first day of the ninth Buddhist month; Vietnam gives the corresponding date as the sixteenth day of its sixth lunar month. They designate the same start of the three-month retreat.

2. Boun Khao Phansa in Laos

Three layers for understanding the day

  • Foundational meaning: the Vinaya period of rains residence and intensive monastic training.
  • Shared practice: entry-into-retreat services, offerings of practical requisites, and stronger lay commitments to ethical conduct or meditation.
  • Custom: rains-retreat candles are offered to monasteries. Lao folk custom also associates gifts of candles between young couples with wishes for clarity and a steady relationship. This is a cultural expression, not a doctrinal rule.

The image of the candle as guiding light can encourage wholesome intentions, but the retreat's core remains the three-month training of the Saṅgha.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Choose a small, sustainable three-month commitment, such as regular Dhamma study or daily meditation.
  • Offerings of candles or requisites are voluntary.
  • Attend quietly and follow the monastery's arrangements.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Boun Khao Phansa begins Laos's rains retreat on the first day of the ninth Buddhist month.
  • Lao customs include retreat candles and seasonal temporary ordination.
  • The retreat ends at Boun Ork Phansa, on the eleventh-month full moon.

Glossary:

  • Boun Khao Phansa (Lao): merit-making observance for entering the rains.
  • Rains retreat (Pali: vassa): three months of residence and intensive practice for monastics.
  • Vassa candles: candles offered during the rains-retreat season as a customary practice.

Source notes

  • The Vinaya basis of the rains retreat is found in the Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga.
  • Lao candle customs are local cultural practices, distinct from the doctrinal core.
  • The lunar date varies in the Gregorian calendar.
