G.E7.14. Vietnam: Kṣitigarbha Bodhisattva Commemoration
Thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month

This observance honours Kṣitigarbha, known in Vietnam as Địa Tạng, on the thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month. He represents vows to care for beings in the most difficult conditions, gratitude to parents, and dedication of merit to the departed.

1. Who is Kṣitigarbha?

Kṣitigarbha is a bodhisattva associated with the aspiration not to abandon beings in painful realms. His imagery should not be used to cultivate fear of punishment. It directs attention toward compassion, moral responsibility, and helping those who suffer.

2. Why this date?

The thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month is a Vietnamese devotional convention. If the month has only twenty-nine days, local temples make their own calendar arrangements.

3. How is the day observed?

Temples may recite the Kṣitigarbha Sutra, make offerings, dedicate merit to ancestors and the departed, and encourage filial care. These actions belong to the broader seventh-month rhythm with Vu Lan (G.E7.15).

4. What newcomers should know

  • Do not confuse care for the departed with fear-based superstition.
  • Offerings are voluntary; serving parents, elders, and vulnerable people is fitting practice.
  • Dedication of merit is meaningful when joined with wholesome living.

5. Summary and next steps

  • Kṣitigarbha is honoured on the thirtieth day of the seventh lunar month.
  • The day centres vows, compassion for suffering beings, filial gratitude, and merit dedication.

Glossary:

  • Kṣitigarbha: bodhisattva associated with vows for beings in difficult realms.
  • Dedication of merit: dedicating wholesome results to others, including the departed.

Source notes

  • Kṣitigarbha practices draw on Mahāyāna scripture and East Asian custom; the Vietnamese date is devotional convention.
