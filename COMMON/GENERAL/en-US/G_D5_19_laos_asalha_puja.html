G.D5.19. Laos: Asalha Puja — Turning the Wheel of Dhamma
Full moon of the eighth Buddhist month (Āsāḷha)

Asalha Puja is the Lao name for Āsāḷha Pūjā, the remembrance of the Buddha's first discourse at the Deer Park. It recalls the middle way and the Four Noble Truths, first taught to his five former companions. In Laos it falls on the full moon of the eighth Buddhist month; the following day begins the rains retreat.

1. The Āsāḷha full moon

The first discourse and the first rains retreat of the Saṅgha are consecutive events in the traditional account. Laos, Thailand, and Cambodia call this the eighth Buddhist month, while Vietnam applies a different lunar-month name to the same full moon.

2. Asalha Puja in Laos

Three layers for understanding the day

  • Foundational meaning: the first discourse, the middle way, and the Four Noble Truths.
  • Shared practice: temple visits, offerings, keeping precepts, Dhamma listening, and evening wian tian.
  • Custom: the season may open a period of temporary ordination for young Lao men. This is a valued custom, not a requirement of the path.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Attending both Asalha Puja and the following day's entry into the rains retreat makes the year's rhythm especially clear: a festival of teaching followed by a season of practice.
  • Listening to the Dhamma is central; offerings are voluntary.
  • Read the Four Noble Truths alongside this observance.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Asalha Puja is Laos's observance of the first discourse, on the eighth-month full moon.
  • It combines shared Theravāda practice with Lao wian tian and temporary-ordination customs.
  • The following day is Boun Khao Phansa, the beginning of the rains retreat.

Glossary:

  • Asalha Puja (Lao, from Pali Āsāḷha pūjā): homage in the month of Āsāḷha.
  • Turning the Wheel of Dhamma (Pali: Dhammacakkappavattana): the traditional expression for the Buddha's first teaching.
  • Temporary ordination: ordination undertaken for a limited period.
  • Wian tian: triple circumambulation.

Source notes

  • The first discourse is preserved in the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta, Saṃyutta Nikāya 56.11.
  • Lao naming, circumambulation, and temporary ordination are customary features.
  • The lunar date varies in the Gregorian calendar.
