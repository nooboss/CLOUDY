G.H1. Index of Commonly Encountered Scriptures — From the Nikāyas to the Tantras

The previous article — G.G2 — closed the comparison and orientation group; from here the G.H group is a reference set — articles not meant to be read in sequence, but consulted as needed. This article is an index of commonly encountered scriptures across the three traditions: each entry gives a one-line "what it is," a suggested Vietnamese translation, and a difficulty level; before the index comes a section on how to read citation codes — once you know what "MN 10" means, you can look up any scripture cited in the other articles yourself. This index is only a map; the full maps of the three canonical collections are in G.D2 (Pali), G.E3 (Chinese), and G.F3 (Tibetan).

1. How to use this index

Three notes before opening the tables. One — difficulty key: ★☆☆ readable right away for a beginner; ★★☆ requires familiarity with a few basic concepts (group G.C); ★★★ is a difficult text, best read with teachings or a commentary. Two — the suggested Vietnamese translations are suggested starting points, not an exhaustive list: many scriptures have multiple translations, and any translation should be read together with teachings by a reputable teacher. Three — this index selects scriptures commonly encountered in temple life and Vietnamese books, not the complete collections; for the full maps of the collections, return to G.D2, G.E3, G.F3.

2. What "MN 10" means — reading citation codes

The articles in the app cite scriptures by international codes. The general structure is: abbreviation of the collection + sutta number; some collections are divided into chapters and so carry two numbers (chapter.sutta):

2.1. DN

  • Collection: Dīgha Nikāya — Long Discourses
  • Example: DN 2
  • Meaning: The second sutta of the Dīgha Nikāya — the Samaññaphala Sutta (Fruits of the Contemplative Life)

2.2. MN

  • Collection: Majjhima Nikāya — Middle-Length Discourses
  • Example: MN 10
  • Meaning: The tenth sutta of the Majjhima Nikāya — the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (Foundations of Mindfulness)

2.3. SN

  • Collection: Saṃyutta Nikāya — Connected Discourses
  • Example: SN 56.11
  • Meaning: Saṃyutta 56, sutta 11 — the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (Setting the Wheel of Dhamma in Motion)

2.4. AN

  • Collection: Aṅguttara Nikāya — Numerical Discourses
  • Example: AN 3.65
  • Meaning: Book of Threes, sutta 65 — the Kesamutti Sutta (Kālāma Sutta)

2.5. Dhp

  • Collection: Dhammapada — (part of the Khuddaka Nikāya)
  • Example: Dhp 1
  • Meaning: Verse 1 — "mind precedes all phenomena"

2.6. Vism

  • Collection: Visuddhimagga — Path of Purification (commentarial literature, not the direct word of the Buddha)
  • Example: Vism XIV
  • Meaning: Chapter 14 of the Visuddhimagga

2.7. T + number

  • Collection: Chinese texts by Taishō (Great Treasury) serial number
  • Example: T 262
  • Meaning: No. 262 in the Taishō Canon — the Saddharmapuṇḍarīka Sūtra (Lotus Sūtra), Kumārajīva translation

2.8. DA / MA / SA / EA

  • Collection: Dīrgha / Madhyama / Saṃyukta / Ekottarika Āgamas — the Chinese parallels of the four Nikāyas
  • Example: MA 26
  • Meaning: Sūtra no. 26 of the Madhyama Āgama

Two small tips. First, the word "Nikāya" (Pali: nikāya - English: collection) means a "collection (of discourses)" — the five Nikāyas are the five "great books" of the Pali Sutta Piṭaka; the Āgamas (Sanskrit: āgama - English: agamas) are Chinese translations from the same source stratum, cross-searchable on Suttacentral. Second, when you meet a "T" number — that is the serial number in the standard Chinese Canon compiled by Japanese scholars in the 20th century (Taishō Shinshū Daizōkyō - English: Taishō Tripiṭaka) — searchable free of charge on CBETA; the entire Pali Canon is searchable on Suttacentral.

3. Theravāda — commonly encountered Pali scriptures

Shared background: the Pali Tipiṭaka (Pali: tipiṭaka - English: Three Baskets) — Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma — with the structure of each basket presented in G.D2. The table below selects the most commonly encountered parts of the Sutta Piṭaka:

3.1. Dhammapada

  • What it is: 423 concise verses on the mind, kamma, and the path — the most widely read scripture in the world
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: Ven. Thích Minh Châu (in the Khuddaka Nikāya)
  • Difficulty: ★☆☆

3.2. Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (SN 56.11)

  • What it is: The first discourse — the Four Noble Truths
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: translations in Vietnamese sutta anthologies (cited in group G.C1)
  • Difficulty: ★☆☆

3.3. Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (MN 10)

  • What it is: The foundational teaching on mindfulness — the four foundations of mindfulness
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: Ven. Thích Minh Châu (Majjhima Nikāya); edition with commentary by Zen Master Thích Nhất Hạnh (Four Establishments of Mindfulness)
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

3.4. Majjhima Nikāya (MN) — 152 suttas

  • What it is: The clearest methodical discourses, many in dialogue form
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: Ven. Thích Minh Châu (Majjhima Nikāya)
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

3.5. Aṅguttara Nikāya (AN) — by numerical sets

  • What it is: "Clustered" discourses — six things, seven things... — close to lay life
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: Ven. Thích Minh Châu (Aṅguttara Nikāya)
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

3.6. Dīgha Nikāya (DN) — 34 long suttas

  • What it is: The big picture: ethics, cosmology, the contemplative sects of the Buddha's time
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: Ven. Thích Minh Châu (Dīgha Nikāya)
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

3.7. Saṃyutta Nikāya (SN) — by theme

  • What it is: Discourses arranged by theme: Four Noble Truths, dependent origination, samādhi – wisdom
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: Ven. Thích Minh Châu (Saṃyutta Nikāya)
  • Difficulty: ★★★

3.8. Jātaka (Pali: Jātaka - English: birth stories)

  • What it is: Over 500 stories of the Buddha's previous lives — a source of the bodhisattva ideal (article G.E2)
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: Vietnamese selections of Jātaka stories (translated and retold editions)
  • Difficulty: ★☆☆

3.9. Visuddhimagga (Path of Purification) (Pali - English: Path of Purification)

  • What it is: Buddhaghosa's synthesis of the path of virtue – concentration – wisdom — commentarial level, not the direct word of the Buddha (article G.D3)
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: No complete Vietnamese translation yet; consult books presenting Theravāda meditation
  • Difficulty: ★★★

3.10. Vinaya Piṭaka

  • What it is: Regulations for monastic life — mainly for the ordained Saṅgha (article G.B4)
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation: Consult books presenting the Vinaya; not a starting book
  • Difficulty: ★★★

The recommendation of G.D2 still stands: beginners should start with the Majjhima and Aṅguttara Nikāyas — and for a slim "starter" volume, the Dhammapada is the most popular choice.

4. Mahāyāna — representative Chinese translations

Shared background: the Chinese Canon — the East Asian collection, organized into great sūtra "families" (article G.E3): Prajñāpāramitā, Lotus, Avataṃsaka, Ratnakūṭa, Mahāsaṃnipāta, Nirvāṇa. The "T" numbers below locate the Chinese source text:

4.1. Heart Sūtra (Prajñāpāramitāhṛdaya) (Sanskrit: Prajñāpāramitā - English: Perfection of Wisdom, "pāramitā" — crossing to the other shore)

  • What it is: 260 characters — a condensation of emptiness; chanted daily in Vietnamese temples
  • T number: T 251
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: translations and explanations by Ven. Thích Trí Quang; with commentary by Zen Master Thích Nhất Hạnh
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

4.2. Diamond Sūtra (Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā)

  • What it is: The short Prajñāpāramitā text — "arouse the mind without dwelling anywhere"
  • T number: T 235
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: translations and explanations by Ven. Thích Trí Quang
  • Difficulty: ★★★

4.3. Lotus Sūtra (Saddharmapuṇḍarīka Sūtra)

  • What it is: "One Buddha Vehicle" — skillful means; foundation of Tiantai and Nichiren
  • T number: T 262
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: translations and explanations by Ven. Thích Trí Quang
  • Difficulty: ★★★

4.4. Avataṃsaka Sūtra (Flower Garland Sūtra) — 80 fascicles

  • What it is: A cosmology of "one in all, all in one" — foundation of the Huayan school and Chan
  • T number: T 279
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: Read through selected chapters (the Gaṇḍavyūha / Entry into the Dharma Realm chapter); partial Vietnamese translations
  • Difficulty: ★★★

4.5. Amitābha Sūtra (Smaller Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra)

  • What it is: The short Pure Land sūtra — the world of Sukhāvatī, buddha-recollection
  • T number: T 366
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: translations and teachings by Ven. Thích Thanh Từ
  • Difficulty: ★☆☆

4.6. Larger Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra (Sūtra of Infinite Life)

  • What it is: The long Pure Land sūtra — the 48 vows of Amitābha Buddha
  • T number: T 365
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: Vietnamese translations and teachings on the Pure Land
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

4.7. Vimalakīrti Sūtra (Vimalakīrtinirdeśa)

  • What it is: The layman Vimalakīrti "teaching" the Dharma through questions — a sūtra close to laypeople
  • T number: T 476 (Kumārajīva translation)
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: Translation and commentary by Ven. Thích Thanh Từ
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

4.8. Śūraṅgama Sūtra

  • What it is: A Mahāyāna sūtra on samādhi – wisdom, from the structure of mind to misleading states
  • T number: T 945
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: Translation and commentary by Ven. Thích Thanh Từ
  • Difficulty: ★★★

4.9. Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra (Descent into Laṅkā)

  • What it is: A source of "mind-only" thought — consciousness-only, buddha-nature
  • T number: T 670
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: Vietnamese translations with presentations in books on Yogācāra
  • Difficulty: ★★★

4.10. Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra

  • What it is: The final teaching — buddha-nature, permanence
  • T number: T 374
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: Read through presentations on buddha-nature (article G.E2)
  • Difficulty: ★★★

4.11. Sūtra of Perfect Enlightenment (Yuanjue jing)

  • What it is: A short Mahāyāna meditation sūtra — "intrinsic nature manifest in action"
  • T number: T 842
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: Read with teachings — a condensed text, hard to read alone
  • Difficulty: ★★★

4.12. Platform Sūtra of the Sixth Patriarch

  • What it is: Teachings of the Sixth Patriarch Huineng — the only teaching by a Chinese master honored as a "sūtra" (article G.E4)
  • T number: T 2008
  • Suggested Vietnamese translation / commentary: Translation and commentary by Ven. Thích Thanh Từ; modern annotated translations
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

The Āgamas — parallels of the four Nikāyas — also belong to the Chinese collection: those studying along comparative three-tradition lines may read the Chinese or English versions (Suttacentral offers cross-references); Vietnamese translations of the Āgamas remain incomplete, so for reading purposes the Pali editions in section 3 are the most convenient.

5. Vajrayāna — tantras and foundational texts

Shared background: the Tibetan Kangyur – Tengyur pair (article G.F3 — the Derge edition is the standard citation edition). Unlike the two tables above: most texts at the tantra level (Sanskrit: tantra - English: tantric scriptures) should not be read on one's own — the tradition requires empowerment and a teacher's guidance (article G.F2); the table below states this clearly:

5.1. Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life (Bodhicaryāvatāra) — Śāntideva

  • What it is: A versified systematization of bodhicitta and the six pāramitās — the most widespread foundational text of Tibetan Mahāyāna
  • Reading note: Complete English translations available; readable without empowerment
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

5.2. Lamp for the Path to Enlightenment (Bodhipathapradīpa) — Atiśa

  • What it is: The "root text" of the lamrim system — the path for three types of persons (article G.F1)
  • Reading note: Short but condensed — read with presentations on the lamrim
  • Difficulty: ★★☆

5.3. Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path (Lamrim Chenmo) — Tsongkhapa

  • What it is: The complete Gelug lamrim system — the "textbook" of the Tibetan path
  • Reading note: Multi-volume English translations available; abridged versions are an easier entry
  • Difficulty: ★★★

5.4. Mahāvairocana Tantra (Great Sun Tantra) (Tibetan and Chinese translations)

  • What it is: A foundational tantra of East Asian Esoteric Buddhism (article G.E4) — read for context
  • Reading note: Tantra level — approach through presentations, do not practice on one's own
  • Difficulty: ★★★

5.5. Guhyasamāja, Hevajra, Kālacakra Tantras

  • What it is: Three representative highest tantras in the Kangyur
  • Reading note: Require empowerment and guidance — presented in the app only as a map (article G.F3)
  • Difficulty: —

On Vietnamese translations: there is as yet no Vietnamese translation set of the Kangyur – Tengyur; Vietnamese materials on this tradition come mainly through explanatory books and teachings. The 84000 project (84000.co) translates the Kangyur – Tengyur into English free of charge — the best access source at present.

6. Where should you start?

Based on purpose, not a mandatory order (in the spirit of the app's roadmap — editorial principle 1):

  • Complete beginner, no tradition chosen yet: Dhammapada (Dhp) → a selection of short Majjhima / Aṅguttara suttas; read in parallel with group G.A of the app. Article G.A4 helps you choose a direction according to your living environment and character.
  • Going deeper into Theravāda: Majjhima (MN) → Aṅguttara (AN) → Dīgha (DN) → Saṃyutta (SN); with article G.D3 on the path of practice.
  • Going deeper into East Asian Mahāyāna: Heart Sūtra → Amitābha / Larger Sukhāvatīvyūha (for Pure Land) → Platform Sūtra (for Chan/Zen) → Lotus Sūtra; with article G.E4 on the schools.
  • Going deeper into Vajrayāna: Bodhicaryāvatāra → Lamp for the Path / abridged lamrim → only then tantras with a teacher; with article G.F2.
  • Reading for an overall picture: return to the comparison table in G.G1 and the three canonical maps in G.D2, G.E3, G.F3.

One principle throughout — the source-stratification principle (editorial principle 3): always know which level you are reading — a discourse spoken by the Buddha, a treatise by a commentator, or a modern presentation; and whenever an article cites a scripture, always check the cited original before reusing it.

7. Summary and next steps

  • A citation code = abbreviation of the collection + sutta number (DN, MN, SN, AN, Dhp; T + number for the Chinese collection) — once you can read the codes you can look up every scripture cited in the app yourself (Suttacentral, CBETA, 84000).
  • Three index tables: Pali (start: Dhammapada → Majjhima – Aṅguttara), Chinese (Heart Sūtra → Pure Land / Chan directions), Tibetan (Bodhicaryāvatāra → lamrim → tantras requiring guidance).
  • The ★ difficulty is a suggestion for choosing the right "vehicle" for your present stage — not a value ranking of the texts (editorial principle 1).

Glossary:

  • Pali Tipiṭaka (Pali: tipiṭaka - English: Three Baskets): the three "baskets" of the Theravāda canon — Vinaya, Sutta, Abhidhamma; texts in Pali, the intermediary language in which the canon was recorded in ancient Sri Lanka.
  • Nikāya (Pali: nikāya - English: collection): "collection (of discourses)" — the five great collections of the Pali Sutta Piṭaka: Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara, Khuddaka.
  • Āgama (Sanskrit: āgama - English: agamas): Chinese collections from the same source as the four Nikāyas — Dīrgha, Madhyama, Saṃyukta, Ekottarika Āgamas.
  • Dhammapada (Pali: dhammapada - English: sayings of the Dhamma): 423 verses — the most widely read Pali scripture.
  • Jātaka (Pali - English: birth stories): stories of the Buddha's previous lives — over 500 stories; a formative source of the Mahāyāna bodhisattva ideal.
  • Visuddhimagga — Path of Purification (Pali - English: Path of Purification): Buddhaghosa's great treatise (5th century) systematizing the path of virtue – concentration – wisdom; commentarial–treatise level, not the direct word of the Buddha.
  • Prajñāpāramitā (Sanskrit - English: Perfection of Wisdom): "pāramitā" — crossing to the other shore; the Mahāyāna Prajñāpāramitā sūtra family.
  • Taishō Tripiṭaka: the standard Chinese Canon compiled by Japanese scholars in the 20th century; "T" citations follow this edition.
  • CBETA (cbeta.org): free Chinese Canon database; Suttacentral (suttacentral.net): Pali texts cross-referenced with Āgamas; 84000 (84000.co): project translating the Kangyur – Tengyur into English.
  • Tantra (Sanskrit: tantra - English: tantric scriptures): the esoteric scripture stratum of Vajrayāna — four levels from Kriyā to Anuttarayoga; reading and practice traditionally require empowerment and a teacher's guidance.
  • Bodhicaryāvatāra — Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life: Śāntideva's great poem (8th century) on bodhicitta and the six pāramitās — a shared foundational text of Tibetan Mahāyāna.
  • Lamrim (Tibetan: lam rim - English: stages of the path): the Gelug path sequence, from Atiśa's "Lamp for the Path to Enlightenment" to Tsongkhapa's "Lamrim Chenmo."
  • Platform Sūtra (T 2008 — Liuzu Dashi Fabao Tanjing): records the teachings of the Sixth Patriarch Huineng; the only case of a Chinese master's teaching honored as a "sūtra."
  • Derge (Tibetan: sde dge): the high-quality 18th-century woodblock edition of the Kangyur – Tengyur — used as the citation standard for Tibetan scriptures.
