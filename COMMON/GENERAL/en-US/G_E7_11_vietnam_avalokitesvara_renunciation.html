G.E7.11. Vietnam: Avalokiteśvara Renunciation Commemoration
Nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month

This day honours Avalokiteśvara's renunciation, traditionally observed in Vietnam on the nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month. It completes the annual Vietnamese Avalokiteśvara cycle of birth, awakening, and renunciation.

1. How should Avalokiteśvara's renunciation be read?

The commemoration is devotional rather than independently documented biography. It presents renunciation as turning from self-centred habits toward the bodhisattva vow: hearing suffering, responding with compassion, and cultivating wisdom.

2. Completing the annual cycle

Vietnamese custom observes Avalokiteśvara's birth on the nineteenth of the second lunar month, awakening on the nineteenth of the sixth, and renunciation on the nineteenth of the ninth. These dates create a communal rhythm of recollection, not a historical chronology.

3. How is the day observed?

Temples may chant the Universal Gate chapter, recite the Great Compassion Dhāraṇī, keep vegetarian practice, offer food or flowers, and dedicate merit. The practical point is compassionate conduct, not seeking favour without changing one's life.

4. What newcomers should know

  • Follow the temple's rhythm quietly; chanting and offerings are voluntary.
  • Choose one habit of speech or action that can become more compassionate.
  • The observance does not require leaving lay life.

5. Summary and next steps

  • The day falls on the nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month.
  • It completes the Avalokiteśvara cycle and directs practice toward compassion in action.

Glossary:

  • Avalokiteśvara: the bodhisattva of compassion, Quán Thế Âm in Vietnam.
  • Renunciation: turning away from what causes harm toward the path of practice.

Source notes

  • The three dates are Vietnamese devotional-calendar conventions, not independently established biography.
  • Ritual forms vary by temple and locality.
