G.D5.6. Vietnam: End of the Rains Retreat (Pavāraṇā)
Fifteenth day of the ninth lunar month

Pavāraṇā marks the end of the three-month rains retreat that began on the sixteenth day of the sixth lunar month (G.D5.5). Pavāraṇā means “invitation”: at the closing full moon, monastics invite their fellow practitioners to point out faults that have been seen, heard, or suspected. It is therefore known in Vietnamese as the Invitation Ceremony. In this article's Vietnamese calendar notation, it falls on the fifteenth day of the ninth lunar month; the kaṭhina robe-offering season begins immediately afterwards (G.D5.7).

1. What does the ceremony mark?

Unlike Māgha Pūjā and Vesak, Pavāraṇā is a monastic-community observance. It marks three things: monastics who followed the retreat properly have completed the rains; they conduct the Invitation Ceremony as the retreat's shared review; and the travelling suspended during the rains may resume.

The closing full moon of the rains retreat

  • Completed rains: this is an eligibility condition for the kaṭhina offering.
  • Mutual invitation: monastics invite correction about faults during the retreat.
  • Movement resumes: monastics may again travel to teach as before.

Tradition also associates the first rains retreat after the Buddha's final passing with the gathering of 500 arahants at Rājagaha to preserve the teachings (G.B3). This account illustrates the value of practitioners living closely enough together to review and preserve the Dhamma.

2. Why this date?

Pavāraṇā is held on the last full moon of the rains retreat, three months after its beginning:

Leaving the rains — date notation in different calendars

  • Thailand: eleventh-month full moon — Ok Phansa.
  • Laos: eleventh-month full moon — Boun Ork Phansa.
  • Cambodia: eleventh-month full moon — Chenh Vossa.
  • Vietnam: fifteenth day of the ninth lunar month.

All three local names mean “leaving the rains.” They are calendar descriptions, not titles of a separate historical event. Because local calendars differ, check the particular year when an exact Gregorian date is needed.

3. The Invitation Ceremony

The ceremony's core is the invitation to correction. Monastics successively invite the community to point out anything seen, heard, or suspected, so that they may acknowledge and correct it. The Vinaya allows three, two, or one formal invitations according to circumstances. [Source: Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga - Theravāda Buddhism]

Its direction is deliberately counterintuitive: rather than waiting to be accused, monastics actively invite correction for their own benefit. The fortnightly Pātimokkha recitation is a regular check; Pavāraṇā is the retreat's closing review. It is an internal monastic procedure; laypeople do not perform it.

4. Pavāraṇā in Vietnam

Three layers for understanding Pavāraṇā in Vietnam

  • Foundational meaning: completion of the rains retreat and the Vinaya Invitation Ceremony.
  • Shared practice: chanting, Dhamma talks, and offerings may take place; the next day begins the one-month kaṭhina season, available only to an eligible Saṅgha (G.D5.7).
  • Custom: Vietnamese Mahāyāna communities also use the language of “leaving the rains.” Khmer, Lao, and Thai communities follow this point with local festival sequences. These are cultural and social layers.

The full moon is also an uposatha day; laypeople may undertake precepts or increase their practice (G.D3).

5. What newcomers should know

  • Laypeople join the general temple service, not the internal Pavāraṇā procedure.
  • Do not be surprised by frank monastic correction: that is the ceremony's essential practice.
  • Giving is voluntary. If you plan to offer robes, first learn about kaṭhina (G.D5.7).
  • The ceremony offers a lesson for everyone: actively invite helpful feedback.
  • For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.

6. Summary and next steps

  • Pavāraṇā is the end of the rains retreat: monastics complete the rains and invite mutual correction; it is not an observance of an event in the Buddha's life.
  • Vietnam records it on the fifteenth day of the ninth lunar month; Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia use the eleventh-month full moon.
  • The next day opens the kaṭhina robe-offering season for a Saṅgha that completed the rains retreat.

Next: G.D5.7 introduces Kaṭhina, the robe-offering season from the sixteenth day of the ninth lunar month to the fifteenth day of the tenth.

Glossary:

  • Pavāraṇā (Pali): the Invitation Ceremony at the closing full moon of the rains retreat.
  • Completed rains: having kept the full retreat, a condition for kaṭhina eligibility.
  • Pātimokkha (Pali): the monastic code recited on uposatha days.
  • Kaṭhina (Pali): the regulated robe-offering ceremony and season after the rains retreat.
  • Uposatha (Pali): a lunar observance day associated with monastic discipline and lay practice.
