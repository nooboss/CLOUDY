G.D5.33. Cambodia: Uposatha Observance Days
The eighth and fifteenth days of each Buddhist lunar month

Uposatha observance days (Pali: uposatha) are recurring days of practice governed by the lunar cycle. Their core meaning and monastic and lay observances are introduced in G.D3; their Thai and Lao organisation is described in G.D5.16 and G.D5. This article records the Khmer calendar convention: the eighth and fifteenth days of each Buddhist lunar month.

1. The uposatha cycle in a lunar month

A lunar month has four common observance points: the new moon, the eighth day of the waxing half, the full moon, and the eighth day of the waning half (often counted as the twenty-third day) (G.D5.16). This subsection records the eighth and fifteenth days of each month for the Khmer calendar, the two markers most widely observed; village monasteries may keep all four. All the major Khmer-calendar observances—Meak Bochea, Visak Bochea, Asalha Puja, Chenh Vossa, and kaṭhina—coincide with a full-moon marker, following the same lunar rhythm as the Thai and Lao calendar groups (G.D5.1).

2. Uposatha observance days in Cambodia

Three layers for understanding uposatha observance days in Cambodia

  • Foundational meaning: monastic uposatha procedures, including the fortnightly recitation of the Pātimokkha, and encouragement for laypeople to undertake precepts and hear the Dhamma (G.D3); the core is the same throughout Theravāda countries.
  • Shared practice: people make early offerings to monastics, keep the precepts more carefully during the day, and hear an evening Dhamma talk. On full-moon days, many undertake the eight precepts (Pali: aṭṭhasīla) for one day and night.
  • Custom: uposatha days are woven into the rhythm of village wat life, with gatherings of monastics and local people. The degree of participation varies from person to person. This is a religious and cultural rhythm.

3. What newcomers should know

  • The full moon is the easiest marker to attend: temples are busier and often hold an evening Dhamma talk.
  • Beginning with one regular observance each month is enough. Ask a nearby monastery about a one-day eight-precept observance for beginners (G.D3).
  • For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.

4. Summary and further reading

  • In the Khmer calendar, uposatha observance days are marked on the eighth and fifteenth days of each Buddhist lunar month, though monasteries may observe all four lunar points.
  • The major Khmer observances coincide with the full-moon marker, following the same lunar rhythm as the other two calendar groups.

Glossary:

  • Uposatha (Pali): a lunar observance day; monastics recite the disciplinary code, while laypeople undertake precepts and hear the Dhamma (G.D3).
  • Eight precepts (Pali: aṭṭhasīla): eight training rules observed for one day and night (G.D3).
  • Pātimokkha (Pali): the monastic code, recited twice each lunar month (G.D3).
  • Wat (Khmer-Thai): a monastery, the religious and communal centre (G.D5.27).
  • Buddhist Era (BE): a calendar era traditionally reckoned from the Buddha's final passing.
