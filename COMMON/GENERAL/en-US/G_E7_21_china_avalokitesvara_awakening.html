G.E7.21. China: Avalokiteśvara Awakening Commemoration (Guānyīn Chéngdào)
Nineteenth day of the sixth lunar month

China's Avalokiteśvara awakening commemoration (Chinese: Guānyīn Chéngdào, Avalokiteśvara's Enlightenment Day) commemorates the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara's awakening, on the nineteenth day of the sixth lunar month. It is the second of the three Avalokiteśvara commemorations (the preceding article — G.E7.20 — is the birth commemoration; the following article — G.E7.22 — is the renunciation commemoration). The core teaching on Avalokiteśvara is presented in G.E7.9 — this article keeps China's distinctive layer and an overview of the three commemorations.

1. The observance in China

On the nineteenth day of the sixth lunar month, Chinese monasteries hold chanting, prostrations, and offerings; devotees offer incense, make aspirations, and keep vegetarian meals on these occasions. Many travel to Avalokiteśvara seats (principal places of devotion to the bodhisattva) such as Mount Putuo to join the festival — pilgrimage in this festival season continues through the year, with three peaks falling exactly on the three commemoration days. In Pure Land monasteries (explained in article G.E7.19), the bodhisattva's name is recited in daily liturgies.

2. Three layers for understanding Avalokiteśvara's awakening

  • Foundational meaning: a bodhisattva's "awakening" is the fulfilment of the vow of compassion — hearing and relieving the suffering of beings; this differs from the Buddha's "awakening," the complete awakening beneath the Bodhi tree (articles G.E7.3 and G.E7.18).
  • Shared practice: chanting, prostrations, incense offerings, aspirations, vegetarian meals; reciting the bodhisattva's name.
  • Custom: pilgrimage to Mount Putuo and Avalokiteśvara temples; the three commemorations become three festival seasons in Chinese culture — known even to people who are not Buddhist.

3. What newcomers should know

  • A bodhisattva's "awakening" differs from the Buddha's "awakening": the bodhisattva chooses to remain in order to help beings, while the Buddha completes the path and teaches it again (articles G.C4 and G.B2).
  • For the core teaching on Avalokiteśvara, see G.E7.
  • For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.

4. Summary and next steps

  • China's Avalokiteśvara awakening commemoration falls on the nineteenth day of the sixth lunar month; its Chinese name is Guānyīn Chéngdào.
  • It is the middle of the three commemorations — the peak of the summer pilgrimage season.

Next: the following article — G.E7.22 — introduces China's Avalokiteśvara Renunciation Commemoration (Guānyīn Chūjiā), on the nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month.

Glossary:

  • Guānyīn Chéngdào (Chinese, Avalokiteśvara's Enlightenment Day): the Chinese name for the Avalokiteśvara awakening commemoration.
  • Vegetarian meal: eating without meat or fish, a practice of restraint and compassion toward living beings.
  • Pilgrimage: visiting sacred places to express reverence, following the festival season.
  • Place of practice: a place of worship and cultivation; here, monasteries principally devoted to Avalokiteśvara.

Source notes

  • The festival's core meaning is presented fully in G.E7.9 with its corresponding sources.
  • Mount Putuo and pilgrimage customs are general descriptions of Chinese Buddhism.
  • The nineteenth day of the sixth lunar month (China) is the standard festival-calendar notation used in this subsection; it is not converted into a fixed Gregorian date, which varies by year.
