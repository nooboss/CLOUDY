G.E7.22. China: Avalokiteśvara Renunciation Commemoration (Guānyīn Chūjiā)
Nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month

China's Avalokiteśvara renunciation commemoration (Chinese: Guānyīn Chūjiā, Avalokiteśvara's Renunciation Day) commemorates the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara's renunciation, on the nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month. It is the last of the three Avalokiteśvara commemorations — after the birth commemoration on the nineteenth day of the second lunar month (two articles earlier — G.E7.20) and the awakening commemoration on the nineteenth day of the sixth lunar month (the preceding article — G.E7.21). The core teaching on Avalokiteśvara is G.E7.

1. The observance in China

The nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month falls in the Chinese autumn; monasteries hold chanting, prostrations, and offerings as on the other two commemoration days. Because this commemoration takes renunciation (leaving household life for dedicated cultivation) as its theme, many monasteries hold taking-precepts ceremonies for laypeople — an opportunity for ordinary practitioners to commit to keeping a set of trainings for a given period, for example the five precepts for one day — see further on the five precepts in article G.C5.

2. Three layers for understanding Avalokiteśvara's renunciation

  • Foundational meaning: a bodhisattva's "renunciation" symbolises letting go — the condition of profound compassion: only by letting go of self-interest can one always listen to others (article G.C4).
  • Shared practice: chanting, prostrations, incense offerings, lay precept observance.
  • Custom: the Chinese autumn festival season associates Avalokiteśvara with the image of relieving suffering and granting peace; many families keep vegetarian meals at home on this occasion.

3. What newcomers should know

  • "Renunciation" is not required of laypeople: this commemoration recalls the spirit of letting go that can be applied in ordinary life — letting go of greed, anger, and clinging (article G.C4).
  • For the core teaching on Avalokiteśvara, see G.E7.
  • If you have not read the three commemorations from the beginning, read G.E7.20 and G.E7.

4. Summary and next steps

  • China's Avalokiteśvara renunciation commemoration falls on the nineteenth day of the ninth lunar month; its Chinese name is Guānyīn Chūjiā.
  • It is the last of the three commemorations on the nineteenth day of the second, sixth, and ninth lunar months; its theme is "letting go in order to be compassionate."

Next: the following article — G.E7.23 — introduces China's Ullambana Festival (Yúlánpén Jié), on the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month.

Glossary:

  • Guānyīn Chūjiā (Chinese, Avalokiteśvara's Renunciation Day): the Chinese name for the Avalokiteśvara renunciation commemoration.
  • Taking precepts: the ceremony of receiving and committing to keep Buddhist trainings.
  • Five precepts: the five foundational trainings for laypeople — see article G.C5.
  • Letting go: not clinging to craving, anger, and delusion — the gateway to freedom.
