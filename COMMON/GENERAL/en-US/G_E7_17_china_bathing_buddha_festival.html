G.E7.17. China: Bathing the Buddha Festival (Yùfó Jié)
Eighth day of the fourth lunar month

The Chinese Bathing the Buddha Festival (Chinese: Yùfó Jié, “Bathing the Buddha Festival”; also called Fódàn, Buddha's Birthday) commemorates the birth of the Buddha. It is the same observance introduced in Vietnam's Vesak article (G.E7.2), but its Chinese lunar-calendar date is the eighth day of the fourth lunar month, rather than Vietnam's fifteenth day of that month.

1. Why does China observe it on the eighth day?

Chinese Buddhism traditionally places the Buddha's birth on the eighth day of the fourth lunar month, following the date given in Chinese-translated Buddhist sources. Vietnam, at the intersection of different East Asian calendar traditions, uses more than one date: the fifteenth day of the fourth lunar month is widespread, while some older temples use the eighth-day marker. Korea also traditionally observes Buddha's Birthday on the eighth day of its fourth lunar month (G.E7.27).

These are different calendar conventions for the same observance, not three separate festivals. Since East Asian lunar and lunisolar calendars are not identical (G.E7.1), they should not be converted into one fixed Gregorian date for every year.

2. Yùfó Jié — the Bathing the Buddha Festival

The Chinese name directly foregrounds the central rite: bathing the Buddha, pouring scented water over a small image of the infant Buddha. This practice is introduced in G.E7. Its point is symbolic: practitioners reflect on cleansing greed, hatred, and delusion in their own minds; it is not a literal washing of a historical person.

Chinese monasteries have long held the rite on a large scale, sometimes placing a bathing basin in the temple courtyard for many participants. The rite spread through East Asia with Mahāyāna scriptures and liturgical traditions (G.E5).

Three layers for understanding Buddha's Birthday in China

  • Foundational meaning: recollecting the birth of the Buddha, with the same core as G.E7.
  • Shared practice: bathing the Buddha, chanting, offerings, life-release practices, and charitable activity.
  • Custom: the eighth day of the fourth lunar month in the Chinese calendar, with forms and scale varying by monastery and locality.

3. Buddha's Birthday in China today

In China today, Buddhist monasteries and communities observe Buddha's Birthday through bathing-the-Buddha ceremonies and cultural or charitable activities. Large monasteries may open services to the public; in regions with long-established Buddhist communities, such as Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Sichuan, and major pilgrimage sites, the occasion may draw many visitors. These are modern social and organisational forms, and vary by place.

4. What newcomers should know

  • In Chinese materials, Fódàn means Buddha's Birthday, while Yùfó Jié means Bathing the Buddha Festival; both refer to the same observance.
  • For the festival's foundational teaching, see G.E7.

5. Summary and next steps

  • China's Buddha's Birthday is the same observance as Vietnam's Vesak, kept on the eighth day of the fourth lunar month in the Chinese calendar.
  • The name Yùfó Jié highlights its central symbolic rite, bathing the Buddha.
  • The Chinese and Korean eighth-day dates and the Vietnamese fifteenth-day date are calendar conventions for one festival.

Glossary:

  • Yùfó Jié (Chinese): “Bathing the Buddha Festival,” the Chinese name for Buddha's Birthday that highlights the bathing rite.
  • Bathing the Buddha: pouring scented water over an infant-Buddha image as a symbol of cleansing one's own mind.
  • Fódàn (Chinese): Buddha's Birthday; another Chinese name for the same festival.
  • Buddha Jayantī (Sanskrit): a formal international name for Buddha's Birthday / Vesak, literally “victory of the Buddha.”
