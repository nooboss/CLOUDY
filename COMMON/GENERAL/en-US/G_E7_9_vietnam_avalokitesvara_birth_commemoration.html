G.E7.9. Vietnam: Avalokiteśvara Birth Commemoration
Nineteenth day of the second lunar month

This observance honours Avalokiteśvara, known in Vietnam as Quán Thế Âm, on the nineteenth day of the second lunar month. Avalokiteśvara is the bodhisattva who hears the cries of the world, an embodiment of active compassion.

1. Who is Avalokiteśvara?

Avalokiteśvara represents compassion that responds to suffering. In East Asia the bodhisattva is often portrayed in feminine form, especially as Guanyin / Quán Thế Âm, though this does not define a fixed biological identity. The Lotus Sutra depicts Avalokiteśvara appearing in forms suitable to those needing help.

2. Why are there three Avalokiteśvara observances?

Vietnamese custom remembers three stages: birth on the nineteenth day of the second lunar month, awakening on the nineteenth day of the sixth, and renunciation on the nineteenth day of the ninth. These are devotional calendar commemorations, not independently verifiable biography.

How is the day observed?

Temples may chant the Universal Gate chapter of the Lotus Sutra, recite the Great Compassion Dhāraṇī, make offerings, keep vegetarian days, and dedicate merit. The practice aims to cultivate compassion in action, rather than merely ask a bodhisattva to solve problems.

What newcomers should know

  • Join with quiet attention; chanting is optional for a first visit.
  • Let compassion include practical help, patient speech, and non-harming in daily life.
  • Offerings are voluntary and should never be a source of pressure.

Summary and next steps

  • The day honours Avalokiteśvara's birth on the nineteenth day of the second lunar month.
  • It is one of three Vietnamese Avalokiteśvara commemorations and centres compassion in action.

Glossary:

  • Avalokiteśvara: the bodhisattva of compassion, called Quán Thế Âm in Vietnam.
  • Universal Gate: a Lotus Sutra chapter focused on Avalokiteśvara.
  • Great Compassion Dhāraṇī: a long chant associated with Avalokiteśvara.

Source notes

  • Avalokiteśvara's responsive compassion is presented in the Lotus Sutra's Universal Gate chapter.
  • The three dates are Vietnamese devotional customs and not independently established biography.
