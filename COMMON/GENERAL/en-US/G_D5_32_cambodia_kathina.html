G.D5.32. Cambodia: Kaṭhina — the Robe-Offering Season
From the eleventh-month full moon to the twelfth-month full moon

Kaṭhina, commonly pronounced Kathen in Khmer, is Cambodia's annual robe-offering season, the year's major period of generosity. It lasts one lunar month, from the eleventh-month full moon to the twelfth-month full moon. The ceremony's core is explained in G.D5.7, in the Vietnamese calendar group; related seasons are observed in Thailand (G.D5.15) and Laos (G.D5.22). It opens immediately after Chenh Vossa (G.D5.31).

1. The one-month season in the Khmer Buddhist calendar

The season opens on the day after the full moon ending the rains retreat and closes at the next full moon. In Cambodia it runs from the eleventh-month full moon to the twelfth-month full moon. Vietnam records the same period as the sixteenth day of the ninth lunar month to the fifteenth day of the tenth (G.D5.7), while Thailand and Laos use the same eleventh-to-twelfth-month notation (G.D5.15, G.D5.22). The shared rules are these: the season lasts one month only; the offering is made to a Saṅgha that completed the rains retreat; robes are offered to the monastic community, not an individual; and each monastery conducts it once per year (G.D5.7).

2. Kaṭhina in Cambodia

Three layers for understanding the robe-offering season in Cambodia

  • Foundational meaning: a Vinaya-regulated robe offering to a Saṅgha that completed the rains retreat, and the merit of generosity (G.D5.7).
  • Shared practice: each village wat chooses a day in the season for a robe procession, chanting, Dhamma listening, the robe offering, and gifts of requisites (Pali: paccaya).
  • Custom: Cambodia's Kathen season is a village community festival, with colourful robe processions, music and dance, and pooled family offerings. Many Khmer people regard it as the year's greatest merit-making occasion and return from cities to join their home village wat. Khmer communities in southern Vietnam observe related ceremonial processions (G.D4; G.D5.7). This is a cultural and social layer.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Each monastery chooses its own ceremony date within the one-month season; ask a nearby temple for its calendar.
  • Join through pooled offerings of money, robes, or requisites, or by taking part in the procession, according to your means. Giving is voluntary (G.C4).
  • Dress modestly and keep orderly in the procession. For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Kaṭhina, or Kathen, is Cambodia's robe-offering season from the eleventh-month full moon to the twelfth-month full moon; its core is explained in G.D5.
  • Its Khmer expression is a village community festival of robe processions and homecoming, shared in related form by Khmer communities in southern Vietnam.

Next: G.D5.33 introduces Cambodia's uposatha observance days, recurring lunar days of practice. It is the final article in the G.D5 group.

Glossary:

  • Kaṭhina / Kathen (Pali: kaṭhina): the regulated robe-offering ceremony and season; Kathen is a common Khmer pronunciation (G.D5.7).
  • Thot Kathin (Thai): Thailand's corresponding robe-offering season (G.D5.15); Laos calls it Kaṭhina (G.D5.22).
  • Requisites (Pali: paccaya): the four basic supports for monastic life—robes, food, lodging, and medicines.
  • Completed the rains retreat: having resided for the full rains retreat at a monastery, a condition for receiving the kaṭhina offering (G.D5.6).
  • Wat (Khmer-Thai): a monastery, the religious and communal centre (G.D5.27).
  • Buddhist Era (BE): a calendar era traditionally reckoned from the Buddha's final passing.

Source notes

  • The core kaṭhina regulations are in the Vinaya Piṭaka, Mahāvagga, Kathinakkhandhaka - Theravāda Buddhism; they are explained fully in G.D5.
  • The name Kathen and village community festival forms in Cambodia are described in accounts of Cambodian Buddhism; these belong to the customary layer.
  • The eleventh-to-twelfth-month full-moon season is the standard festival-date notation used in this subsection; it is not converted into a fixed Gregorian date, which varies by year.
