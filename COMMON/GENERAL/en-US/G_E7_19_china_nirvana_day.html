G.E7.19. China: Nirvana Day (Nièpán Rì)
Fifteenth day of the second lunar month

Nièpán Rì, China's Nirvana Day, commemorates the Buddha's final passing on the fifteenth day of the second lunar month. This is the same lunar marker as Vietnam's observance (G.E7.4) and Korea's (G.E7.29); Japan observes it on 15 February in the Gregorian calendar (G.E7.31). Its event and teaching are introduced fully in G.E7.

1. The observance in China

On the second-lunar-month full moon, Chinese Buddhist monasteries hold chanting, prostrations, precept observance, and offerings. Many also recite Amitābha and Pure Land texts. This Pure Land emphasis is a characteristic Mahāyāna Chinese form: offering and dedication of merit are linked to reflecting on the Buddha's final teaching and the continuing path of practice.

2. Three layers for understanding Nirvana Day in China

  • Foundational meaning: nirvana is liberation, the cessation of suffering; the Buddha's final passing bears witness to the path (G.E7.4).
  • Shared practice: chanting, prostrations, offerings, and teaching on nirvana and the Buddha's final exhortation.
  • Custom: Chinese observance often turns toward Amitābha's Pure Land, a distinctive Mahāyāna devotional context (G.E7.7).

3. What newcomers should know

  • Final passing is the final release of an awakened one, not ordinary death and not the travel of a permanent soul.

4. Summary and next steps

  • China's Nirvana Day is Nièpán Rì, on the fifteenth day of the second lunar month.
  • Its Chinese Mahāyāna tone includes chanting, offerings, dedication of merit, and a Pure Land orientation.

Glossary:

  • Nièpán Rì (Chinese): Nirvana Day, the Chinese observance of the Buddha's final passing.
  • Nirvana (Pali: Nibbāna): liberation, the cessation of suffering.
  • Amitābha: the Buddha of Infinite Light and the Western Pure Land.
  • Pure Land: Amitābha's realm of bliss, a favourable setting for practice.
