G.F5.10. (Phugpa) Ganden Ngamcho — Tsongkhapa Commemoration
Twenty-fifth day of the tenth Tibetan month

The Ganden Ngamcho (Tibetan: Ganden Ngamcho, meaning "the 25th day of the Ganden month" — the tenth month of the Tibetan calendar — the Ganden Ngamchoe Festival) is the day that commemorates the passing away of Je Tsongkhapa (Tibetan: Tsongkhapa, 1357–1419) — the founder of the Gelug school (the Gelug school), the largest school of Tibetan Buddhism — on the 25th day of the tenth Tibetan month. It is one of the great founder-commemoration days of the Tibetan tradition — similar in spirit to the founder commemorations of the East Asian schools (for example Hōnen of Japan — article G.E7.34).

1. Who was Tsongkhapa

Je Tsongkhapa (Tibetan: Tsongkhapa, 1357–1419) was the great reformer of Tibetan Buddhism in the 14th–15th centuries: widely learned in many lineages, compiler of foundational treatises on the path of practice, and founder of the Gelug school — the school that stresses thorough study of the teachings before entering Tantric practices (see the Tibetan schools in article G.F2). The great monasteries founded by him and his disciples — Ganden, Drepung, Sera — became the greatest centres of learning in Tibet; this lineage later became associated with the titles of the Dalai Lama (the Dalai Lama) and the Panchen Lama (the Panchen Lama) — the famous reincarnate teachers (the tulku system — see article G.F2). The festival name "Ganden Ngamcho" means "the 25th day of the Ganden month" — the day he passed away at Ganden Monastery in 1419.

2. How it is observed

  • At Gelug monasteries: sutra chanting — offerings — retelling of his life; great lamp-lighting sessions: the popular subsidiary name of the festival is the "butter-lamp festival" (Ngamcho) — thousands of butter lamps (made with yak butter, used as candles) are lit throughout the monasteries.
  • In old Lhasa: the occasion was associated with a great butter-lamp display — artisans modelled colourful figures from butter (Tibetan: yak butter sculptures — butter figures — butter sculptures) exhibited at the monasteries — a famous distinctive feature (the custom layer).
  • Lay devotees: chant sutras, recite his mantra (Sanskrit: mantra) (the prayer "Mañjuśrī through his words"), perform wholesome deeds; many keep precepts on the day.

3. Three layers for reading Ganden Ngamcho

  • Doctrinal — practice layer: commemorating a great founder — recalling the characteristic features of his path of practice: thorough study — keeping the precepts — combining doctrine and practice.
  • Shared practice layer: chanting sutras, offering lamps, retelling his life, taking short-term precepts.
  • Custom layer: the butter-lamp display, butter sculptures, community customs — Tibetan culture.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Ganden Ngamcho = the 25th day of the tenth Tibetan month — the day of the passing away of Je Tsongkhapa (1357–1419), founder of the Gelug school.
  • Distinctive features: butter lamps and butter sculptures — as with other founder commemorations, the theme is continuing the spirit of the founder's path of practice.

Glossary:

  • Ganden Ngamcho (Tibetan, meaning "the 25th day of the Ganden month" — the Ganden Ngamchoe Festival): the commemoration of the passing away of Je Tsongkhapa.
  • Je Tsongkhapa (Tibetan: Tsongkhapa, 1357–1419): the founder of the Gelug school — the great reformer of Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Gelug school (the Gelug school): the Tibetan Buddhist school founded by Je Tsongkhapa — stressing thorough study of the teachings before entering Tantric practices.
  • Dalai Lama (the Dalai Lama): the title of the reincarnate teachers of the Gelug school — see article G.F2 on the tulku system.
  • Butter lamps: lamps fuelled by yak butter — a familiar offering at Himalayan monasteries.
  • Butter sculptures (Tibetan: yak butter sculptures): figures — images modelled from colourful butter — a characteristic art of the festival at Tibetan monasteries.
  • Passing away: the term used for the death of highly virtuous monastics.
