G.F5.6. (Phugpa) Saga Dawa Düchen — Festival of the Vaiśākha Full Moon
Fifteenth day of the fourth Tibetan month

Saga Dawa Düchen (Tibetan: Saga Dawa Düchen — "Düchen" means "great festival occasion", explained in article G.F5.1 — the Festival of the Vaiśākha Full Moon) commemorates the birth, enlightenment, and passing away of the Buddha — all three events converging on a single day in the Tibetan tradition — on the 15th day of the fourth Tibetan month, the full moon (Sanskrit: pūrṇimā) of the Saga Dawa month (previous article — G.F5.5). It is the greatest festival day of the year in Vajrayāna Buddhism, corresponding to the Theravāda Vesak (article G.D5.3 — the core article on these three events) and combining the Mahāyāna days of the Buddha's Birth — Enlightenment — Passing Away (articles G.E7.2 — G.E7.19).

1. Three events — one day

According to Tibetan tradition, on the full moon of Vaiśākha, all three of the greatest events of the Buddha's life took place: birth (article G.B1), enlightenment (article G.B2) and passing away (article G.B2, final part). This "convergence of three events on one day" is characteristic of the traditions using the ancient Indian calendar (Theravāda celebrates Vesak in the same way — article G.D5.3); East Asia separates the three milestones into three distinct days (article G.E7.1, section 2). The detailed narrative of each event — presented in article G.D5.3 — is not repeated here.

2. How it is observed

  • At monasteries: great sutra chanting dedicated to the three events; many monasteries hold taking precepts (receiving and keeping the Buddhist precepts) on this day; large numbers of butter lamps (fuelled by butter) are offered.
  • Lay followers: as it is the most merit-multiplying day of the year, people give special attention to wholesome deeds: keeping precepts (many eat vegetarian throughout the whole month — see article G.F5.5), life release (Tibetan: tsetar), offerings, and pilgrimage circuit (Tibetan: kora, from the Sanskrit "cakra" — circle) around sacred places.
  • Community tradition: in this month many people make the pilgrimage circuit around Mount Kailash (Tibetan: Kang Rinpoche — "precious mountain"); the full-moon 15th day is the peak of the pilgrimage season.

3. Three layers for reading Saga Dawa Düchen

  • Foundational meaning: the three milestones of the Buddha's life — birth, enlightenment, and passing away — whose core is in articles G.D5.3 and G.B2.
  • Shared practice: sutra chanting, keeping precepts, making offerings, offering lamps, tsetar, kora.
  • Custom: the Kailash pilgrimage season; high-plateau community festivals; the custom of hanging new prayer flags (article G.F2).

4. Summary and next steps

  • Saga Dawa Düchen is the 15th day of the fourth Tibetan month — the full moon; it commemorates all three milestones of birth — enlightenment — passing away; the greatest festival day of the year in Vajrayāna Buddhism.
  • How to read it: the same core as Vesak — read article G.D5.3 for the full content.

Glossary:

  • Saga Dawa Düchen (Tibetan — the Festival of the Vaiśākha Full Moon): the Tibetan name of the full-moon festival of the Saga Dawa month — commemorating the birth — enlightenment — passing away of the Buddha.
  • Vaiśākha Pūrṇimā (Sanskrit: Vaiśākha Pūrṇimā — the full moon of Vaisakha): the Sanskrit name of the full-moon day of the month of Vaiśākha — the shared root name of the Vesak — Buddha's Birthday observances.
  • Full moon (Sanskrit: pūrṇimā): the full moon.
  • Life release (Tibetan: tsetar): ransoming and freeing beings about to be killed — a widespread compassionate act of Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Pilgrimage circuit (Tibetan: kora, from the Sanskrit "cakra" — circle): a devotional circuit around a sacred place — see article G.F5.
  • Mount Kailash (Tibetan: Kang Rinpoche — "precious mountain"): the sacred mountain in Tibet — the greatest pilgrimage site of the Saga Dawa season.
  • Butter lamps: lamps fuelled by butter — a familiar offering at Himalayan monasteries.

Source notes

  • The core of the three events — presented in full in article G.D5.3 (Vesak) and G.B2 (the life of the Buddha); this article keeps the distinctive Tibetan-tradition layer.
  • The understanding that the three events converge on the full-moon day of the month of Vaiśākha — the shared understanding of the traditions using the ancient Indian calendar.
  • The festival date of the 15th day of the fourth Tibetan month follows the standard festival calendar of this subsection (the Phugpa system); it is not converted into a fixed Gregorian date, which varies by year.
