G.F5.4. (Phugpa) Chotrul Düchen — Great Festival of Miracles
Fifteenth day of the first Tibetan month

The Great Festival of Miracles (Tibetan: Chotrul Düchen, meaning "great festival of miracles") commemorates the fifteenth day — the climax of the miracle season (previous article — G.F5.3), on the 15th day of the 1st Tibetan month — the full-moon day. It is one of the four Düchen days — the four greatest festival days of the year in Tibetan Buddhism (article G.F5.1, section 3).

1. The commemorated event

The 15th day of the 1st Tibetan month commemorates the final day of the miracle contest at Shravasti (the ancient capital — Pali: Sāvatthī, Sanskrit: Śrāvastī) — when, according to the traditional account, the Buddha fully displayed his miracles before the crowds and the rival teachers were defeated, with many taking refuge. The Tibetan tradition regards this as one of the greatest teaching events of the Buddha's life — on a par with the turning of the wheel of Dharma (article G.F5.8). The day coincides with the full moon of the first Tibetan month — as with the other great Buddhist festivals (article G.D3 on Uposatha days by the phases of the moon).

2. Correct understanding of "miracle display"

Newcomers easily misunderstand "miracle display" as an occasion for honouring magic. Traditional teachers explain the opposite: the purpose of the miracles was to open the door to the teaching — miracles have value when they lead people to a right understanding of suffering and of the path to the end of suffering. The Buddha repeatedly advised his disciples not to use psychic powers to attract followers (to use them only when truly necessary for teaching); what should be honoured today is the wisdom and compassion that this event symbolises. This is the point to separate from the customary layer: the fairs and lamps of the festival season are culture, not "miracles".

3. How to observe

  • At monasteries: grand sutra chanting, offerings of butter lamps; many monasteries display butter lamps in public and draw a mandala (Sanskrit: maṇḍala, a sacred circular map symbolising the pure world) in coloured sand on the great festival days.
  • Lay devotees: chanting sutras, reciting mantras (Sanskrit: mantra), offering lamps, making offerings, life release (Tibetan: tsetar) — good deeds said to be multiplied many times on a Düchen day.
  • Community: public chanting assemblies; in old Lhasa and at the great monasteries, the occasion is linked with the Monlam prayer festival (Tibetan: Monlam, meaning "aspiration" — the Great Prayer Festival) — the great assembly for prayer.

4. Summary and next steps

  • Chotrul Düchen = the 15th day of the 1st Tibetan month — the full moon; the climax of the miracle season; one of the four Düchen days.
  • The spirit of the festival: honouring wisdom — compassion over magic; doing good deeds because merit is multiplied many times.

Glossary:

  • Chotrul Düchen (Tibetan, meaning "great festival of miracles" — the Great Festival of Miracles): the Tibetan name for the Great Festival of Miracle Display — the 15th day of the 1st Tibetan month.
  • Miracle display (meaning "wondrous transformation" — Sanskrit: prātihārya): miracles displayed for the purpose of teaching.
  • Shravasti (the ancient capital of the kingdom of Kośala — Pali: Sāvatthī, Sanskrit: Śrāvastī): the city where the miracle contest took place according to the traditional account.
  • Mandala (Sanskrit: maṇḍala): a sacred circular map symbolising the pure world — drawn in coloured sand on the great festivals of the Tibetan tradition.
  • Butter lamps: lamps fuelled by yak butter — a familiar offering of Himalayan monasteries.
  • Tsetar (Tibetan — life release): ransoming and releasing creatures about to be killed — a widespread compassionate practice of Tibetan Buddhism.
  • Monlam (Tibetan, meaning "aspiration" — the Great Prayer Festival): the great prayer assembly — the public chanting festival of Tibetan Buddhism.
