G.D5.27. Cambodia: Meak Bochea — Saṅgha Day
Full moon of the third Buddhist month (Tawai)

Meak Bochea is the Khmer name for Māgha Pūjā, or Saṅgha Day (Pali: Māgha Pūjā)—the observance of the traditional gathering of 1,250 arahants (Pali: arahant, one fully liberated from the mental defilements) and the Ovāda Pāṭimokkha teaching. Its core meaning is explained fully in G.D5.2, in the Vietnamese calendar group. In Cambodia, it falls on the full moon of the third Buddhist month, the month of Tawai, the same lunar occasion as Thailand's Makha Bucha (G.D5.8) and Laos's Boun Khao Chi (G.D5.17).

1. What does “Meak Bochea” mean?

Khmer preserves Pali roots in the names of its festivals: Meak is the month of Māgha; Bochea, from Pali pūjā, means paying homage or making an offering. Thus Meak Bochea means “homage in the month of Māgha”, following the same structure as the Thai name (G.D5.8). Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia use festival names with the same Pali roots but their own sound changes: Bucha in Thai, Boucha in Lao, and Bochea in Khmer. The articles in this group make those relationships clear for each festival.

2. Meak Bochea in Cambodia

Three layers for understanding Meak Bochea in Cambodia

  • Foundational meaning: the gathering of 1,250 arahants and the threefold teaching: avoid evil, cultivate good, and purify the mind (G.D5.2).
  • Shared practice: temple visits, Dhamma listening, offerings, and undertaking the eight precepts (Pali: aṭṭhasīla) for one day and night. In the evening, people make a candle procession around the temple, circling three times with candles, incense, and flowers—a shared Theravāda custom, such as the wian tian described in G.D5.
  • Custom: Meak Bochea is observed nationally in Cambodia, while its scale varies by locality. Rural Khmer communities often centre the day on the village wat, the hub of community life (G.D4). Its status in the public-holiday calendar may change by year.

3. What newcomers should know

  • Visit the temple on the full-moon evening for the candle procession, the day's most solemn part.
  • Offer according to your means and dress modestly. For the spirit of learning without anxiety about mistakes, see G.E6.
  • For the festival's teaching, read the core article: G.D5.

Glossary:

  • Meak Bochea (Khmer): the Khmer name for Māgha Pūjā; its core meaning is explained in G.D5.
  • Bochea (Khmer, from Pali pūjā): the “homage” element in Khmer festival names; cognate with Thai Bucha and Lao Boucha.
  • Wat (Khmer-Thai): a monastery, the religious and communal centre in Theravāda countries.
  • Ovāda Pāṭimokkha: the teaching associated with the gathering of 1,250 arahants (G.D5.2).
  • Arahant (Pali: arahant): one who has completely ended the defilements of mind and the round of rebirth.
  • Buddhist Era (BE): a calendar era traditionally reckoned from the Buddha's final passing.

Source notes
