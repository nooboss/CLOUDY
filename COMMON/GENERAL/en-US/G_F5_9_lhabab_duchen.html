G.F5.9. (Phugpa) Lhabab Düchen — Festival of the Descent from Heaven
Twenty-second day of the ninth Tibetan month

Lhabab Düchen (Tibetan: Lhabab Düchen — "lha" means god or heaven, "bab" means descent — the Festival of the Descent from Heaven) commemorates the day the Buddha descended from the Heaven of the Thirty-Three (Pali: Tāvatiṃsa, Sanskrit: Trāyastriṃśa) back to the human realm, on the twenty-second day of the ninth Tibetan month. The Sanskrit name of the event is Devāvarohaṇa (Sanskrit: devāvarohaṇa — the Descent). It is one of the four Düchen days (article G.F5.1, section 3); the same event as in article G.D5.14 (Thailand — Tak Bat Devo).

1. The event commemorated

According to the account shared by the Buddhist traditions: during one rains retreat (Pali: vassa), the Buddha ascended to the Heaven of the Thirty-Three (Pali: Tāvatiṃsa, Sanskrit: Trāyastriṃśa — the second heaven among the six heavens of the desire realm) to teach the Dharma to his mother — Queen Mahāmāyā — who had passed away not long after giving birth to the Prince. After three months of teaching, the Buddha descended back to the human realm at the city of Sankissa (Sanskrit: Saṃkāśya) — by a sacred triple stairway (as told: the middle stair of jewels for the Buddha, the two side stairs for gods and humans). The detailed story — presented in article G.D5.14; the Tibetan tradition commemorates this day of descent as one of the four greatest events of the ritual year.

2. How it is observed

  • At monasteries: recitation of sūtras and prayers; teachings on gratitude toward parents — for this event is linked to the Buddha's repayment of his debt of gratitude to his mother; offering rituals of butter lamps.
  • Lay devotees: wholesome deeds whose merit is multiplied many times — offerings, life release (tsetar), sūtra recitation, recitation of mantras (Sanskrit: mantra).
  • Tibetan emphasis: this day often opens the end of the summer practice cycle of Tibetan monasteries — the end of the rains retreat (Pali: pavāraṇā) — in the same spirit as the Pavāraṇā of the Southern tradition: article G.D5.6; many monasteries hold gratitude ceremonies — offerings to teachers — parents.

3. Three levels for reading Lhabab Düchen

  • Root doctrinal level: commemorating the Descent — and the Buddha's filial devotion to his mother; the same core as article G.D5.
  • Shared practice level: recitation, offerings, tsetar; repaying the kindness of parents — teachers.
  • Customary level: autumn festivities; the custom of hanging new prayer flags; in Bhutan — Himalayan regions, the occasion is joined with end-of-rains-season community festivals.

4. Summary and next step

  • Lhabab Düchen = 22nd day of the 9th Tibetan month; commemorating the Buddha's Descent from the Heaven of the Thirty-Three — one of the four Düchens.
  • Central theme: filial devotion — repaying the kindness of parents; a day of many-times-multiplied merit.

Glossary:

  • Lhabab Düchen (Tibetan, "lha" means god or heaven, "bab" means descent — the Festival of the Descent from Heaven): the Tibetan name of the great festival of the Buddha's Descent from heaven.
  • Devāvarohaṇa (Sanskrit: devāvarohaṇa — the Descent): the Sanskrit name of the Descent event.
  • Heaven of the Thirty-Three (the second heaven among the six heavens of the desire realm — Pali: Tāvatiṃsa, Sanskrit: Trāyastriṃśa): the heaven where the Buddha taught the Dharma for three months to his mother (see article G.C4 on the six realms).
  • City of Sankissa (Sanskrit: Saṃkāśya): where the Buddha descended back to the human realm according to the account.
  • Tsetar (Tibetan — life release (tsetar)): ransoming and releasing creatures about to be killed — see article G.F5.
  • Butter lamps: lamps fuelled with yak butter — a familiar offering of Himalayan monasteries.
  • End of the rains retreat (Pali: pavāraṇā): the close of the three-month rains retreat — see article G.D5.
