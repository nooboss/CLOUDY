M.C2.5. The Progress of Insight

The previous article's analytical frameworks showed experience has structure. This article presents the map of the whole road: the seven purifications and sixteen knowledges — the classical progress framework many insight systems use as reference — with the ten corruptions: the characteristic mid-way misreading traps. These two contents are the most advanced level. The reading principle throughout: this is a map for orientation, not a ladder for self-grading — repeating the principle used in "Levels of Concentration" (article M.C1.4), and even more important here.

1. The seven purifications — the path framework

The seven purifications (Pali: satta visuddhi) are the overall framework the Visuddhimagga (Pali: visuddhimagga) erects — taking its very name from here: "The Path of Purification". [Source: Visuddhimagga - the Theravāda commentarial system; the root frame from Saṃyutta Nikāya, SN 12.23 - Theravāda Buddhism.]

  1. Purification of ethics: a harmless way of life.
  2. Purification of mind: collectedness — a settled, usable mind.
  3. Purification of view (Pali: diṭṭhi-visuddhi): correctly understanding how to view experience — body and mind as phenomena, not a "self" (based on distinguishing mind – matter).
  4. Purification by overcoming doubt (Pali: kaṅkhāvitaraṇa-visuddhi): directly seeing phenomena arising conditionally — doubt about "what makes these experiences" dissolves by itself.
  5. Purification by knowledge and vision of what is and is not the path (Pali: maggāmagga-ñāṇadassana-visuddhi): distinguishing the genuine path from things "resembling the path" — exactly section 3's place: the ten corruptions.
  6. Purification by knowledge and vision of the way: wisdom flowing along the main sequence — observation knowledges deep enough leading to path (Pali: magga) and fruition (Pali: phala) — the path's breakthroughs, presented in detail in traditional sources, not goals for self-description through writing.
  7. Purification by knowledge and vision (Pali: ñāṇadassana-visuddhi): peak seeing knowledge — where the path crystallizes.

These seven levels are an organizing frame — the path's logical order, not a rigid time sequence: one may return to training foundations (ethics, mind) many times while knowledges have not yet deepened.

2. The sixteen knowledges — the sequence of seeing levels

Within the fifth of the seven purifications, the system unfolds the sixteen knowledges (Pali: soḷasa ñāṇa) — the sequence of seeing levels practitioners successively pass through in insight systems. Presented by main groups: [Source: Visuddhimagga, ch. XXI - the Theravāda commentarial system.]

  • Foundation group (1–4): distinguishing mind – matter; seeing phenomena arising conditionally; clearly knowing all parts of experience; seeing one is practising in the right direction — these four erect the survey foundation.
  • Arising – passing group (5–6): knowledge of arising – passing — all phenomena popping up and dissolving continuously; followed by sudden alerting, uplifting, peaceful states.
  • Clearly-seeing-unsatisfactoriness group (7–11): from seeing arising – passing, the mind gradually sees clearly the unsatisfactoriness of all phenomena — knowledges of suffering, its faces, and deep disenchantment before all forms of clinging.
  • Release group (12–14): wanting to escape the phenomenon-block; reviewing with personal witnessing; naturally smashing attachments — knowledges from "wanting escape" to "full release".
  • Path and fruition (15–16): the final two knowledges are breakthrough states — path and fruition (Pali: magga and phala) — the path's crystallization point; traditional sources describe differently and always require confirmation from a qualified guide.

To remember: the sixteen-knowledge sequence is the commentarial system's synthesized map — the canonical basket describes seeing levels in more general language; modern schools rearrange details differently. The map's practical value: when a difficult phase arrives (a despondent, doubting, escape-wanting phase) — knowing this is a normal stretch of the long road prevents panic and abandonment. Beginners need not memorize each knowledge's name — only know the map exists and places differ between lineages.

3. The ten corruptions of insight

This is the article's most practical part — naming exactly where the taxonomy points: states easily mistaken mid-way. The corruptions of insight (Pali: vipassanupakkilesa) are ten pleasant or strikingly luminous phenomena that may appear once knowledge of arising – passing (section 2, group 5–6) has firmed — all not attainments: [Source: Yamaka Sutta, Saṃyutta Nikāya, SN 22.85 - Theravāda Buddhism; detailed listing per Visuddhimagga, ch. XX - the Theravāda commentarial system.]

  1. Light (in the mind, unusually radiant).
  2. Rapture — upwelling excitement (of kinds: gently beginning, trembling, floating...).
  3. Tranquillity — body and mind smooth and soft.
  4. Bliss — strong bliss, deep ease.
  5. Resolution — sudden strong confidence ("this Dhamma is truly right!").
  6. Exertive effort — abundant energy, sitting very long without fatigue.
  7. Excessively distinct mindfulness — everything appearing strangely sharp.
  8. Equanimity — easeful balance to the point of "nothing left to do".
  9. The wish to pluck — liking the above states, wanting them to return.
  10. And that very liking itself — the final link making the whole ten into corruptions.

The shared recognition — one sentence: all pleasant or radiant, all arriving suddenly, all accompanied by an "I have attained something" sentence. Those three marks are the corruptions' fingerprint, not wisdom's. The classical handling has two steps (as article M.C1.3 said): recognizing ("this is phenomenon, not attainment") and returning to the subject, continuing to observe arising – passing as if nothing happened. The commentarial system places these states in the path and not-path (maggāmagga) part — the fifth of the seven purifications (section 1) is exactly this distinguishing step. Practitioners alone, without teacher: meeting such a chain of states, best to lower intensity, discuss with someone experienced, and never declare "attaining anything" to others.

4. Using the map rightly

  • The map orients; it does not roll-call. The question "which knowledge am I at?" is a skewed question — self-grading on the map easily leads to self-deception or self-scolding; the map's value appears only when a guide uses it to look over practitioners.
  • Difficult phases are stretches of the long road, not malfunctions. Despondency, boredom, escape-wanting, doubt — per the map, these states are ordinary stretches of the middle knowledges; so understanding helps passing calmly, without hasty conclusions about oneself (safe responses in article M.C2.6).
  • The seven purifications remind the foundation: when wisdom stalls, the answer is usually not "digging wisdom deeper" but returning to foundation — ethics (article M.A1), collectedness (group C1), and right wise attention (article M.B4).
  • Do not self-declare attainments. The tradition's general principle: high levels need confirmation from the qualified; self-concluding is one of the fastest ways to turn practice into selfhood.

5. Summary and next steps

  • Seven purifications: the overall frame from ethics – collectedness foundation to peak seeing knowledge; the fifth level — distinguishing path and not-path — is where the ten corruptions sit.
  • Sixteen knowledges: the map of seeing levels, from distinguishing mind – matter to path – fruition; traditional lineages arrange details differently; the greatest value is understanding difficult stretches as normal.
  • Ten corruptions: pleasant / radiant states arriving suddenly kèm the "attained" thought; fingerprint: pleasant + sudden + declaration-wish; handling: recognizing — returning to the subject — lowering intensity if needed — discussing with the experienced.
  • Golden rule: the map orients; do not self-grade; difficult is normal; stalled, return to foundation; high needs confirmation.

Suggested next reading: the final article "Difficulties and Safety" — neutrally describing difficult phases (fear, despondency, prolonged unease...), ways to lower intensity, raise collectedness and loving-kindness foundations, and boundaries needing a teacher or professional. See you there.

Glossary:

  • Seven purifications (Pali: satta visuddhi): seven purifying levels of the path per the Visuddhimagga: ethics, mind, right view, overcoming doubt, distinguishing path – not-path, the way, purification by knowledge and vision.
  • Sixteen knowledges (Pali: soḷasa ñāṇa): the sequence of seeing levels in the insight progress per the commentarial system; "ñāṇa" means knowledge / wisdom.
  • Corruptions of insight (Pali: vipassanupakkilesa): ten pleasant / radiant phenomena easily mistaken for attainment; the final link is the very liking of them.
  • Path and fruition (Pali: magga and phala): breakthroughs of the insight path; the tradition always requires confirmation from the qualified.
  • Nikāya (Pali: Nikāya): the names of the major collections of discourses in Theravāda Buddhism, compiled by theme or length.
  • Visuddhimagga (Pali: visuddhimagga): the compendium of meditation practice in Theravāda Buddhism, composed by the Elder Buddhaghosa around the 5th century.
  • Pali: the language in which the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism are preserved.
  • Sanskrit: the language of much of the Mahāyāna Buddhist and Vajrayāna Buddhist scriptural literature.
  • Sino-Vietnamese: the Vietnamese pronunciation of Chinese characters; most meditation terms entered Vietnamese through scriptures translated into Chinese (the Chinese Canon).
