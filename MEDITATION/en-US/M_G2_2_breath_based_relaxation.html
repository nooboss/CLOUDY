M.G2.2. Breath-Based Relaxation

This is group G2's first practice article — written for those arriving at meditation for health, needing no religious beliefs. Breath-based relaxing meditation takes exactly one material: breath sensations and the body's releasing; doable today, in all postures, needing no props. Those already practising in group B will see this is a "light version" of breath-mindfulness articles — the same material, differing purposes and uses.

1. How does this article differ from breath-mindfulness in group B?

Two articles use the same material — breath — but differ in purposes and attention ways:

  • Breath-mindfulness (group B articles, and group C1 advanced articles) is attention-training exercises: breath is the "gym" — counting, closely holding, recognizing distraction, returning; the goal is mindfulness (Pali: sati) capacities gradually firming across sessions.
  • Breath-based relaxing meditation is releasing and recovering exercises: breath is the "road leading relaxing signals throughout bodies" — attention going where, bodies releasing there; expected results are pleasant states, less tension, easier sleep entry — not measurable skills.

Two articles supplement each other: relaxing for recovering, breath-mindfulness for training — like warm baths and walking both good for bodies but doing two different tasks. Health-motivated beginners may begin at once from this article; regularly training persons using this article as "rest sessions" between training sessions.

2. Following step by step

Recommended durations: 5–10 minutes, sitting or lying both fine (lying easily sleeps — noting this when choosing moments):

  • Step 1 — Entering postures (≈ 1 minute): sitting leaning steadily, both feet touching ground; or lying on backs, hands comfortably alongside bodies. Closing eyes or lowering gazes to floors. First, letting bodies make a few small natural movements — shrugging shoulders, lightly nodding heads — then settling.
  • Step 2 — Three opening extended breaths: breathing in through noses counting three, breathing out through mouths counting six — out-breaths longer than in-breaths (the 3:6 rhythm already introduced in article M.G1.4); doing three times. This uneven rhythm is the "switch" helping bodies shift to resting modes.
  • Step 3 — Returning breath to natural, only following: now uncontrolled — breathing slowly however much also fine, shallowly however much also fine. Choosing one distinctest sensation place: rising – falling bellies, or in – out air at nostrils. Only knowing each breath.
  • Step 4 — Going with breath through "release words": each breathing out, silently speaking one short phrase: "breathing out — releasing shoulders", "breathing out — releasing jaws", "breathing out — releasing bellies", "breathing out — releasing whole persons"... cycling through phrases. This is this article's distinctive point against breath-mindfulness: each out-breath is one actively releasing time of one region.
  • Step 5 — Soft ending: when time ends (soft bells or self-counting), no hasty eye opening — taking a few breaths, moving fingers and toes, then opening eyes. This soft "landing" keeps whole post-session pleasant feelings.

If astray mid-sessions — thoughts flying away, hands suddenly scratching everywhere — only seeing then returning to step 3: this is not faults; and because the purpose is relaxing, not counting astray times as attention-training articles still do (article M.B7). Guidance recordings in apps may lead each step when newly beginning — and exactly article M.G1.8, when articles are memorized then gradually shifting to unguided sitting.

3. Needing to know — to unbracing and unworrying

  • No bracing to "relax successfully": excessive effort is the surest way to unrelax. Seeing oneself "trying to breathe deeply", retreating to only observing — relaxing is results when no longer bracing, not tasks done through pressing force.
  • Light dizziness when deeply breathing: some slightly dizzy, hot-faced in first sessions because breathing deeper than usual. Countermeasures: breathing a little shallower, shortening sessions (three–five minutes), later gradually increasing. If much dizziness — sitting instead of lying, and breathing in natural rhythms only.
  • Nodding off mid-sessions: drowsiness is a normal response to releasing articles — not faults. If the goal is sleeping, nodding off lying in this article is exactly desired; if midday needing alertness, choosing straight sitting postures and lightly opening eyes.
  • Difficult feelings arising instead of pleasantness: sometimes, when bodies begin settling, heavy, sad feelings, or unpleasant flashbacks arise — possible with all meditation articles. Immediate countermeasures: opening eyes, sensing bodies' contact points with chairs / cushions, looking around rooms naming a few objects — "grounding" ways. If this repeats many sessions, temporarily reducing amounts, and if trauma-related — reading article M.G2.10 (trauma-sensitive meditation) or discussing with professionals before continuing.

4. Uses in everyday life

This article has one great advantage: its abridged versions are usable right in situations without needing long sessions:

  • After work hours — shifting states: sitting in vehicles or before doorways, five extended breaths with "release words" (step 4) — helping not carrying workplace tension home; this is a perfect "anchor" for busy persons (article M.G1.5).
  • Before tense events: before meetings, before entering exam rooms, before difficult conversations — three 3:6 breaths (step 2) are thirty-second abridged versions.
  • When in pain or discomfort: extended breaths kèm "release words" for aching regions — very short versions of pain-coping articles (article M.G2.8 more fully presents).
  • Before sleep hours: doing this article lying down — combining with forms already discussed in article M.G1.6 — the simplest sleep-entry way.

On amounts: one 5–10 minute session daily suffices for relaxing purposes — longer is not necessarily better with this article, because it is not training articles with progress roads like group B; post-session feelings of "more pleasant and lighter than before" signal rightly doing.

5. Summary and next steps

  • This article is releasing and recovering exercises — breath leading releasing signals throughout bodies; unlike group B's breath-mindfulness (training attention) — two articles supplementing each other.
  • Five steps: entering postures → three 3:6 breaths → returning natural breath, choosing distinct sensation places → "release words" each breathing out → soft ending.
  • Four need-to-knows: no bracing for relaxing; light dizziness, breathing shallower and short sessions; nodding off normal; difficult feelings arising, grounding and temporarily reducing amounts — trauma backgrounds applying article M.G2.10 first.
  • On-the-spot abridged versions: after work hours, before tense events, when in pain, before sleep hours; one 5–10 minute daily session suffices.

Suggested next reading: when this article is familiar, the natural next step is widening "release words" into one systematically observing session of the whole body: Body Scan — moving attention through each region from feet to heads, the most studied article in modern health programmes. See you there.

Glossary:

  • Nikāya (Pali: Nikāya): the names of the major collections of discourses in Theravāda Buddhism, compiled by theme or length.
  • Pali: the language in which the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism are preserved.
  • Sanskrit: the language of much of the Mahāyāna Buddhist and Vajrayāna Buddhist scriptural literature.
  • Sino-Vietnamese: the Vietnamese pronunciation of Chinese characters; most meditation terms entered Vietnamese through scriptures translated into Chinese (the Chinese Canon).
  • Breath-mindfulness (Pali: ānāpānasati): attention-training articles through breath — presented in groups B and C1; this article using the same material but releasing purposes.
  • 3:6 rhythms: breathing in counting three, breathing out counting six — out-breaths longer than in-breaths; these rhythm groups join the body's natural relaxing responses.
  • Rest-and-digest modes: everyday ways of saying bodies self-lowering heartbeats and blood pressure when no longer bracing — opposite "fight or flight" states.
  • Grounding: simple ways bringing attention to present senses (contact points, looking around rooms) when feelings or flashbacks are too strong — presented in article A3.
  • Trauma: deep psychological wounds from major events; meditation articles for trauma-background persons need distinct adjustments (article M.G2.10).
  • Mindfulness (Pali: sati): remembering and staying with what is happening right now — the basis capacity trained in group B.
