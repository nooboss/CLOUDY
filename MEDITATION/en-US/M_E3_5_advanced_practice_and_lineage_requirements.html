1. Mahāmudrā

Mahāmudrā (Sanskrit: mahāmudrā; Tibetan: phyag chen) is the system directly pointing at the mind's nature — literally "the great seal": the seal of truth nothing can alter, like seal stamps pressed on texts. The central practice of the Kagyu lineage, with origins traced to Tilopa, Naropa (India) and Marpa, Milarepa (Tibet); presented in parallel with tantra systems and calm abiding – insight (article M.E3.3).

Core contents at map level:

  • Objects are mind itself: unlike methods with outer-placed objects (breath, deities), Mahāmudrā turns knowing toward knowing states themselves — the mind recognizing itself, in originally empty – luminous states, before and after all thoughts (joining the "recognizing the mind's nature" concept introduced in article M.E3.3).
  • Four yogas: one-pointedness — focusing one point (gathering the mind firm), simplicity (seeing experience not as complex as imagined), one taste (all experience of one taste), non-meditation (meditation no longer having meditation forms) — this ladder meets across Kagyu books; the mandatory basis is firm calm abiding and direct pointers (exactly the basis structure discussed in articles M.E3.2 – M.E3.3).

2. Dzogchen

Dzogchen (Tibetan: rdzogs chen — meaning "utterly complete") is the Nyingma lineage's peak practice, self-defined as the highest teaching class among the lineage's nine practice-vehicle classes; origins traced to Garab Dorje — traditionally the lineage's opener — systematized through Nyingma lineage texts.

Core contents at map level:

  • The central idea — Dzogchen's three points: primordially pure nature (Tibetan: ka dag — primordially pure from the start), spontaneously present (Tibetan: lhun grub — self-manifesting all phenomena), and compassion (the automatic capacity to help others). The first and second points explain "why emptiness and appearing are not two things" — joining emptiness already learned in article E2.6.
  • Two states of mind: original states (Tibetan: rig pa — original knowing, before thoughts) and thoughts – phenomena (Tibetan: sem) like waves on water surfaces; practice is distinguishing and holding original states — unlike methods "making" minds into something, Dzogchen aims at "not fixing" minds, only recognizing.
  • Scholarly vs. oral language — M079's sealing point: scholarly books describing Dzogchen through concepts and history; while Nyingma lineages presenting Dzogchen through oral language — only when transmitted by teachers bearing full practice meaning; many keywords have internal understandings translations cannot convey. Readers need knowing this difference to avoid taking book descriptions as "practice guidance".

Neutrally comparing the two names: Mahāmudrā and Dzogchen use different languages (Kagyu vs. Nyingma transmissions), different entries (one side heavy on in-harness analysis – placement, one side direct pointing through instructions), yet the same directly pointing at the mind's nature destination — many great teachers teach both; comparing "which deeper" is meaningless because each side measures by its own ruler (the neutrality principle — articles M.E1.1, M.E1.3).

3. Why some practices cannot be self-study articles — lineage roles

This is M080's central content, answering the whole group E3's question: why should some practices not be presented as self-study articles on the app? Four reasons ordered from principle to practice:

  • The methods' nature demands guides: advanced methods working in "pre-thought" regions — regions where beginners lack capacity to self-recognize their own right and wrong (joining progress-illusion warnings — article M.C2.5); the guidance of those already traversed is part of the method, not accessories.
  • Lineage transmission is a validity condition: as discussed in article M.E3.1 — Tantric-floor methods only "live" when transmitted through teacher lines; the app has no capacity to provide lineage transmission, hence deep parts cannot stand as self-study articles, though textual contents are available in books.
  • Practical risks: self-practising strong-visualization methods, holding deep states, or self-"solving" huàtóu (article M.E2.5) may produce hard-to-self-correct skews (article M.A3 — increased anxiety, insomnia, dissociated sensations); guides help early correction, before skews harden.
  • The app's functional boundary: what the app can supply at educational / overview level — exactly what group E3 articles did: maps, names, places, warnings; what it cannot be is self-training practice guidance. This is exactly how L5 labels are designed to work: when users see L5 labels, the meaning is "understanding for recognizing — for practising, meeting teachers".

This is also practical advice for readers meeting advertisements like "learning Dzogchen online in 7 days", "receiving direct pointing through videos needing no teachers": introductory level is fine, "grasping practice methods" level the traditional systems do not recognize — and buyers do not receive what they think they buy. This spirit matches article M.A6's warning signs and article M.E3.4's teacher – student boundary part.

4. Two roads meeting — summary and closing group E

Summary:

  • Mahāmudrā — Kagyu; "the great seal"; directly pointing at the mind's nature; the "four yogas" frame; the basis is calm abiding + pointing persons; details per teachings.
  • Dzogchen — Nyingma; "utterly complete"; three points (primordial – spontaneous – compassion); distinguishing rig pa and sem; the language seal: scholarly ≠ oral — book descriptions do not replace practice.
  • Two roads, one destination: the same directly pointing at the mind's nature, different languages and entries — comparing "deeper" is meaningless.
  • Lineage transmission and the app's boundary: advanced methods demanding guides because of nature, validity conditions, and practical risks; the app presents educational / overview level — L5 labels mean "understanding for recognizing, practising then meeting teachers".

Closing group E — reviewing three traditions: E1 (Theravāda) erecting maps from canon and commentarial systems, weighting distinct progress maps (article M.E1.4); E2 (Mahāyāna) unfolding the bodhicitta – emptiness frame with many East Asian systems (article M.E2.1); E3 (Vajrayāna) keeping the Mahāyāna basis and adding the Tantric floor, where lineage transmission becomes the method's condition. Three traditions differing in languages, frames, and conditions — sharing the shared root: three trainings, mindfulness, loving-kindness – compassion (groups A–D). Readers completing group E now have enough maps to read any meditation books untangled by names.

Glossary:

  • Mahāmudrā (Sanskrit; Tibetan: phyag chen): "the great seal" — Kagyu's mind-nature-directly-pointing system.
  • Dzogchen (Tibetan: rdzogs chen): "utterly complete" — Nyingma's peak practice.
  • Ka dag / lhun grub (Tibetan): primordially pure from the start / spontaneously present — the first two of Dzogchen's three points.
  • Rig pa / sem (Tibetan): original knowing states / thoughts – phenomena — Dzogchen's central distinguishing pair.
  • Four yogas: Mahāmudrā's four road levels — one-pointedness, simplicity, one taste, non-meditation.
  • Nikāya (Pali: Nikāya): the names of the major collections of discourses in Theravāda Buddhism, compiled by theme or length.
  • Pali: the language in which the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism are preserved.
  • Sanskrit: the language of much of the Mahāyāna Buddhist and Vajrayāna Buddhist scriptural literature.
  • Sino-Vietnamese: the Vietnamese pronunciation of Chinese characters; most meditation terms entered Vietnamese through scriptures translated into Chinese (the Chinese Canon).
