M.E1.2. Core Frameworks in Theravāda

The overview (article M.E1.1) already sealed: within Theravāda exist many lineages differing in technique — yet sharing one frame set. This article presents those three core frames per exactly group M.E1.2's three sub-articles: mindfulness of breathing in Theravāda (M056), the four establishments of mindfulness in the Theravāda approach (M057), and tranquillity – insight in Theravāda (M058). This article's viewpoint is not reteaching from scratch — but viewing learned frames through how the Southern tradition reads and uses them, seeing canonical origins and unfolding roads.

1. Mindfulness of breathing (Ānāpānassati) in Theravāda

Mindfulness of breathing (Pali: ānāpānassati) is the subject with the densest textual explanations in the Southern tradition: the discourse devoted to it (the Ānāpānasati Sutta — MN 118) presents sixteen steps in four groups, corresponding to the four establishments of mindfulness; the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (MN 10) places it first in body contemplation; and the canon joins it to four jhānas (Pali: jhāna) — exactly the discourses' presentation. [Source: Ānāpānasati Sutta, Majjhima Nikāya, MN 118 - Theravāda Buddhism.]

How Theravāda sources unfold mindfulness of breathing goes from steadying attention to the basis for wisdom — and modern lineages divide over which level is the destination:

  • Canonical layer: the breath leads through all four establishments of mindfulness — not stopping at collectedness; the Ānāpānasati Sutta's destination is fulfilled four establishments, then fulfilled awakening factors, then liberation – seeing wisdom.
  • Commentarial layer (Visuddhimagga): the stage sequence — counting (for the mind to follow), connecting, touching, settling — then the meditative sign (Pali: nimitta) appearing at deep collectedness; this road leads to jhāna and is fully kept by the concentration-first – observing-after lineage (Pa-Auk — article M.E1.3).
  • Modern observing lineages: using the breath as a momentary-concentration basis then shifting to observing experience — the breath is the starting point, not the destination; some other lineages (U Tejaniya) use the breath more loosely — as the knowing point itself.

The practitioner's takeaway: the breath is a subject "opening both roads" — firm breath foundation, whichever lineage followed has standing; and the sixteen steps are a ready map when wanting to see the breath joining the whole establishments frame (the app's advanced L4 article).

2. The four establishments of mindfulness (Satipaṭṭhāna) — the Theravāda approach

The four establishments of mindfulness (Pali: satipaṭṭhāna) in the Pali canonical context have two discourse versions: the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (MN 10 — the version used in previous articles) and the Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta (DN 22 — the longer version, adding four-noble-truths detail). The tradition's modern implementations differ around three points:

  • Where to depart: most lineages begin from body — distinctest; some lineages begin directly from feeling or mind for those already grounded.
  • Traversing all four or choosing one: some lineages maintain all four domains as the discourses' sequence; some choose one principal object (rising-falling belly, steps) and let feeling – mind – dhammas appear naturally then note — both ways have their own canonical basis.
  • The repeated template — "arising – passing": each domain's end in the discourses is arising – passing contemplation — modern lineages all keep this as the practice's wisdom quality; differing only in when arising – passing is introduced (early — observing lineages; late — after collectedness has firmed).

The tradition's general principle when reading implementations: the same discourse, lineages stressing different parts — like conductors performing the same score at different tempi; there is no "sole correct performance".

3. Tranquillity and insight (Samatha–Vipassanā) in Theravāda

The tranquillity (Pali: samatha) and insight (Pali: vipassanā) pair — fully presented in groups C1 – C2 — in Theravāda has three combining models (exactly article M.C1.5 summarized), and modern lineages distribute across those three models:

  • Concentration as vehicle (samatha-yānika): sharpening collectedness to jhāna then taking that collectedness as observation basis — fully kept in the Pa-Auk lineage and commentarial-system monasteries;
  • Wisdom as vehicle (vipassanā-yānika): going straight to observing on a momentary-concentration basis — the basis of modern insight lineages (Mahāsi, Goenka); the canon has a basis for this road through discourses on minimal collectedness for wisdom;
  • In harness (yuganaddha): tranquillity – insight alternating — the common way in the Thai Forest Tradition and many modern teachers.

This pair's canonical basis in the tradition: two things to be cultivated are tranquillity and insight (Aṅguttara Nikāya, AN 2.31 — already cited from article M.A1); and the canon in many places presents divine abodes and other subjects leading to either tranquillity or insight by use. What must be avoided is assuming only one orthodox path.

4. Three frames in one sitting — and summary

Merging the three frames above into one Southern-tradition sample sitting — seeing they are not three subjects but three faces of one task:

  • Opening — breath: 5–10 minutes steadying attention on the breath;
  • Body — four establishments: clearly knowing the sitting body, feelings present, the mind's current mode;
  • Observing — tranquillity or insight: per the chosen model: holding the breath deeper (tranquillity), or opening to arising – passing observation on present experience (insight) — using the "recognize – release – return" loop when distracted;
  • Closing — dedication: a few beats clearly knowing the state, brief gratitude, transitioning to daily life.

Summary:

  • Mindfulness of breathing in Theravāda is the densest-text subject — leading through all four establishments (canonical layer) with a complete road to jhāna through the sign (commentarial layer); lineages divide over the destination level.
  • The four establishments have two discourse versions (MN 10, DN 22); implementations differ in departure point, traversing all four or choosing one, and when arising – passing is introduced — the same discourse, different stresses.
  • Tranquillity – insight distributes across three models (concentration as vehicle / wisdom as vehicle / in harness) on modern lineages; not assuming a sole orthodox path.
  • The three frames are not three subjects — three faces of one sitting: steadying → clearly knowing → observing; this is the "shared language" for reading modern lineages in article E1.3.

Glossary:

  • Mindfulness of breathing (Pali: ānāpānassati): the practice of keeping mindfulness on the breath — the Southern tradition's central subject.
  • Counting – connecting – touching (Pali: gaṇanā – anubandhanā – phuṭṭha): breath-working stages per Visuddhimagga chapter VIII — before the sign stage.
  • Meditative sign (Pali: nimitta): the subject's mental image appearing in deep collectedness (article M.C1.3).
  • Parallel discourse versions: the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta (MN 10) and Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta (DN 22) — two versions of the same four-establishments frame, the long version adding the four noble truths.
  • Samatha-yānika / vipassanā-yānika / yuganaddha: Pali names of the three tranquillity – insight combining models (article M.C1.5); yuganaddha meaning "hitching two horses to the same yoke".
  • Nikāya (Pali: Nikāya): the names of the major collections of discourses in Theravāda Buddhism, compiled by theme or length.
  • Pali: the language in which the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism are preserved.
  • Sanskrit: the language of much of the Mahāyāna Buddhist and Vajrayāna Buddhist scriptural literature.
  • Sino-Vietnamese: the Vietnamese pronunciation of Chinese characters; most meditation terms entered Vietnamese through scriptures translated into Chinese (the Chinese Canon).
