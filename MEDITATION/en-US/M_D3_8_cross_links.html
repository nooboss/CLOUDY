M.D3.8. Cross-Links

Group D3 — The Meditation-Subject System (Kammaṭṭhāna) closes with a short navigating article. Why this article exists: the forty-subject system (article M.D3.1) is not an island — nearly every subject group within it already has full separate articles in other path groups; and the app's editorial principle is each content fully presented in one place only. 1. What are "cross-links" — and why needed?

In the app's taxonomy, cross-links are how the relations between one classification group (like the 40-subject system) and content articles specializing in each component are recorded. The 40 system is a classification map — answering "which subjects exist, grouped how, leading to which collectedness levels"; while each subject's complete practice lies in specialized groups: four divine abodes in group M.D2, four formless bases in article M.C1.4, establishments in group D1...

This organization brings readers three benefits: (1) no duplication — one practice not taught twice in two vocabularies; (2) updating in one place — fixing the master article fixes the whole content; (3) a distinct path — beginners following the A → B → C → D group order, meeting the 40 system as an overall reference map, not stuffed with 40 subjects at once.

2. Links with group M.D2 — four divine abodes

Within the 40 system (article M.D3.1), the four divine abodes (Pali: brahmavihāra) — loving-kindness, compassion, joy, equanimity — are four collectedness subjects. The full master articles are group M.D2 (article M.D2 overview, articles M.D2.1–M.D2.4 each quality, article M.D2.5 synthesis and near / far enemies). When reading the 40 system meeting "four divine abodes":

  • Definitions, cultivation, object sequences → group M.D2;
  • Collectedness level in the 40 system: loving-kindness – compassion – joy leading to the first three jhāna (Pali: jhāna) levels; equanimity to the fourth (article M.D3.1 section 3; reasons in article M.D2.4);
  • Equanimity's place in two meanings (divine-abode subjects vs. jhāna / awakening factors) → article M.D2.4 section 3.

3. Links with group C1 — four formless bases

Four formless bases (Pali: āruppa) have their master article as article M.C1.4 (section 4) — presented at educational-map level, with notes needing a teacher and firm foundation. Within the 40 system, the four formless bases develop from kasiṇas (article M.D3.2): eight of ten kasiṇas opening the road to imageless levels — the road's details in Visuddhimagga chapter X and article C1.4. If the app's data needs representing this relation, article M.C1.4 may attach the kammaṭṭhāna classification — this is the taxonomy's recording, not changing the article's content.

4. Links with group M.D1 — establishments in the 40 system

The two most important subjects of daily practice stand at the 40-system – four-establishments intersection:

  • Mindfulness of breathing (Pali: ānāpānassati): master articles — article M.D1.1 (within the establishments frame) and article M.C1.2 (as a collectedness subject); the sixteen-step advanced part is the app's L4 article. Within the 40 system, this is one of ten recollections but specially noted by the commentaries — leading to all four jhāna levels (articles M.D3.1, M.D3.7 section 5).
  • Body recollection / mindfulness immersed in the body (Pali: kāyagatāsati): master article — article M.D1.1; detailed parts: 32 parts (article M.D3.4), four elements (article M.D3.5).

In other words: group M.D1 teaches the establishments as a four-domain frame; group D3 places them into the 40-subject map and collectedness levels — two views of one practice; reading both is fully enough.

5. Remaining links: B foundation, C2 destination, M.A3 safety

Beyond subject groups, the 40 system also joins three "throughout" article layers:

  • Skills foundation — group B: all subjects share already-trained skills: releasing and breath (article M.B1), following and anchoring the mind (article M.B2), handling distraction (article M.B3), noting (article M.B4), walking meditation (article M.B6), journaling (article M.B7). Without a firm foundation, changing subjects solves no problems (article M.D3.1 section 5).
  • Wisdom destination — group C2: collectedness created by subjects (even only access concentration) is the basis for observing arising – passing (article M.C2.3); the collectedness – wisdom combining models already summarized in article C1.5.
  • Safety and teachers — articles M.A3, M.A6: strong countering subjects (foulness — article M.D3.3, food — article M.D3.6, death recollection — article M.D3.7) have their own warnings; deep-collectedness roads (kasiṇas, formless bases) need direct guidance. Group D3's general rule: the map for understanding and choosing — deep-level practice with guides.

Glossary:

  • Cross-links: how relations between one classification system and content articles specializing in each component are recorded — the app's editorial term, not a Buddhist term.
  • Taxonomy: the classification map of the app's entire meditation article store — introduced through groups A, B, C, D structures themselves.
  • Nikāya (Pali: Nikāya): the names of the major collections of discourses in Theravāda Buddhism, compiled by theme or length.
  • Pali: the language in which the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism are preserved.
  • Sanskrit: the language of much of the Mahāyāna Buddhist and Vajrayāna Buddhist scriptural literature.
  • Sino-Vietnamese: the Vietnamese pronunciation of Chinese characters; most meditation terms entered Vietnamese through scriptures translated into Chinese (the Chinese Canon).
