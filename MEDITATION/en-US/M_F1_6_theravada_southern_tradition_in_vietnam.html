M.F1.6. Theravāda / The Southern Tradition in Vietnam

One more meditation lineage already named from the opening article but not yet fully told: the Southern tradition — Theravāda Buddhism. This tradition at world scale; this article narrows the lens to Vietnam with three contents: the formation of two streams (Kinh Theravāda and Khmer Theravāda), currently available practice centres, and the Pali terminology set learners will meet entering this lineage.

1. Theravāda in Vietnam — where from?

Within Vietnamese Buddhism — where Mahāyāna overwhelmingly dominates in temple and head counts — Theravāda keeps a minority yet distinctive place: preserving the Pali canonical basket and a practice style very near the canonical basket. Geographically, Vietnam lies at the eastern edge of the Theravāda cultural region (Cambodia, Thailand, Laos) — hence this lineage entered Vietnam through two roads, forming two historically different streams: one stream present for centuries with southern Khmer communities, and one stream formed in the 20th century when Vietnamese actively received it.

The shared recognition points of both streams: chanting sūtras in Pali (with translations), closely keeping the Vinaya in monastic life, weighting sūtra learning – meditation practice, and using terminology in Pali instead of Sino-Vietnamese like Mahāyāna temples — the final point is exactly section 4's content.

2. Two streams: Kinh Theravāda and Khmer Theravāda

Khmer Theravāda is the Buddhism of Khmer communities (southern Khmer), present for many centuries in the southwest region — the most Khmer-temple-dense provinces are Trà Vinh and Sóc Trăng, with An Giang, Bạc Liêu, and Cà Mau. Khmer temples (Khmer: wat — temples) have recognizable architecture: curved roofs, high tops, nāga dragons (Sanskrit: nāga) embracing steps. Temples here are both worship places and community schools — places teaching Khmer script, customs, and Pali scriptures circulated in Khmer script. Monastic life keeps a strict traditional rhythm: each morning alms rounds (requesting food with bowls — in detail in section 4), festival cycles tightly joined to community seasons, most prominent the Khmer traditional new year (Chôl Chnăm Thmây).

Kinh Theravāda is Vietnamese Theravāda, formed much later: the commonly cited milestone is the 1930s in Saigon, joined to Bửu Quang Temple and the Theravāda Buddhist study association — the first organization gathering Vietnamese wanting to practise in the Southern transmission. The first Vietnamese monastics were sent to study and receive full ordination in Theravāda countries (Sri Lanka, Thailand) then brought back; in opening-phase stories, two frequently met names are Vietnamese Thiền Master (Teav Mao) — the Khmer meditation master supporting transmission roles, and Nun Hộ (Phạm Bảo Thiều) — Bửu Quang Temple's protector wholeheartedly supporting the first transmission ordinations. [Source: modern Vietnamese Buddhist historical materials — 1930–1950 phase details told with more or less differences between materials.]

Placed side by side, the two streams share one root: the same Pali Three-Basket canon, the same Vinaya, the same basis practice style (seated meditation, walking meditation, insight meditation); differing in language – culture (Khmer vs. Vietnamese) and formation history (centuries vs. less than a century). Hence one should not say "Khmer Theravāda differs from Kinh Theravāda in doctrine" — differences lie mainly at the cultural floor.

3. Practice centres today

Those wanting to learn and practise in Theravāda in Vietnam today may meet the following forms:

  • Kinh Theravāda temples in Ho Chi Minh City and many provinces — like Vietnam Quoc Tu Temple, among the most famous centres: Pali-and-Vietnamese chanting activities, doctrinal teaching, and regularly year-round meditation retreats.
  • The Mendicant Saṅgha — a prominent branch within Kinh Theravāda, originating from the 1940s following Venerable Bửu Chơn's orientation: taking the alms-begging spirit (living on bowl-requested food — the Buddha-era Saṅgha's style) as its distinction; Vĩnh Nghiêm Temple in Ho Chi Minh City belongs to this system.
  • Insight-meditation retreats (Pali: vipassanā) — many Theravāda temples and meditation halls organizing insight retreats in modern-system templates: dense morning – evening schedules, alternating sitting and walking meditation, noting by body and breath.
  • Khmer temples in the Southwest — operating in community traditional rhythms; Vietnamese wanting to learn usually arrive through Khmer culture – language courses or festival occasions; meditation practice here lies compactly within monastic and community life.

Practical advice for newcomers: each centre has its own sequence and daily rhythms — asking distinctly the method and style of one's arriving place, and keeping the familiar principle: one teacher – one lineage – one time.

4. Pali terms met entering this lineage

Unlike Mahāyāna temples — where terminology is mainly Sino-Vietnamese (buddha-recitation, seated meditation, dedication...) — in Theravāda temples, learners will hear words in Pali. This is the minimal vocabulary set, with short explanations (basis concepts fully presented in previous articles):

  • Bhikkhus (Pali: bhikkhu): fully ordained renunciants; female bhikkhunīs (Pali: bhikkhunī).
  • Novices (Pali: sāmaṇera): young renunciants or not yet fully ordained.
  • Saṅgha (Pali: saṅgha): the renunciant-practitioner community — the concept already met from article A6.
  • Sādhu (Pali: sādhu): exclamations read three times after chanting sūtras or after giving occasions.
  • Generosity (Pali: dāna): giving deeds — paired with merit (Pali: puñña) — wholesome value accumulated from good deeds.
  • Alms rounds (Pali: piṇḍapāta): requesting food with bowls each morning — the tradition closely kept in many Theravāda temples; why one branch is called "Mendicant".
  • Three Baskets (Pali: tipiṭaka): the canon of Vinaya – Sutta – Abhidhamma (article M.E1.1).
  • Discourses (Pali: sutta): each article in the Sutta Piṭaka; people often hear "listening to Dhamma – chanting sūtras – learning suttas" interchangeably.
  • Tranquillity – insight (Pali: samatha / vipassanā): two basis practice directions of all retreats (article M.A1).
  • Mindfulness (Pali: sati): the basis capacity of all methods (group B articles); with mindfulness of breathing (Pali: ānāpānasati) — the most widespread subject.
  • Loving-kindness (Pali: mettā): the goodwill-radiating method — presented in article D2.1.
  • Nirvana (Pali: nibbāna): the path's destination (article M.A1).

With this word set, newcomers to Theravāda temples will understand most daily announcements: chanting hours, meditation hours, giving occasions, retreats — especially untangled when Pali – Vietnamese transliterations differ between places (the same bhikkhu written various ways by translations).

5. Summary and next steps

  • Theravāda in Vietnam has two streams of one root: Khmer Theravāda (centuries, joined to southern Khmer communities) and Kinh Theravāda (20th century, the 1930s milestone — Bửu Quang Temple, the Theravāda study association).
  • The two streams differ mainly at the culture – language floor, sharing the Pali Three-Basket canon and the near-canonical practice style.
  • Today's centres: Kinh Theravāda temples, the Mendicant Saṅgha, insight-meditation retreats, and Khmer temples in traditional rhythms.
  • Section 4's Pali terminology set is the "doorway key" — learners need only a dozen words to avoid confusion in first activities.

Glossary:

  • Nikāya (Pali: Nikāya): the names of the major collections of discourses in Theravāda Buddhism, compiled by theme or length.
  • Pali: the language in which the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism are preserved.
  • Sanskrit: the language of much of the Mahāyāna Buddhist and Vajrayāna Buddhist scriptural literature.
  • Sino-Vietnamese: the Vietnamese pronunciation of Chinese characters; most meditation terms entered Vietnamese through scriptures translated into Chinese (the Chinese Canon).
  • Southern Buddhism: Vietnamese addressing of Theravāda Buddhism; including Kinh Theravāda (Vietnamese) and Khmer Theravāda (southern Khmer).
  • Wat (Khmer): temples — the worship – practice places of Khmer Theravāda Buddhism.
  • Nāga (Sanskrit: nāga): serpent deities in Khmer culture, appearing much on Khmer temple architecture.
  • Chôl Chnăm Thmây: the Khmer traditional new year, usually mid-April, joined to temple activities.
  • Alms rounds (Pali: piṇḍapāta): living on bowl-requested food — the Buddha-era Saṅgha's original style, strictly kept in many Theravāda lineages.
  • Mendicant Saṅgha: the Kinh Theravāda system originating from the 1940s following Venerable Bửu Chơn's orientation, taking alms-begging as its distinction.
  • Vietnamese Thiền Master (Teav Mao): the Khmer Theravāda meditation master (1886–1964), joined to Kinh Theravāda's opening phase; "Vietnam" is this one's distinct title, not nationality.
  • Nun Hộ (Phạm Bảo Thiều): the female lay devotee (1897–1970), Bửu Quang Temple's protector vigorously supporting Kinh Theravāda's first transmission-ordination phase.
  • Bửu Quang Temple: the Saigon temple joined to the Theravāda study association — Kinh Theravāda's 1930s formation milestone.
  • Insight (Pali: vipassanā): insight meditation — directly observing body and mind; in detail in group C2 articles.
  • Mindfulness of breathing (Pali: ānāpānasati): the breath-following subject — presented in groups B and C1 articles.
