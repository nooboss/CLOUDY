M.G2.7. Meditation for Sleep

After stress, the next health goal is sleep — because the two tightly join each other: prolonged tension worsens sleep, and poor sleep worsens tension. This article presents relaxing – pre-sleep deactivating practices exactly its focus; stating one important thing from the start: meditation supports most flickering cases — but does not replace seeking insomnia causes when difficult sleep prolongs many weeks.

1. Why do minds refuse to sleep?

Sleeping is not "making happen" business — it is abandoning business: releasing all handholds, letting consciousness drift. Modern persons' most widespread insomnia is therefore not lacking fatigue, but minds refusing to release: just placing heads down, machines begin running — summarizing days, calculating tomorrow, replaying unfinished conversations. Bodies lying still yet systems still in running modes — called arousal — nervous systems' wakefulness levels; insomnia usually means excessively high arousal at lying hours.

And one spiral to state plainly: the more effort to sleep, the less sleep possible. Sleep does not obey orders; forcing "sleeping" adds one more task for minds to worry — exactly what needs releasing braced more. Below practices therefore aim not at "making sleep", but at reducing arousal and releasing handholds — sleep arriving after, naturally. Changing these expectations is this whole article's most important step: the goal before sleep hours is resting, not sleeping — sleeping is what happens when one has comfortably given up.

2. Preparing before sleep hours

The final 30–60 minutes of days decide most: systems need lowering numbers before lying:

  • Cutting late activating substances: afternoon coffee (needing many hours to excrete), pre-sleep alcohol (initial drowsiness but mid-night sleep breaking — easily waking around two–three AM), bright screens and activating contents (social media, news, work) — these are not meditation articles but the basis: meditation articles cannot beat three late coffees.
  • One "task interval" before sleep: if minds often calculate work business — spending five minutes writing on paper tomorrow's tasks, before sleep. Externally written tasks are permitted by minds to skip; this is a practical technique many use helping considerably reduce "calculating tomorrow" machines while lying.
  • Gradually lowering rhythms: dimmer yellow lights, slower activities (paper-book reading, soft music listening, tooth-brushing — conscious face-washing articles), and one 5–10 minute practice session on chairs or bed edges: breath-based relaxing meditation (article M.G2.2) or short body-scan versions (article M.G2.3) — two already-learned articles, rightly used for arousal-lowering roles.

3. While lying — practices

This is the principal part. Articles used in lying postures, closed eyes, no hour-counting needed, no need to "finish" — sinking into sleep mid-articles is the best result:

  • Extended out-breaths + "release words": a few 3:6 breaths, then letting breath be natural; each out-breath, one phrase: "releasing shoulders... releasing jaws... releasing backs... releasing legs..." — cycling; no need to traverse whole bodies (article M.G2.2).
  • Sleep-standard body-scan versions: beginning from feet — far slower than article M.G2.3: one region a few breaths; the goal not traversing all — going where sleeping there is normal and good.
  • Countdown breaths: counting down from ten to one, each number one breath; missing numbers — restarting from ten, no blame; this version gives minds light tasks so "calculating business" handholds release easier (the same mechanism as counting articles — group B, but used for lowering instead of training).
  • Directing attention to surrounding sounds: fan sounds, distant traffic, rain sounds — hearing unclassifying unscoring (sound subjects — article M.C1.2); quiet even sounds help minds have resting places without processing needed.
  • Abandoning hours: if all above articles are done yet still alert — returning to resting without sleeping: lying still, soft, warm, knowing one is resting. Many nights, sleep arrives exactly when one stops awaiting it.

4. When waking midnights — and when to seek causes

Midnight waking (most widespread around two–three AM): the first rule — not viewing hours (viewing hours switches on calculating machines: "X hours remaining" — one worrying calculation). Reusing one of section 3's articles at very light levels; if after about twenty minutes still distinctly alert — many sleep guidance advising standing up to chairs doing one soft task (reading a few pages under yellow lights), until genuinely drowsy really returning to beds — so beds do not become lying-worrying places. Standing-up business sounds counterintuitive, but it exactly breaks the "lying alert – worrying – lying alert" rounds chronic insomnia nourishes.

When to seek causes — professional boundaries: below situations are when exercises are insufficient and doctors should be met (article M.G2.1 on boundaries):

  • Prolonged insomnia: hard sleeping, frequent waking, early rising — three nights weekly or more, prolonged over three months (medicine calls chronic insomnia) — having distinct causes and treatment ways (for example cognitive – behavioural therapy specialized for sleep, abbreviated CBT-I, regarded as standard treatment);
  • "Enough" sleeping yet all-day fatigue, loud snoring, beside persons seeing occasional breathing cessations — signs of sleep respiratory disorders (for example sleep apnea) — needing medical examinations;
  • Restless legs, pain pressing on sleep, or other prolonged symptoms — similarly;
  • Insomnia going with depression and intense anxiety — the two tightly join each other; handling principal conditions is how to handle sleep.

One sentence to carry: meditation articles are sleep's assistants — not sleep repairers. With most flickering nights from running minds, they genuinely help; with medically caused conditions, they accompany treatment, and seeking repairers is must-do business.

5. Summary and next steps

  • Changing expectations is the most important step: the goal before sleep hours is resting, not sleeping; arousal-lowering articles (arousal) — sleep arriving after, naturally; "the more forcing sleep the more losing sleep" is the principal spiral.
  • Preparing: cutting late activating substances (coffee, alcohol, screens), five minutes writing tomorrow's business on paper, gradually lowering rhythms + one "breath-based relaxing" (article M.G2.2) or "body scan" (article M.G2.3) short sessions.
  • While lying: extended out-breaths + release words; slow scanning; countdowns; hearing surrounding sounds; and "resting without sleeping" when all articles are done — stopping awaiting sleep is usually when sleep arrives.
  • Midnight waking: not viewing hours; light articles; distinctly alert over twenty minutes → chairs reading under yellow lights, genuinely drowsy returning; chronic insomnia, snoring with breathing cessations, all-day fatigue though enough sleep — medical signals needing examinations (article M.G2.1).

Glossary:

  • Nikāya (Pali: Nikāya): the names of the major collections of discourses in Theravāda Buddhism, compiled by theme or length.
  • Pali: the language in which the scriptures of Theravāda Buddhism are preserved.
  • Sanskrit: the language of much of the Mahāyāna Buddhist and Vajrayāna Buddhist scriptural literature.
  • Sino-Vietnamese: the Vietnamese pronunciation of Chinese characters; most meditation terms entered Vietnamese through scriptures translated into Chinese (the Chinese Canon).
  • Arousal: nervous systems' wakefulness levels; insomnia usually joins excessively high arousal at lying hours.
  • Chronic insomnia: prolonged hard sleeping (≥3 nights / week, over 3 months) — medical conditions with standard treatment (CBT-I); unlike ordinary flickering insomnia.
  • CBT-I (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia): cognitive – behavioural therapy specialized for insomnia — the first-line standard treatment in current medicine.
  • Sleep apnea: sleep respiratory disorders — loud snoring, occasional breathing cessations, all-day fatigue though enough sleep; needing medical examinations.
  • 3:6 rhythms: breathing in counting three, breathing out counting six — presented in article G2.2.
  • Sound subjects: using sounds present as objects — presented in article C1.2.
  • Self-care: the whole group G2's frame — self-care contents do not replace examinations (article M.G2.1).
